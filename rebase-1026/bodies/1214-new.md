Lesson 10 ("Major and minor") found two places where the app's own copy says something the lesson shows is wrong (Lesson 10 FACTS 49 and 55). This corrects both. It holds with the other lesson PRs: no merge before the owner's go after the v0.9.22 tag.

## The Learn chapter "Common Musical Scales"

It wrote moods as what the scales are, and named the wrong relative major. A minor key's tonic sits three half steps below its relative major's, so C minor's relative major is E flat major (OMT 2e 1.13).

| Line | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | Different scales create different moods and are used in various musical contexts. | Different scales have their own sound and are used in various musical contexts. |
| C major | ... scale. Bright, cheerful, and uplifting. | ... scale. |
| C major | Best for: Beginners and optimistic melodies | Best for: Beginners |
| Natural minor | Emotional, reflective, and sometimes sad. | Many Western listeners hear minor as sadder than major, but that is a tendency, not a rule: tempo, the rest of the music and what you grew up hearing all shape how happy or sad a piece sounds. |
| C minor | Uses the same notes as relative major (A Major) ... | Uses the same notes as its relative major (Eb Major) ... |
| C minor | Best for: Melancholic or emotional expressions | Compared with C Major, three notes are a semitone lower: Eb, Ab and Bb |

The new minor line takes Lesson 10's Step 4 words. Each line changes in place, so no line of the chapter moves.

## Drone Intonation

Its idle line promised "just intonation", its help promised "pure intonation", and the help told the singer to tune until the two sounds "lock together and stop beating". But every target is the equal-tempered note (`midiToFreq`, twelve equal steps), the same as in the rest of the app.

- Idle line: "Sing intervals against a sustained drone. Each target is an equal-tempered note, in the same tuning as the rest of the app."
- Help summary: "Match and hold notes against a steady drone. Every target is equal-tempered."
- Help body, first two paragraphs: each round names an interval for you to sing and hold, and the targets use the app's twelve equal steps, so the singer adjusts until their note sits on the target. The third paragraph and the menu card stay as they are.

The copy makes no claim about just intervals, because no source on them was read.
