# Rules: what holds across the series' films

From `lesson-01/` (Lesson 1, rc-07, 314 s) and `lesson-02/` (Lesson 2, rc-04, 431 s), the BRIEF's
two style references. Each rule names the times that show it; the notes in each folder have the
numbers. Study only: nothing here is footage to copy.

1. **One continuous room.** Neither film has a cut by the detector. A change of subject is a camera
   move on the same world, an object that carries across, or a swap under something that stays (the
   Mercs in their corners, L2 357.65) or on a line that matches (L1 32.43, 34.08).
2. **Through an object.** The object comes at the lens accelerating (L1 1.8 s, L2 0.75 s) and
   defocuses as it nears (edge detail falls to 0); a radial field of its own colour fills the frame
   and keeps changing a little (L1 warm, peak brightness 190, breathing; L2 chrome, peak 122) for
   0.2 to 0.35 s; then it leaves fast (0.3 to 0.35 s) onto a mark already waiting in the next scene:
   a note head on the staff (L2 25.55), the dot of a question mark (L1 24.03). The title lands in
   the 0.2 s after. No type stays in the field (L2's question fades first).
3. **Move, then act.** Pushes and pulls run 0.65 to 1.3 s, ease-in-out with a long settle (L2
   142.3, 159.3, 195.0, 198.9); the change the move went for happens once it has settled there (L2
   143.18, the black key lights).
4. **Labels and moves.** A label that would read wrong at the new size leaves before the move and
   its replacement lands after (L2 198.85 to 199.45). A label that reads at both sizes rides the
   world and scales with it (L2 159.3). Running sums sit in screen space and stay put through a move
   (L2 "7 + 5 = 12"). After a swap, the label comes after the picture (L1 32.48, 34.13).
5. **Objects become the next scene's furniture.** The piano turns into the canvas's vertical keyboard
   (L1 232.1 to 233.9, 1.8 s); the bead's light becomes a note on the staff (L2) or the "?" (L1).
   Nothing is replaced when it could transform.
6. **The Mercs.** They hold their corners and keep idling while the board between them changes (L2
   357.65); they leave quickly to give a diagram the frame, by a hop (L1 76.0, about 0.25 s) or a
   sink (L1 147.25, about 0.4 s); for the turn they go to their corners as the title lands and come
   back after it (L1 258.2, 261.5).
7. **Your turn.** The guide keeps running after the title leaves (L2 357.2); a short instruction
   sits beside the guide's head (L1 "hum an A" with a mic icon; L2 a small mono note beside the
   guide); the title and instruction leave before the Mercs come back.
8. **Never still.** A diagram that must sit through a line drifts or settles slightly (L2 251.9, about
   0.85 s) while a label leaves; the Mercs' idles and blinks carry spans where little else moves.
   Near-still spans in both films stay under about 0.5 s.
9. **Colour.** Merc's blue and Tavi's orange mark what each sings and the octave's two ends (L1's A3
   and A4 keys, L2's keyboard ends); everything else is chrome; one gold relation per film (L1 the
   2 : 1 marks, L2 "E♯ = F" and the twelve wedges).
10. **Type.** Serif italic for the big idea and the turn ("What is a note?", "Twelve notes",
    "Predict.", "Your turn."); mono caps for kickers and small notes; Outfit for numbers and labels.
