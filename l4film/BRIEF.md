# Brief

## Intent

- What the video is for: Lesson 4 of "Find Your Pitch", MercuryPitch's music theory and singing
  series, and its third theory lesson (topics T09, the major scale and the tonic as home, and T10,
  scale degrees and movable do). It answers the question Lesson 3's next-lesson card ends on ("Merc
  climbs seven stairs and stops, one short of home") and embeds in the Lesson 4 web page, which
  today has no Watch section (WEB-SPEC, opening).
- Who is watching: beginners who have seen Lesson 2 or know what a semitone and a tone are. Many
  think a scale is a fixed set of keys that starts on C, often because the white keys from C are
  the one scale a keyboard shows plainly. They want to understand why the scale they already know
  sounds the way it does, not memorise a formula. Most watch with the sound on (this lesson is
  about hearing an unfinished scale), but the picture must carry every idea with the sound off and
  captions on.
- What they should do at the end: answer the recap in their head, then sing a major scale back
  one note at a time in MercuryPitch's Scale Runner. Spoken at the end of the recap: "In
  MercuryPitch's Scale Runner, sing a major scale back, one note at a time." On screen: the
  staircase turning into Scale Runner's ladder of notes still to come (labelled as an
  illustration), then "Practise it free: mercurypitch.com/pitch-training", "then tap Scale
  Runner". Next-episode tease: Lesson 5's hook, ending on Tavi's "If that doesn't mean pushing,
  what keeps it steady?"

## Teaching

### Objectives (CURRICULUM § 2.1, Lesson 4)

1. Build a major scale from its pattern of steps: tone, tone, semitone, tone, tone, tone,
   semitone, two tone-tone-semitone groups joined by a tone.
2. Start the pattern on any note, and know that a scale is a pattern, not a set of keys (D major
   needs F♯ and C♯).
3. Name the stairs: the tonic as home, the scale degrees 1 to 7, and do re mi fa so la ti in
   movable do.
4. Hear ti, a semitone under do, lean towards home.

### Hook

The cold open is the scene Lesson 3's card promises: Merc climbs seven stairs and stops, one short
of home. Tavi guesses it hangs "because we started on C"; Merc has her start on D; it still hangs,
and lands home only with the last step. Tavi turns it into the series' hook question (rule 1),
done by about 0:30: "So why does do-re-mi sound finished when it lands on do, and restless when it
stops on ti?" The web page's title is the same question (LESSON.md, Header). The recall question
from Lesson 2 comes straight after the promise, not before the hook, as every page now orders
them (MASTER-PLAN § 12 item 13). YouTube title variants for Test & Compare (MASTER-PLAN § 4.1):
"Why does do-re-mi sound finished on do, but not on ti?" (54 characters); "The major scale:
why do-re-mi wants to land on do" (48); search-style, in the audience's own words, "How is a
major scale built?" (28).

### Misconceptions (Tavi voices them sincerely; a demonstration breaks them)

1. "A scale is a fixed set of notes, and it starts on C" (the CURRICULUM card; o1, "Is that
   because we started on C?"). Broken by sound in the cold open: from D the scale still hangs on
   ti and still lands home, so home moved with the start.
2. "Same white keys, just starting on D: surely that's the same scale?" (o7). Broken by a predict
   ("Is it still do-re-mi?"), then the white keys from D and the pattern from D, one after the
   other, then the picture: the dashed white-keys staircase climbs tone, semitone, tone, tone,
   tone, semitone, tone, and its third and seventh stairs sit one semitone row below the real
   ones (F under F♯, C under C♯). Tavi says the answer in her own words (o8).
3. "So do is always C?" (o9). Half right, and Merc says so: in fixed do it is; this series uses
   movable do, and the slide to D shows the names riding on their stairs while the letters under
   them change.

### Signature object and its transformation

The staircase: one stair per note, each stair's height its semitones above do on a ruled grid of
semitone rows (Lesson 2's equal tiles stood upright), each riser one or two rows. In the cold open
it is compact and bare, names only, the top stair home under an unlit house glyph. Lesson 2's
keyboard slides in under it and every stair stands over its key; the risers are measured, and the
two one-row risers turn gold. Brackets split it into two groups of the same shape. It slides,
whole, along the keyboard to D, G and E, never changing shape, while the keys under it turn black
where the pattern needs them; Tavi's dashed staircase on the white keys from D sags beside it. Its
labels switch from letters to numbers to do-re-mi, and slide with it. Its stairs regroup into
tiers for the experiment on endings, and settle back. Finally it tips into the scale guide's
lanes, where Merc and Tavi sing it, and then into Scale Runner's ladder.

### Three signature moments

1. **One short of home.** Merc's blue bead climbs seven stairs with his voice and stops; the
   eighth stair waits above it, the house unlit. The whole staircase rises one tone, Tavi climbs
   it, it hangs again, and her last step lands home: the house lights on the new top stair.
2. **The pattern slides, the keys change.** The staircase, its two gold short steps in place,
   glides along the keyboard from C to D, then G, then E, without a stair moving out of line,
   while the black keys under it light up two, one, four at a time. Beside it, Tavi's dashed
   staircase on the white keys from D shows two stairs sitting a row too low.
3. **The names ride the stairs.** As the staircase slides to D, the letters under it slide out
   and new ones slide in (D, E, F♯ ...), while do, re, mi stay on their own stairs and the house
   moves with do.

### Checkpoint (sung)

Do-re-mi-fa-so and back, then up to ti, a wait, and do (FACTS 63). Merc sings the first half (up
to so and back, from D3); Tavi says what comes next and sings the second half an octave up, the
climb to ti, the held ti and home (D4 to D5), which models the pull; then the viewer sings the
whole of it (rule 16: Merc does it, Tavi does part, the viewer does it alone). The care lines come
before the invitation: "Sore, hoarse or unwell today? Just listen along." (FACTS 50), "at about
speaking volume" (51), and "Stop immediately if anything feels uncomfortable." (53; the short
form approved for Lessons 1 and 2 is not used). The viewer starts on Merc's note or an octave up
(52), and is asked to notice, when they stop on ti, where it wants to go. One soft count-in note,
then an 18.2-second guide: a plain chrome line sweeping the path, no microphone, nothing listening
and nothing scored, and nothing of the app's drawing on screen (as Lesson 2's DECISIONS 68). The
bed's open fifth on D sounds under the turn and gives the starting note.

### Practice link

Scale Runner (FACTS 36 to 41), reached at mercurypitch.com/pitch-training, then tapped in the
exercises (35; free, 34). It plays a major scale one note at a time, do up to do, and the viewer
sings each note back; it draws the notes still to come as a ladder (39), which is why the film
ends the staircase as a ladder, labelled "Illustration". Today no link opens it at a beginner

page's second drill and is not in the film.
