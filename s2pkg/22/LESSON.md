## Four words you'll meet

- **Vibrato**: a regular wave in a held note's pitch, up and down, several times a second. [Play:
  a note with a vibrato]
- **Rate**: how many swings a vibrato makes a second. [Play: 5 a second, then 7]
- **Extent**: how far each swing reaches, up and down, in cents. It's written with ±: ±30 means 30
  up and 30 down. [Play: ±20, then ±60]
- **Straight note**: a held note with no wave. [Play: a straight note]

## Step 1 of 4: What exactly is waving?

[Play straight] [Play with a wave] A straight note draws a level line. Give the same note a vibrato
and the line itself waves, up and down by the same distance each time. That's the pitch moving, and
pitch is height. A swell, Lesson 14's, changes the loudness instead: the strip under the line
grows, and the line stays level. [Play a swell]

**Try:** play the straight note, then the one with a wave, then the swell.
**Notice:** the wave moves the line; the swell moves only the strip.

_Toy: Lesson 5's band chart with its level strip, on A3, Merc's lines in his amber: Play straight
and Play with a wave (six swings a second, ±40 cents), 3 seconds each, and Play a swell, Lesson
14's, 4 seconds, straight and on pitch. The toy waits for the cold open's choice, as every step's
does: until then its controls are disabled, under the line "Choose an answer at the top to unlock
this figure."_

## Step 2 of 4: How fast, and how far?

A vibrato has two measures. Its rate is how many swings it makes a second. Its extent is how far
each swing reaches from the middle, up and down, in cents. Trained singers have been measured at
about 5 to 8 swings a second, usually under ±100 cents, a semitone either way; averages sit near
six. Hertz counts swings a second, as in Lesson 1. Merc's "ooh", heard since Lesson 1, carries a
small vibrato: 5.5 a second, ±14 cents, inside Lesson 5's band. [Play Merc's "ooh"]

**Try:** set Rate to 6 and Extent to ±30, then double Extent.
**Notice:** the count stays at 6 as the swing widens: rate and extent are separate.

_Toy: the band chart zoomed to one second, with a counter that ticks once a swing and a ruler from
the middle to the top of a swing; Rate, 5 to 8 swings a second, and Extent, ±10 to ±60 cents, with
Play, 4 seconds, its sound following the sliders. The toy waits for the cold open's choice._

Tavi: "So a wave that crosses the band isn't out of tune?"

> **Common mix-up:** "A wobbling note is out of tune."
> **Why it seems right:** the line leaves the band swing after swing.
> **The evidence:** people match an even wave to a straight note near its middle, and in one
> experiment they judged the pitch of vibrato notes as well as straight ones.
> **The better idea:** Lesson 5 said a small vibrato can sit inside the band. One wider than the
> band's 35 cents swings past its edges, so on this page the band holds the middle of the line. That
> middle is the note: if it drifts, the note drifts.

**Try:** slide Rate from 7 down to 2, then switch on Drift.
**Notice:** as the wave slows, the note starts to move. With Drift, the middle leaves the band.

_Toy: the band chart with Merc's wave at ±50 cents and its middle drawn as a dashed line; Play: six
a second and Play: two a second; Rate, 2 to 7 swings a second, with Play at this rate, and Drift,
which lifts the middle 50 cents over 3 seconds. The toy waits for the cold open's choice; Play: two
a second, Rate and Drift wait for this step's choice too._

> **Go deeper**
>
> - **Which middle?** One study found the note heard close to the geometric mean of the wave's top
>   and bottom frequencies: for Merc's wave, 220 Hz, A3 exactly. The plain average is 0.7 cents
>   higher, almost the same.

## Step 4 of 4: Does a singer need one?

No. Choir experts' views range from a free vibrato, like a soloist's, to a minimal one or none, and
singers can change theirs. This lesson asks for none and scores none: a straight note is a finished
answer.

[Play the sung wave] Voice Mirror's hold reports a wave of 3.5 to 8.5 swings a second as, say,
"Vibrato 5.6 Hz, ±38 cents. That's a feature, not scored against you." The app's wavy ribbon for a
vibrato note is a mark: its swings follow how wide the note is drawn, not a singer's rate.

**Try:** play the sung wave, then stretch the note.
**Notice:** the ribbon gains swings as it widens; the sung line keeps its 11 swings.

_Toy: the band chart's last state, Merc's sung wave of 5.5 swings a second at ±30 cents over 2
seconds, in his amber and labelled "sung", beside the ribbon the app draws for a vibrato note, in
chrome and labelled "the app's mark"; Stretch widens the note. The toy waits for the cold open's
choice._

## Wrap

- A vibrato is a regular wave in a held note's pitch, measured by its rate, in swings a second, and
  its extent, in cents.
- Quick enough, and we hear one note, at the middle of the swing. Slow it right down, and the ear
  follows it.
- A straight note and a vibrato are both used in singing, and both are finished notes.

### Before Sing it

- The steps play everything for listening only, and nothing in them asks the viewer to sing, so the
  comfort check comes once, before Sing it. Among the sounds heard only: a wave of ±50 cents at six
  swings a second (the cold open), the same wave at two a second (Step 3 and quick check 3), the
  Drift (Step 3) and Step 2's sliders, up to ±60 cents (FACTS 64 to 71).
- **To confirm: Merc's waves drawn on the band chart**, in his amber, as examples to listen to. The
  kit's chart otherwise leaves his vibrato out of his line, so that the wobble is never read as
  something to copy; this page draws it because the wave is its subject, and it asks for none
  (FACTS 38).
- **Step 4, every sentence**: "No. Choir experts' views range from a free vibrato, like a soloist's,
  to a minimal one or none, and singers can change theirs. This lesson asks for none and scores
  none: a straight note is a finished answer." and its reading of Voice Mirror's report and the
  app's ribbon (FACTS 25, 26, 28, 30). Its "a wave of 3.5 to 8.5 swings a second" leaves out Voice
  Mirror's 10-cent floor, for brevity.
- **The lines that carry the same claims**: quick check 4, "A held note without a vibrato isn't
  finished", with its right answer, "Both are used in singing, and a straight note is finished", and
  its three feedback lines; the Wrap's third line, "A straight note and a vibrato are both used in
  singing, and both are finished notes."; and the one-sentence challenge, "why a note that swings 50
  cents either way can still be in tune" (FACTS 18, 25, 26).
- **Lesson 5's rule, restated**: the cold open's reveal, "a wave this wide doesn't fit inside it, but
  its middle does, and that's the note you hear", and Step 3's mix-up card, "Lesson 5 said a small
  vibrato can sit inside the band. One wider than the band's 35 cents swings past its edges, so on
  this page the band holds the middle of the line." (FACTS 22).
- Step 2's numbers, "about 5 to 8 swings a second, usually under ±100 cents, a semitone either
  way", "averages sit near six" and Merc's "5.5 a second, ±14 cents": measurements of trained
  singers and of the page's own voice, which nothing asks anyone to copy (FACTS 12 to 15).
- **To confirm: the page gives no instruction for making a vibrato**, and no line says how one is

### The holds

- **The two holds and their order**: Hold 1, straight, then Hold 2, floating, ten seconds each, on
  A3 or A4, moved up to four semitones either way with Lower and Higher, as in Lesson 5. Hold 2 comes
  only after "Easy" or "Workable" on Hold 1 and a rest the singer ends, from a button that reads
  "Hold 2: floating"; up to three takes a hold (FACTS 51, 54, 55).
- **To confirm: Hold 1's cue**, "ten seconds on your note, as level as feels easy". It asks for a
  straight note without forcing one; for a voice with a vibrato of its own, holding it back may take
  effort. The alternative is "ten seconds on your note, as in Lesson 5", with no word about a wave
  (FACTS 54).
- **To confirm: Hold 2's cue**, the card's words: "Let the note float. If a wave comes, let it; if
  it doesn't, the note is just as finished." (FACTS 54).
- **To confirm: Merc's model notes**: his usual "ooh" before both holds, as in Lesson 5, whose small
  wave, 5.5 a second at ±14 cents, sits inside the band. The alternative, a straight model before
  Hold 1 to match its cue, would have to wait for the cold open's choice: a straight note on the
  learner's A beside the cold open's wave would answer its predict (FACTS 1a, 56).
- **To confirm: the range.** The note, A3 or A4, moved up to four semitones either way, keeps every
  preset of its voice at least three semitones inside its floor and top; the least room is 3, at the
  bass preset's top (C♯4 against E4), and 4 at the alto's top (C♯5 against F5). No octave lower is
  offered: the line above Sing it's heading keeps Season 1's "any octave counts", which the reader
  bears out (FACTS 55):

- **To confirm: what the holds score**: the time the middle of the line stays inside the band, 35
  cents either side of Merc's note, in any octave, with any vibrato the reader finds taken out
  first: one of 3.5 to 8.5 swings a second, reaching 10 cents, found in any 3-second stretch of the
  hold, so a vibrato that comes late, or whose speed wanders, counts in full too. Nothing scores a
  vibrato's presence, rate or extent (FACTS 43, 44, 46).
- **To confirm: the reader's floor of 3.5 swings a second**, Voice Mirror's. It takes out a wave of
  3.5 to 4 a second too, near where Sundberg's review hears the pitch "clearly undulating", so such a
  hold is read on its middle. A floor of 4 would make the page's rule differ from Voice Mirror's
  (FACTS 21, 28, 44).
- **To confirm: the vibrato line**, "The note had a wave: about 5.6 swings a second, ±38 cents.",
  shown only when the hold had one, a description of the sound, and no line at all when none was
  found. A wave wider than the band, say ±60 cents, is described the same way, with nothing added, as
  the lead answered. **Its extent reads low**: it measures the line after the app's smoothing, so a
  real vibrato is reported about 10 to 30 % below its sung extent, here and in Voice Mirror (FACTS
  46).
- **To confirm: no live band state during a take.** The line draws as the singer holds, but nothing
  says "in the band" until the take ends, since a wave wider than the band would flicker it on every
  swing; Lesson 5's take shows it live (FACTS 57).
- The reading lines, Lesson 5's from the kit's hold reader, worked out from the middle of the line,
  under Lesson 5's "After each hold, one line, such as:": "In the band for 9.8 of the 10 seconds. It
  settled after about 0.3 seconds.", the octave line, the lines for a hold that stopped early or
  never settled, and the summary after Hold 2, "This time the middle of your line was in the band for
  9.8 of 10 seconds, then 9.6 of 10." (FACTS 45, 58).
- "What this does", new words: "it draws your line, takes a vibrato's wave out to find its middle,
  and measures how long that middle stays in Merc's band, in any octave. A vibrato doesn't count

### Proposed, not applied: the four answers to "How did that feel?"

As in Lessons 3, 5, 14, 16 and 23, the plain-language review proposes [ Easy ] [ OK ] [ Hard work ]
[ Uncomfortable ] in place of [ Easy ] [ Workable ] [ Effortful ] [ Uncomfortable ]. Here the answer
decides whether Hold 2 is offered, so a word a learner guesses at can misreport how the singing
felt. The labels are REQ-GVC-022's, so the page keeps them unless the spec changes, for every lesson
at once (FACTS 58).
