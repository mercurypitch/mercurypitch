## Four words you'll meet

- **Vibrato**: a regular wave in a held note's pitch, up and down, several times a second. [Play:
  a note with a vibrato]
- **Rate**: how many swings a vibrato makes a second. [Play: 5 a second, then 7]
- **Extent**: how far each swing reaches, up and down, in cents. It's written with ±: ±30 means 30
  up and 30 down. [Play: ±20, then ±60]
- **Straight note**: a held note with no wave. [Play: a straight note]

(Each word's figure is a small band chart that draws its play's line, with no line at the band's

## Step 1 of 4: What exactly is waving?

[Play straight] [Play with a wave] A straight note draws a level line. Give the same note a vibrato
and the line itself waves, up and down by the same distance each time. That's the pitch moving, and
pitch is height. A swell, Lesson 14's, changes the loudness instead: the strip under the line
grows, and the line stays level. [Play a swell]

**Try:** play the straight note, then the one with a wave, then the swell.
**Notice:** the wave moves the line; the swell moves only the strip.

_Toy: Lesson 5's band chart with its level strip, on A3 or A4: Play straight and Play with a wave
(six swings a second, ±40 cents), 3 seconds each, and Play a swell, Lesson 14's, 4 seconds, straight
and on pitch. The toy waits for the cold open's choice, as every step's does: until then its
controls are disabled, under the line "Choose an answer at the top to unlock this figure."_

## Step 2 of 4: How fast, and how far?

A vibrato has two measures. Its rate is how many swings it makes a second. Its extent is how far
each swing reaches from the middle, up and down, in cents. Trained singers have been measured at
about 5 to 8 swings a second, usually under a semitone, ±100 cents; averages sit near six. Hertz
counts swings a second, as in Lesson 1. Merc's "ooh", heard since Lesson 1, carries a small
vibrato: 5.5 a second, ±14 cents, inside Lesson 5's band. [Play Merc's "ooh"]

**Try:** set Rate to 6 and Extent to ±30, then double Extent.
**Notice:** the count stays at 6 as the swing widens: rate and extent are separate.

_Toy: the band chart zoomed to one second, with a counter that ticks once a swing and a ruler from
the middle to the top of a swing; Rate, 5 to 8 swings a second, and Extent, ±10 to ±60 cents, with
Play, 4 seconds, its sound following the sliders. The toy waits for the cold open's choice._

Tavi: "So a wave that crosses the band isn't out of tune?"

> **Common mix-up:** "A wobbling note is out of tune."
> **Why it seems right:** the line leaves the band on every swing.
> **The evidence:** people match an even wave to a straight note near its middle, and in one
> experiment they judged the pitch of vibrato notes as well as straight ones.
> **The better idea:** Lesson 5 said a small vibrato can sit inside the band. A wider one swings
> past its edges, so from here the band holds the middle of the line. That middle is the note: if it
> drifts, the note drifts.

**Try:** slide Rate from 7 down to 2, then switch on Drift.
**Notice:** as the wave slows, the note starts to move. With Drift, the middle leaves the band.

_Toy: the band chart with Merc's wave at ±50 cents and its middle drawn as a dashed line; Play: six
a second and Play: two a second; Rate, 2 to 7 swings a second, with Play at this rate, and Drift,
which lifts the middle 50 cents over 3 seconds. The toy waits for the cold open's choice; Play: two
a second, Rate and Drift wait for this step's choice too._

> **Go deeper**
>
> - **Which middle?** One study found the note heard close to the geometric mean of the wave's top
>   and bottom frequencies: for Merc's wave, 220 Hz, A3 exactly. The plain average is 0.7 cents

## Step 4 of 4: Does a singer need one?

No. Choir experts' views range from a free vibrato, like a soloist's, to a minimal one or none, and
singers can change theirs. This lesson asks for none and scores none: a straight note is a finished
answer.

[Play the measured wave] Voice Mirror's hold reports a wave of 3.5 to 8.5 swings a second as, say,
"Vibrato 5.6 Hz, ±38 cents. That's a feature, not scored against you." The app's wavy ribbon for a
vibrato note is a mark: its waves follow how wide the note is drawn, not a singer's rate.

**Try:** play the measured wave, then stretch the note.
**Notice:** the ribbon gains waves as it widens; the measured line keeps its 11 swings.

_Toy: the band chart's last state, a measured wave of 5.5 swings a second at ±30 cents over 2
seconds, labelled "measured", beside the ribbon the app draws for a vibrato note, labelled "the
app's mark"; Stretch widens the note. The toy waits for the cold open's choice._

## Wrap

- A vibrato is a regular wave in a held note's pitch, measured by its rate, in swings a second, and
  its extent, in cents.
- Quick enough, and we hear one note, at the middle of the swing. Slow it right down, and the ear
  follows it.
- A straight note and a vibrato are both used in singing, and both are finished notes.

### The holds

- **The two holds and their order**: Hold 1, straight, then Hold 2, floating, ten seconds each, on
  A3 or A4, moved up to four semitones either way with Lower and Higher, as in Lesson 5. Hold 2 comes
  only after "Easy" or "Workable" on Hold 1 and a rest the singer ends; up to three takes a hold
  (FACTS 51, 54, 55).
- **To confirm: Hold 1's cue**, "ten seconds on your note, as level as feels easy". It asks for a
  straight note without forcing one; for a voice with a vibrato of its own, holding it back may take
  effort. The alternative is "ten seconds on your note, as in Lesson 5", with no word about a wave
  (FACTS 54).
- **To confirm: Hold 2's cue**, the card's words: "Let the note float. If a wave comes, let it; if
  it doesn't, the note is just as finished." (FACTS 54).
- **To confirm: Merc's model notes**: straight before Hold 1, and before Hold 2 his usual "ooh",
  whose small wave, 5.5 a second at ±14 cents, sits inside the band and is the model every Season 1
  hold has used. The alternative is a straight model before both, so no wave is modelled at all
  (FACTS 56).
