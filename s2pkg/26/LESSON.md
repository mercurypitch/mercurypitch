## Step 1 of 4: What's a third above each note?

A third spans three letters, both ends counted, as in Lesson 23: C, D, E. Above each note of C
major, take the key's own note three letters up: C to E, D to F, E to G. [Play the thirds up the
scale] Counted in semitones, they're not all one size. That's why Lesson 12's chord on re, D, F and
A, is minor: D up to F is 3.

**Try:** walk the third up the scale, from do to ti.
**Notice:** 4, 3, 3, 4, 4, 3, 3: major on do, fa and so; minor on re, mi, la and ti.

## Step 2 of 4: Is a harmony the tune moved up?

[Play ‘Twinkle’ with its third above] Back to ‘Twinkle’ and the copy that stays in C major. It rises
and falls with the tune, a third above, in both sizes: four semitones over C, F and G, three over D,
E and A. Both sizes count as consonances. Moved four semitones everywhere, a copy keeps one size and
leaves the key.

> **Common mix-up:** "A harmony is the tune moved up by the same distance everywhere."
> **Why it seems right:** a harmony in thirds does move up and down with the tune.
> **The evidence:** four semitones above re gives F♯, but C major's third above re is F, three
> semitones up.
> **The better idea:** keep the tune's shape and the key's notes. The key decides each third's size.

**Try:** switch the copy between C major's thirds and four semitones up.
**Notice:** the copy's notes over D, E and A move up a semitone, onto F♯, G♯ and C♯, and every
third turns to 4.

(Held until the cold open's reveal, the text above, its play, the mix-up card, Try and Notice show
with it. Until then their place reads "Hear the copy at the top to unlock this step." FACTS 1a,
41.)

_Toy: the tune line with "Twinkle" and its copy a third above, on C major's lanes, a bracket between

## Step 4 of 4: What's the note in the middle?

Under the part above, hold a C major chord: C, E and G. [Play mi, fa, so over the chord] Mi and so,
E and G, are chord notes. Fa, F, isn't: it steps from one to the other, in one direction, a passing
note. [Play do, re, mi over the chord] The tune does it too: re passes from do to mi.

**Try:** play the tune and the part together over the chord.

## Wrap

- A harmony in thirds takes the key's own note a third above or below each note of the tune: some
  thirds are 4 semitones, some 3.
- It moves with the tune, above or below. An octave further apart, a third is a tenth.
- A note outside the chord that steps from one chord note to the next, in one direction, is a
  passing note.

**One-sentence challenge:** explain to a friend, in one sentence, why moving every note of a tune up
four semitones doesn't give a harmony in its key.
