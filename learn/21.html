Four words you'll meet before the first step

Key, key signature, circle of fifths, equal temperament

Key

The scale a piece uses, named after its do: G major's do is G.

Key signature

The sharps or flats written once at the start of each staff, in place of a sign before every note.

Circle of fifths

The twelve major keys in a ring, each a fifth from the next.

Equal temperament

Tuning that makes every semitone the same size, 100 cents.

1Step 1 of 5

Why does G major need F♯ sharp?

Build Lesson 4's pattern on G with white keys only: G, A, B, C, D, E, F, G. The first five steps are right. Then come a semitone and a tone, the wrong way round. Raise F to F♯ sharp and the last step is a semitone again: ti sits just under do. G major's one sharp is its ti. On the staff, write that sharp once, at the start: that's G major's key signature, and it makes every F a sharp, in every octave.

TrySwitch White keys only on and off, and play the scale each time.

NoticeOnly one note has to move, the seventh.

2Step 2 of 5

Why does each key a fifth up add a sharp?

Thread Lesson 2's ring again, a fifth at a time: C, G, D, A. A key a fifth up starts on the last key's so. Its first six notes are ones the last key had. Its seventh, ti, was the last key's fa, a tone under the new do: it has to rise a semitone. C to G adds F♯ sharp, G to D adds C♯ sharp, D to A adds G♯ sharp, each a fifth above the last. The order of sharps is F, C, G, D, A, E, B.

TryStep the house from C to A, listening to each scale.

NoticeEach key keeps the last key's sharps, and adds one.

3Step 3 of 5

Where do the flats come from?

TaviA♯ sharp and B♭ flat are one piano key. Can't F major use A♯ sharp?

Half right. On white keys, the fourth note from F, B, sits a tone above A, where the pattern needs a semitone: it falls to B♭ flat. A♯ sharp would sound the same, but a scale uses each letter once. A fifth down each time, each key adds a flat on its fa: B♭ flat for F major, E♭ flat for B♭ flat major, A♭ flat for E♭ flat major. The order of flats is the sharps', backwards: B, E, A, D, G, C, F.

Common mix-up

“A♯ sharp and B♭ flat are the same piano key: a key can use either.”

Why it seems right: they're one piano key, with one sound.

The evidence: F major with A♯ sharp would read F, G, A, A♯ sharp, C, D, E: two As, and no B.

The better idea: one letter for each step. The key decides which name the note takes.

TrySwitch the fourth note between A♯ sharp and B♭ flat.

NoticeOnly B♭ flat puts a note on every letter.

4Step 4 of 5

How do I find do from a key signature?

Read the signature, then find home. With sharps, the last sharp is ti, and do is a semitone above it. Three sharps, F♯ sharp, C♯ sharp and G♯ sharp: G♯ sharp is ti, and do is A. With flats, the second-to-last flat is do. Two flats, B♭ flat and E♭ flat: do is B♭ flat. One flat means F major, and no sharps or flats, C major. Wherever do lands, the house sits there, and re, mi and the rest follow. In each clef, a signature's signs always sit in the same places.

TryPlay Find do: a key signature appears in your clef. Tap where do goes, then hear it.

NoticeA sharp or a flat in the signature counts for its letter in every octave.

5Step 5 of 5

Why does the circle close after twelve?

Walk twelve fifths up from C and you meet every note once, then land on a C, seven octaves up. Counting semitones, that's exact. But a pure fifth, two notes whose frequencies are exactly 3 to 2, is a hair wider than an equal-tempered one: about 702 cents, against 700. Twelve pure fifths overshoot seven octaves by about a quarter of a semitone, 23.46 cents. Equal temperament, the tuning MercuryPitch uses, makes each fifth about 2 cents narrower, and the ring closes.

TryPlay the walk with pure fifths, then with equal ones.

NoticeThe pure walk ends a little higher than it began: about 2 cents for every step.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • A major scale keeps Lesson 4's pattern on any note, with one letter for each step.
  • Each key a fifth up keeps its sharps and adds one on its ti, in the order F C G D A E B. Each key a fifth down adds a flat on its fa: B E A D G C F.
  • The last sharp is ti; the second-to-last flat is do. Twelve fifths close the circle because equal temperament narrows each one by about 2 cents.
