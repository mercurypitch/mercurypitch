Can you make a harmony by singing the tune a third higher?

TaviA harmony is just the tune, higher. Move every note up a third and you've got one.

MercLet's try a major third, four semitones, above every note.

Predict before you listen

‘Twinkle’ is in C major. Merc moves every note up four semitones and plays the copy with the tune. Will every note of the copy fit the key?

Choose an answer to unlock the copy.

Your guess

Some notes leave the key. Four semitones above A, E and D are C♯ sharp, G♯ sharp and F♯ sharp, three notes C major doesn't have; its own notes a third above them are C, G and F, three semitones up. That copy takes every third from C major's own notes. (The four-semitone copy is still ‘Twinkle’, as in Lesson 6, in another key, which made “every note fits the key” a fair guess.)

Three words you'll meet before the first step

Part, chord note, passing note

Part

The notes one voice sings while others sing theirs: a tune, or a harmony to it.

Chord note

A note of the chord sounding at that moment.

Passing note

Or passing tone: a note outside the chord that steps from one chord note to the next, in one direction.

What's a third above each note?

Hear the copy at the top to unlock this step.

A third spans three letters, both ends included, as in Lesson 23: C, D, E. Above each note of C major, take the key's own note a third up: C to E, D to F, E to G. Counted in semitones, they're not all one size. That's why Lesson 12's chord on re, D, F and A, is minor: D up to F is 3, then F up to A is 4.

TryWalk the third up the scale.

Notice4, 3, 3, 4, 4, 3, 3: major thirds on do, fa and so; minor thirds on re, mi, la and ti.

2Step 2 of 4

Is a harmony the tune moved up?

Hear the copy at the top to unlock this step.

The copy that stays in C major rises and falls with the tune, a third above, in both sizes: four semitones over C, F and G, three over D, E and A. Moved four semitones everywhere, a copy keeps one size and leaves the key.

Common mix-up

“A harmony is the tune moved up by the same distance everywhere.”

Why it seems right: a harmony in thirds does move up and down with the tune.

The evidence: four semitones above re gives F♯ sharp, but C major's third above re is F, three semitones up.

The better idea: keep the tune's shape and the key's notes. The key decides each third's size.

TrySwitch the copy to four semitones up, then back.

NoticeThe copy's notes over D, E and A move up a semitone, to F♯ sharp, G♯ sharp and C♯ sharp: every third is 4.

3Step 3 of 4

Above or below?

A harmony can sit a third below the tune too. Above do re mi re do, it's mi fa so fa mi. Below, it's la ti do ti la.

Tavi sings an octave above Merc. With Merc on the tune and Tavi a third above, their third becomes a tenth: an octave and a third, the same letter names, still major or minor.

TaviWhichever side I'm on, I count three letters, in the key.

TryPlay the part above, then below.

NoticeBoth move with the tune, every note in the key.

4Step 4 of 4

What's the note in the middle?

Under the part above, hold a C major chord: C, E and G. Mi and so, E and G, are chord notes. Fa, F, isn't: it steps from one to the other, in one direction, a passing note. The tune does it too: re passes from do to mi.

TryPlay both together over the chord.

NoticeRe and fa pass at the same moment, still a third apart.

What this does: it measures how far each of your notes sits from your part's note, in cents, in any octave, listening for each note in its own beats, counted from your first note, and shows your pitch line after each run, not while you sing. It doesn't score your timing. It doesn't diagnose anything, and it isn't a test of whether you can sing. Your voice is analysed on this device: nothing is recorded, kept or sent.

    Three lines to keep

    • A harmony in thirds takes the key's own note a third from each note of the tune: some thirds are 4 semitones, some 3.
    • It moves with the tune, above or below.
    • A passing note, outside the chord, steps from one chord note to the next in one direction.
