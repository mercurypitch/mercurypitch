How do you name an interval?

An interval's name has two parts. The number counts letters, both ends included: C, D, E is three letters, so C up to E is a third. The quality says which third: C up to E is 4 semitones, a major third. C up to E♭ flat is still three letters, but 3 semitones: a minor third. The staff counts the letters, a line or a space each, and the pitch line the semitones. Seconds, sixths and sevenths come in major and minor too.

NoticeFlat changes the semitones, never the letters.

2Step 2 of 4

Is every interval major or minor?

TaviSo every interval comes major and minor, like the thirds?

Not the unison, fourth, fifth and octave: those are perfect. A semitone wider, the perfect fourth C up to F becomes augmented: C up to F♯ sharp. A semitone narrower, the perfect fifth C up to G becomes diminished: C up to G♭ flat. Both are six semitones, one piano key with two names (Lesson 2): the letters make one a fourth, the other a fifth. That's the tritone, “three tones”: C to D to E to F♯ sharp.

Common mix-up

“Every interval comes in a major and a minor.”

Why it seems right: seconds, thirds, sixths and sevenths do.

The evidence: unisons, fourths, fifths and octaves are perfect, never major or minor. A perfect or major interval made a semitone wider is augmented; a perfect or minor one made a semitone narrower is diminished.

The better idea: two families: perfect, and major and minor.

TryPress Sharp on F, then Flat on G.

NoticeOne piano key, two places on the staff.

Go deeper
  • Halfway in hertz. Halfway between C4, 261.63 Hz, and C5, 523.25 Hz, is 392.44 Hz: exactly 3: to 2 above C, a pure fifth, 2 cents above the piano's G. From there up to C5 is 4: to 3, a pure fourth. But your ear hears steps by multiplying, not adding (Lesson 2), so to the ear the halfway note is F♯ sharp.
  • Perfect is a name, not a promise. In counterpoint, the perfect fourth counts as a dissonance when it sits against the lowest voice, and as a consonance between two upper voices.

3Step 3 of 4

What happens if you turn an interval upside down?

Move the bottom note up an octave. C up to G, a fifth, becomes G up to C, a fourth. The numbers add up to 9, 5 and 4, and the semitones to 12, 7 and 5: the whole octave. A major third, 4 semitones, turns into a minor sixth, 8. Major turns minor and minor turns major, perfect stays perfect, and augmented and diminished swap.

TryFold a fifth, then a third, then C up to F♯ sharp.

NoticeThe numbers always make 9. The tritone folds onto six semitones again: it's its own upside down.

4Step 4 of 4

Why does the halfway interval want to move?

TaviAn exact half: shouldn't that be the calmest interval of all?

Exact, yes, but not settled: the tritone counts as a dissonance, less stable, as if it needs to resolve. Among the white keys, only F and B are a tritone apart: fa and ti in C major. Ti leans up to do, as in Lesson 4. Move each a semitone, B up to C and F down to E, and it closes into a major third: do and mi. Lesson 27 comes back to this pair.

TryPlay B and F, then curl the pitch line into a ring.

NoticeOn the ring, B and F sit straight across.

Go deeper
  • The devil in music. The tritone's old nickname is diabolus in musica, Latin for “the devil in music”. Johann Joseph Fux's counterpoint textbook of 1725 quotes the phrase in a proverb and bars the tritone leap in counterpoint.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • An interval's name has two parts: the number counts letters, both ends included, and the quality comes from the semitones.
  • Unisons, fourths, fifths and octaves are perfect; seconds, thirds, sixths and sevenths are major or minor. A perfect interval a semitone wider is augmented, a semitone narrower diminished.
  • Turn an interval upside down and the numbers make 9, the semitones 12. The tritone, six semitones, stays a tritone, and leans.
