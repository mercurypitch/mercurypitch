Your guess

Most of them. Tune 1 lands on a corner with 11 of its 15 notes, and tune 2 with 10 of its 12. Four words you'll meet before the first step

Diatonic, Roman numeral, diminished, augmented

Diatonic

Made only from the notes of one key's scale. In C major, that's the white keys.

Roman numeral

A chord's name from the scale degree it's built on: Ione on do, Vfive on so.

Diminished

A triad of two minor thirds, 3 and then 3 semitones: B, D, F.

Augmented

A triad of two major thirds, 4 and then 4 semitones: C, E, G♯ sharp.

Chords and the four-chord song

1Step 1 of 5

Where do a key's chords come from?

Lesson 4 numbered the notes of the major scale 1 to 7, do to ti. Lesson 10 stacked thirds into a triad. Take any note of the scale as a chord's root, its bottom note. Add the scale's notes a third and a fifth above it, skipping a note each time. On do, that's C, E, G. On re it's D, F, A, and on fa it's F, A, C. Seven notes give seven chords. In C major, every one uses only white keys: they're the key's diatonic chords.

TryTap each chip in turn, from do to ti.

NoticeNo room ever reaches a black key. Every corner is a note of C major.

2Step 2 of 5

How do Roman numerals name them?

Name each chord by its root's place in the scale, in Roman numerals. Capitals or small letters show its quality: capitals for major, small letters for minor. C major's seven read Ione, iitwo minor, iiithree minor, IVfour, Vfive, visix minor and vii°seven diminished. The small circle marks the one diminished chord, on ti. No chord built from a major scale is augmented. The numerals belong to the key, not to the letters. In G major, Ione is G, B, D, and Vfive is D, F♯ sharp, A, with the key's one sharp.

TrySwitch the key from C to G, then tap Ione, IVfour and Vfive again.

NoticeEvery room turns round the ring together, and each keeps its shape and its numeral.

Go deeper
  • Plain numbers. Not every text writes capitals and small letters. Ableton's free lessons call chords by plain numbers, such as “the 1 and 5 chords”, and some theorists write every numeral in capitals.

3Step 3 of 5

Why is the chord on la minor?

On la, the sixth degree, the chord is la, do and mi: A, C, E. A up to C is 3 semitones, and C up to E is 4. That's Lesson 10's minor shape, and its numeral is visix minor: A minor. In Lesson 10, the minor chord's root was do: do-me-so. Here its root is la, and we're still in C major. Do stays on C, and all three notes are the scale's own. Compared with Ione (C, E, G), it keeps C and E and swaps G for A.

TrySwitch the names to do-re-mi, then play Ione, then visix minor.

NoticeOne corner slides from G to A. The new room is the old one's mirror image.

4Step 4 of 5

Which four chords go round and round?

Play Ione, Vfive, visix minor and IVfour, then go back to Ione and round again. In C major that's C, G, A minor and F, built on do, so, la and fa. A series of chords played in order is a progression, and this one is a loop. Ableton's free lessons call it 1-5-6-4. Open Music Theory, a free textbook, lists it among its four-chord loops. Its four chords hold all seven notes of the scale between them. From Vfive to visix minor, all three notes move up a step.

TryPlay the loop, then play its roots alone.

NoticeThe roots go down to so, up to la, then down to fa.

Go deeper
  • Start on visix minor. Open Music Theory gives the same loop starting from visix minor, vi–IV–I–Vsix minor, four, one, five: the same four chords in the same order. It calls the turn that starts on Ione “exceedingly common” too.
  • Another loop. The same four chords make other loops. The one the textbook calls doo-wop runs I–vi–IV–Vone, six minor, four, five, and it says that loop was “common in 1960s pop music”.

5Step 5 of 5

If the chords match, is it the same song?

TaviSame chords, same song. Isn't that how it works?

Back to the cold open's two tunes, over the same four chords. Tune 1 walks down, mostly by steps, in even notes. Tune 2 leaps between corners in a long-short-long rhythm. Both land mostly on corners, and they're still two tunes. Now hear tune 2 with even notes: the same notes over the same chords, with only its rhythm changed.

Common mix-up

“Songs with the same chords are the same song.”

Why it seems right: one open textbook calls Ione, IVfour, Vfive and visix minor “probably the most common chords in all of pop music”, and says the loops made from them “all sound somewhat similar”.

The evidence: a song has two building blocks, its chords and its tune. Ableton's lessons say melodies “define the ‘identity’ of the song in many styles of music”: “if you ask someone to sing a particular song, they'll usually sing the melody”. This lesson's two tunes share every chord, in the same order, and they're still two tunes.

The better idea: the chords are the rooms; the song is the walk through them, its tune in its rhythm.

TryPlay tune 1, then tune 2, then tune 2 with even notes.

NoticeThe chords never change. Only the tune's path and its rhythm do.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • Stack the scale's notes a third and a fifth above each degree of a major scale and you get its seven chords. In C major they're all on white keys.
  • A Roman numeral names a chord by its root's degree. Capitals or small letters show its quality: Ione, IVfour and Vfive are major, iitwo minor, iiithree minor and visix minor minor, and vii°seven diminished diminished.
  • I–V–vi–IVone, five, six minor, four is C, G, A minor and F in C major. Two songs can share it and still differ: the tune, in its rhythm, can make the song.
