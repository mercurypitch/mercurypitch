A piano has 88 keys. How can five lines write down every one of them?

Predict before you look

Merc will draw five lines. From the bottom line to the top line, how many places will there be for a note?

Choose an answer to unlock the five lines.

Your guess

Nine. A note can sit on a line or in the space between two lines, so five lines and their four spaces give nine places, each the next letter up: E, F, G, A, B, C, D, E, F. A clef says which nine. Short extra lines carry on past the edges, and a second staff takes the low notes. That's this lesson's story. (One note to each line made “five” a fair guess.)

Four words you'll meet before the first step

Staff, clef, ledger line, grand staff

Staff

The five lines music is written on.

Clef

The sign at the start of a staff that names one of its lines, so you can count the rest.

Ledger line

A short line that carries the staff on past its top or bottom.

Grand staff

A treble staff and a bass staff joined, the way piano music is written.

1Step 1 of 5

Which notes does a staff hold?

Listen first. That's the G above middle C, G4, and the treble clef names its line: the clef curls round the second line from the bottom. Count letters from that line. One step up is the space above it, A, and one more the middle line, B. One step down is F, in a space, then E, the bottom line. Lines and spaces take turns, and each step up is the next letter. So the treble staff's lines read E, G, B, D and F, and its spaces F, A, C and E.

TryTap any line or space to hear its note and see its letter.

NoticeLine, space, line: no letter is ever skipped.

2Step 2 of 5

Is every step up the staff the same size?

TaviEach step up the staff looks the same. Isn't it the same step in sound?

In letters, yes: each step is one letter. In sound, no. E to F is a semitone and F to G a tone, and B to C is a semitone too: Lesson 4's two short steps. The staff counts letters, not semitones. To move a note by a semitone, write a sharp or a flat to its left: F♯ sharp stays in F's space, B♭ flat on B's line.

Common mix-up

“Every step up the staff is the same step in sound.”

Why it seems right: the lines and spaces are evenly spaced.

The evidence: E to F and B to C have no black key between them. On the pitch line they're a semitone apart, and every other pair of neighbouring letters a tone.

The better idea: the staff counts letters. A sharp or a flat adjusts the sound.

TryTurn on See it on the pitch line, then play E to F and F to G.

NoticeOne lane, then two.

3Step 3 of 5

What happens past the top or bottom line?

Keep climbing. G sits in the space just above the top line. A needs a short line of its own, a ledger line, and C, two letters higher, needs two. Below the staff it works the same way: D hangs under the bottom line, and middle C sits on one ledger line. A ledger line is drawn only as far as its note needs. This season reads up to two each way.

TryMove the note above the staff, then below it.

NoticeLedger lines appear only as far as the note goes.

4Step 4 of 5

Where do the low notes go?

Merc's voice sits low. On the treble staff, that F would need three ledger lines, so low voices read another clef. The bass clef names the second line from the top: F3, the F below middle C that Merc sang. Its two dots sit either side of that line. Count from it as before. The bass staff's lines read G, B, D, F and A, and its spaces A, C, E and G. Above it: B in the space, C on middle C's ledger line, D just over it.

TrySwitch Treble · Bass and tap the same line in each.

NoticeThe same line names a different note. The clef decides.

Go deeper
  • The octave treble clef. Tenor parts are often written in the treble clef, though they sound an octave lower. A small 8 under the clef, where it's drawn, says so.

5Step 5 of 5

Where do the two staves meet?

Put the treble staff above the bass staff, joined by a brace: that's the grand staff. Middle C sits on the one ledger line between them. Below the treble or above the bass, it's one key and one sound. Under the staves, every note has one key. So a piano writes its 88 keys, A0 to C8, on two staves of five lines, with ledger lines past their edges. Which clef is yours? This season, lower voices read the bass clef and higher voices the treble.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • A staff's five lines and four spaces hold nine notes, one letter each. A clef names one line, the treble clef G4 and the bass clef F3, and you count letters from it.
  • The staff counts letters, not semitones: E to F and B to C are semitones, the other steps tones.
  • Ledger lines carry the staff past its edges, and the grand staff joins the two staves at middle C.
