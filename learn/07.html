Range

The span from your lowest note to your highest.

Siren

One smooth glide up from a low note and back down.

Lip trill

Voice type

1Step 1 of 5

What does a siren do?

For a higher note, your vocal folds stretch longer and tighter (Lesson 3). A siren does that smoothly: one glide up from a low note and back down, sliding, never stepping. On the pitch line it draws a hill. When you sing one, keep it gentle, at about speaking volume, and go only as high as is easy.

Lesson 3's care lines still apply: stop if your voice feels scratchy or hoarse.

Sore, hoarse or unwell today? Just listen along. Every step works without singing.

2Step 2 of 5

Why lip trills and “ng”?

A lip trill sends the siren through loose, buzzing lips. “Ng” is the sound at the end of “sing”: the back of your tongue touches the soft palate, at the back of the roof of your mouth, so the sound leaves through your nose. Both partly close the sound's way out. Voice teachers and therapists have long used them, and in a computer model, partly closed lips raised the air pressure above the vocal folds. Do they help singers? Studies that test singers are still few.

TryPlay Merc's siren on “ng”, on a lip trill and on “oo”. Then, if you like, siren along once, quietly, on the easiest. Stop immediately if anything feels uncomfortable.

NoticeOne line for all three: the siren is the shape; the sound is your choice.

Go deeper
  • The evidence. The model is Ingo Titze's (2006). A review of voice training for singers found eight studies, four with exercises like these: most had no comparison group, and most results were the singers' own ratings, mostly positive (Carvalho and colleagues, 2026). In healthy voices, many studies look mainly at what changes straight away (Oliveira and colleagues, 2024).

3Step 3 of 5

How many notes can my voice reach?

Your range runs from your lowest note to your highest. Books often give a voice type two octaves, 24 semitones, and some voices reach further: these spans are a guide, not a rule. And a range can move. In one study, adults were recorded three times a day, on two days a month or two apart. For the average adult, the lowest note, highest note and range each varied by several semitones. A song needs only part of it.

TrySlide “Twinkle” up and down the column, playing it as you go.

NoticeIt fits in many places, but in the lit middle it has room to spare at both ends.

4Step 4 of 5

So why is the middle worth more?

Because a note there may need the least push. In a small study, eight adults with untrained voices sang their quietest note at ten pitches across their range. The air pressure each note needed seemed least near a comfortable pitch, and higher towards the top. More push also makes a note louder and a little higher (Lesson 5). Singers call that comfortable middle, where singing feels easiest, your tessitura.

TryPress and hold Higher: the note climbs the column, bottom to top.

NoticeThe push is lowest a third of the way up, and rises most towards the top.

Common mix-up

“A good singer has the widest range.”

Why it seems right: a range is one number, easy to count and compare.

The evidence: “Twinkle” spans 9 of a two-octave voice's 24 semitones. In one small study, a note needed the least push near a comfortable pitch. And teachers place solo singers in a voice type more by where their voices sit comfortably than by how far they reach.

The better idea: a song asks for a slice of your voice, sung with ease. Put the slice in your comfortable middle.

Go deeper
  • Pulled towards the middle. In one study, singers who sang off tended to sing notes above their comfortable note flat, too low, and notes below it sharp, too high (Pfordresher and Brown, 2007).

So am I a tenor, or an alto?

Predict before the reveal

Two singers have the same range. Same voice type?

Choose an answer to unlock Singer A and Singer B.

Your guess

Not always. Teachers also listen for where a voice is most comfortable, its colour (how it sounds) and where it changes gear (Lesson 11). Two singers who both reach from A3 to C6 could be a soprano, comfortable high, and a mezzo-soprano, comfortable lower. (A range is easy to compare, so “yes” was a fair guess.)

A voice type is a label, not a box: the spans overlap, and books draw them differently. In MercuryPitch, the voice type in Settings just picks the notes drills use; change it freely.

Go deeper
  • Partly built in. In X-rays of 132 singers, sopranos' vocal folds came out the shortest on average and basses' the longest (Roers and colleagues, 2009). Yet even scans of the throat picked out sopranos and altos correctly only about seven times in ten (Rast and colleagues, 2023).

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • A siren is one smooth glide up and back down. Warm up on “ng” or a lip trill, gently, at about speaking volume; studies of whether they help singers are still few.
  • A range can move, and a song uses only a slice of it: put the slice in your comfortable middle, where a note may need the least push.
  • A voice type is a label, not a box: teachers listen for where a voice sits comfortably, not only how far it reaches.
