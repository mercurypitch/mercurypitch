Four words you'll meet before the first step

Beat, tempo, bar, backbeat

Beat

The steady pulse under the music, the one you tap your foot to.

Tempo

How fast the beats come, counted in beats per minute, or BPM.

Bar

A group of beats, counted from 1. Written music ends each bar with a bar line.

Backbeat

A clap or a snare drum on beats 2 and 4 of a bar of four.

1Step 1 of 5

Where is the beat?

Tap your foot to a song and you're tapping its beat: a pulse that comes again and again, evenly spaced. People also say “a beat” for a drum pattern, but “the beat” is the pulse under it. A metronome makes the pulse plain: it clicks on every beat. This one has an arm that swings to one side, then the other, one swing a beat.

TryPress Start. Then tap the pad, or press Space, with each click.

NoticeThe clicks are evenly spaced. That evenness is what makes them a pulse.

2Step 2 of 5

How fast is the beat?

How fast the beats come is the tempo, counted in beats per minute, BPM. At 60 BPM a beat comes once a second; at 120, twice a second. A modern march typically moves at around 120. Slide the tempo and the clicks come closer together or further apart, but they stay evenly spaced: a faster beat is still a beat.

TryDrag Tempo, then tap along. The readout works out your taps per minute.

NoticeTap faster and your number climbs. The metronome's number counts the same thing: beats per minute.

3Step 3 of 5

Why do musicians count to four?

Here the metronome's beats are laid in a row, one pad each. They fall into groups called bars, each counted from 1. Musicians call beat 1 the downbeat; here its click is higher. Bars of 2, 3 or 4 count 1 2, 1 2 3 or 1 2 3 4. Written music shows the group with a time signature: in 3/4, the top number means three beats in a bar. ABRSM, a British exam board, starts Grade 1 theory with 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4.

TrySwitch the bars between 2, 3 and 4 and count aloud. Then play six rounds of Count the bar.

NoticeThe count goes back to 1 on every high click.

Count the bar

Six rounds

Each round plays two bars, then the next bar's first beat, with beat 1 on the high click. How many beats are in each bar?

Keys 2, 3 and 4 answer while you are in the game.

Go deeper
  • Two or four? Four beats split into two pairs, so a bar of four can feel like two bars of two. Open Music Theory, a free online textbook, notes that by ear alone it isn't always clear which one a piece is in.

4Step 4 of 5

What makes a backbeat?

Each sound gets a row of pads, a column per beat: the inside of a drum pattern. The kick, a bass drum played by foot, sits on the strong beats, 1 and 3. The hi-hat, two small cymbals, ticks twice a beat. Claps sit on the weak beats, 2 and 4: a backbeat. Many styles with four-beat bars have one, a basic rock beat among them. Move the claps off 2 and 4 and the backbeat goes: the rhythm changes, at the same speed.

TryMove the claps to 1 and 3 and play. Then move them back to 2 and 4.

NoticeOn 1 and 3 the claps land with the kick; on 2 and 4, between its hits.

So is rhythm just speed?

Predict before you listen

This march pattern hits beat 1 hard, BOOM-whack-whack-whack, at 120 BPM, a typical march tempo. Slowed to the rock beat's 90, is it the rock beat?

Choose an answer to unlock the play buttons.

Your guess

No. It's slower, but still BOOM on 1 and whack on 2, 3 and 4. The rock beat claps on 2 and 4 only. The tempo changed, not the rhythm. (They now share a speed, so “yes” was a fair guess.)

Orange MercSo the tempo is the speed, and the rhythm is what happens on the beats.

Common mix-up

“Rhythm is just how fast the song goes.”

Why it seems right: a faster song does sound different, and a tempo is one easy number.

The evidence: move the claps and the rhythm changes at the same speed; slow the march and it keeps its BOOM-whack-whack-whack. A song's style comes from its sounds and patterns as well as its tempo.

The better idea: tempo is how fast the beats come. Rhythm is the pattern of sounds on them: which beats are stressed, or made to stand out, and how they group into bars.

TrySwap the tempos: the march at 90, the rock beat at 120.

NoticeEach keeps its stresses at either speed.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • The beat is the steady pulse you tap along to; the tempo is how fast it comes, in beats per minute.
  • Beats group into bars of 2, 3 or 4, counted from the downbeat, beat 1. A time signature such as 4/4 says how many.
  • In a bar of four, claps or a snare on the weak beats, 2 and 4, make a backbeat. Move them and the rhythm changes, even at the same speed.
