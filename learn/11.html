Your guess

Every voice can. A flip is a change of register: a switch between your voice's gears, which every voice has. A sudden jump in pitch is the most common sign of that switch, in men's and women's voices, in speech and in song. (Classical singers train to hide it, so “a mistake” was a fair guess.)

Register

One of your voice's gears, a way your vocal folds vibrate.

Chest voice

The lower gear, the one most speech uses.

Head voice, or falsetto

Names often given to the upper gear; how they differ depends on who you ask.

Flip

A sudden switch between gears, often with a jump in pitch. Also called a crack or a break.

What changes when a voice flips?

Your voice has gears, called registers: different ways your vocal folds vibrate. In chest voice, the lower gear, the folds are thick and vibrate along their whole length. Most speech uses it. In head voice or falsetto, the upper gear, the folds are stretched thinner, and less of them vibrates. A flip is a sudden switch from one gear to the other. Around the middle of a voice, both gears can sing the same notes, and they usually sound different.

TryPlay one note in each gear.

NoticeThe same height on the chart, two sounds: the upper gear is closer to a plain tone. Merc's gears are a model of what voices tend to do.

Go deeper
  • Two more gears. Below chest voice, many researchers count a lowest gear, vocal fry, which usually makes a voice's lowest notes. Above head voice, some singers reach a whistle register, the gear researchers understand least. Singing mostly uses the two middle gears.

2Step 2 of 5

Why does a slide flip?

For a higher note, your vocal folds stretch longer and tighter (Lesson 3). On a slow slide up, the stretch grows smoothly. But at some point the layers of each fold stop moving together, and suddenly less of each fold vibrates. That's the switch, and the pitch jumps with it. Researchers have seen this even in a larynx studied in a lab, with no throat or mouth above it: a small, smooth change in stretch was enough to set off the jump.

TryPlay the slide slowly, and watch the stretch gauge.

NoticeThe stretch rises smoothly the whole way, but the line jumps at the seam.

Common mix-up

“A crack means you're doing it wrong.”

Why it seems right: a flip is sudden, it can happen without your choosing it, and classical singers train to hide it.

The evidence: a small, smooth change in stretch can set off the switch, even in a larynx in a lab. Every voice has the gears.

The better idea: a flip is your voice changing gear. If it comes, let it, gently.

3Step 3 of 5

Why only sometimes?

Around the middle of a voice, either gear can sing the same notes, and you may or may not get to choose which one sings. So a slide doesn't have to switch at one fixed note, and a short slide may not switch at all. In one study, 67 choir singers each slid up to their highest note. Listening, and looking at each slide drawn as a chart, researchers found a break in fewer than a third of the slides. The app can't tell which gear you're in.

TryPlay Merc's three model slides.

NoticeThe first two switch at different notes. The third stays in one gear the whole way, with no seam.

Go deeper
  • Up and down differ. In one lab study, the jump on the way up came at a higher note than the jump on the way down. The gear a voice is in can depend on the gear it was just in.

4Step 4 of 5

Can a flip get smoother?

Yes: singers can learn to smooth the switch until listeners hear no flip at all. The gears are still there. A lab instrument that tracks how the folds touch can still pick up the switch, and registers can't be removed. One guide for new singing teachers suggests slow two-note moves, shifted up and down through the switch, and sirens.

TryPlay Merc's five slow two-note slides.

NoticeThe jump shrinks with each slide, and the seam narrows to a hairline that stays. It's a model of what practice can do, not a promise.

5Step 5 of 5

What do other singers do with a flip?

It depends on the style. Classical singing works to hide the switch. Yodelling, from the Alps to parts of Africa, does the opposite: it uses the flip as an ornament, or as a whole way of singing. An international committee of voice experts was set up to agree on what registers are. It agreed: register effects can be fundamental to some kinds of singing. Whether to hide a flip or show it off is up to the style.

TryPlay the figure both ways: flipping on purpose, then hiding the switch.

NoticeThe same notes each time. Flipped, each leap across the seam changes gear, and you hear it. Hidden, the sound stays even.

Three lines to keep

  • Your voice has gears, called registers, and every voice has them. A flip is a sudden switch from one gear to the other.
  • On a slow slide, the switch comes when the layers of each fold stop moving together. Around the middle either gear can sing, so a flip comes only sometimes.
  • Practice can smooth the switch but never remove it. Classical singers hide it; yodellers show it off.
