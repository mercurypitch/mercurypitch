Your guess

In threes. Three and three make two groups, and each group is one beat: two beats, each split in three. Two, two and two make three groups: three beats, each split in two. Same six notes, same length of bar; the grouping makes the beat. That's this lesson's story. (A group of two has “two” in it, which made “in twos” a fair guess.)

Threes, offbeats and syncopation

1Step 1 of 4

What do the numbers in 6/8 mean?

TaviSix on top means six beats a bar, doesn't it?

In 4/4 the top number counts beats and the bottom names the beat's note. In 6/8 both numbers are about eighth notes: six of them, in two groups of three. Each group is a beat, a dotted quarter split in three, so the 8 names the split, not the beat. That's compound metre. 3/4 holds six eighths too, in three groups of two: three quarter-note beats, each split in two. That's simple metre. Beams join each beat's eighths.

Common mix-up

“The top number is how many beats you feel.”

Why it seems right: in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4, it is.

The evidence: a bar of 6/8 holds six eighth notes, but Open Music Theory, a free textbook, counts two beats in it: six divided by three.

The better idea: in 6/8 the top number counts eighths. Count the groups of three to find the beats, each a dotted quarter (in Britain, a dotted crotchet).

TrySwitch 6/8 · 3/4 and play each.

NoticeThe six notes don't move. The beams and the accents do.

How do you count 6/8?

Slowly, you can count all six, leaning on 1 and 4. At speed, count the two beats and say their thirds: “1 and a, 2 and a”. 3/4's six eighths count “1 and 2 and 3 and”. Books often say “1 la li”; this series keeps “la” for singing. An 1881 song book prints the round “Row, row, row your boat” in 6/8: each “mer-ri-ly” fills one beat, “1 and a”, then “2 and a”. Go deeper
  • Takadimi, the syllables from Lesson 17's Go deeper, counts each beat of 6/8 “ta ki da”, with “ta” on the beat. Its authors note that, unlike the other systems they looked at, it gives every place in a beat its own syllable.
  • A count for threes. In one study, young schoolchildren taught a count that told twos from threes improved more at recognising rhythms than children taught one that didn't.
  • Exam grades. In ABRSM's theory exams, triplets come at Grade 2 and 6/8 at Grade 3.

3Step 3 of 4

What's a triplet?

In 4/4 a beat usually splits in two: “1 and”. A triplet splits it in three, three notes in the time of two, marked with a 3. It borrows compound metre's split for one beat. Count it “1 trip-let”. This bar counts “1, 2 and, 3 trip-let, 4”. At 60 BPM the triplet's notes come a third of a second apart, as fast as the 6/8 tune's eighths.

TrySwitch Eighths · Triplet on beat 3, and play each.

NoticeBeat 3 still starts on its click. It just splits three ways.

4Step 4 of 4

What makes a rhythm syncopated?

An offbeat falls between the beats, such as on an “and”. A syncopation puts an accent there, off the beat. A note can start on an “and” and be held over the next beat, by a tie or a dot. A rest can take the beat. Or the “and” can simply be played louder. Here the note on beat 3 moves half a beat earlier, to “2 and”, and is held through beat 3, so nothing new starts on 3. It counts “1, 2 and (3) 4”.

TrySwitch On 3 · On 2 and, and listen to each.

NoticeWith the note on “2 and”, the click on 3 falls inside a held note.

Coda: the closing bars

Three lines to keep

  • Group six eighths in threes and you feel two beats, as in 6/8; group them in twos and you feel three, as in 3/4.
  • In 6/8 the top number counts eighths. Count it “1 and a, 2 and a”.
  • A triplet fits three notes into the time of two. An accent off the beat is a syncopation.
