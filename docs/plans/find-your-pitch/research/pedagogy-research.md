# Evidence-based pedagogy for "Find Your Pitch"

Research report for the MercuryPitch premium tutorial series (music theory and singing): a web
lesson page with motion graphics and a lesson explorer per lesson, plus a YouTube explainer per
lesson hosted by two mascots, blue Merc (teacher) and orange Merc (curious learner).

Compiled 16 October 2026. Every claim carries an inline citation (author, year, URL). DOIs are
given as `https://doi.org/...` links.

## How to read this report

**Method.** Web search plus direct checks of primary sources. Most journal claims were checked
against the paper's own abstract, pulled from the OpenAlex index by DOI, or against the full text
where it was open (Guo et al. 2014; the Seattle Singing Accuracy Protocol chapter; Brom et al.
2018; Lamesch et al. 2007). Where only a secondary summary was reachable, the claim is marked
**[secondary]**.

**Evidence strength tags** used throughout:

- **[Strong]**: several randomised experiments or a meta-analysis that agree.
- **[Moderate]**: a few experiments, or a meta-analysis with real moderators or caveats.
- **[Weak]**: single small studies, mixed results, or mostly theory.
- **[Practitioner]**: teaching tradition or expert opinion with little controlled testing.

## 0. Executive summary: the twelve findings that should shape every lesson

1. **Clear exposition alone can teach almost nothing to people who hold a misconception.**
   Muller's PhD studies found that a polished explanatory physics video left scores almost
   unchanged and made viewers _more_ confident in wrong ideas. A version that stated the
   misconception and then refuted it, either as narration or as a tutor-student dialogue,
   produced effect sizes of 0.79 and 0.83 against plain exposition
   ([Muller et al. 2008, JCAL](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x);
   [Muller, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20244)). A later meta-analysis
   of refutation text found g = 0.41
   ([Schroeder & Kucera 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z), effect size via
   [search summary of the PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8784251)). **[Strong]**
2. **The two-mascot format is supported, on one condition: orange Merc asks deep questions.**
   People who overheard two virtual agents in dialogue, with the tutee asking deep questions,
   learned more than people who heard the same content as a monologue
   ([Driscoll et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.2190/Q8CM-FH7L-6HJU-DT9W);
   [Craig et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci2404_4)). Shallow questions or
   comments from the tutee did not help. **[Moderate]**
3. **A mascot's mere presence does not help learning. What helps is what it does.** The image
   principle says showing an agent does not by itself improve learning
   ([Moreno et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1207/S1532690XCI1902_02);
   [Fiorella & Mayer 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-based-on-social-cues-in-multimedia-learning/093C1CDE75CF486DACD4CABC325ABE47)).
   Agents that gesture, especially pointing at the content, gaze at it and move like a person do
   improve learning ([Mayer & DaPra 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028616);
   [Wang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000221)). Overall agent effects are small
   ([Schroeder et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.49.1.a);
   [Castro-Alonso et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09587-1)). **[Moderate]**
4. **Predict, then reveal.** Asking a question before teaching the answer improves learning even
   when the guess is wrong ([Richland et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496);
   [Kornell et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015729)). This holds for video too
   ([Carpenter & Toftness 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2016.07.014)). Committing
   to a prediction creates surprise, and surprise predicts learning
   ([Brod et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.013)). **[Strong]**
5. **Cut everything that is not the idea. Never put music under a listening example.** Removing
   extraneous material helped in 23 of 23 tests, median d = 0.86
   ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E)).
   Interesting but irrelevant details hurt learning
   ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)). Background
   music evidence is mixed, but in a pitch lesson the music competes directly with the content.
   **[Strong]**
6. **Sound before symbol.** Every major music-pedagogy tradition (Pestalozzi and Mason, Kodály,
   Orff, Gordon, Suzuki) sequences hearing and singing before naming and notating. Controlled
   evidence is thin but points the same way. **[Practitioner, Weak experimental support]**
7. **Most people who think they are "tone deaf" are not.** About 17% of students call themselves
   tone deaf ([Cuddy et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1196/annals.1360.026)). Congenital amusia
   affects about 1.5% ([Peretz & Vuvan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejhg.2017.15)). Poor-pitch
   singing usually reflects a pitch-to-voice mapping problem, not a hearing problem
   ([Pfordresher & Brown 2007](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.25.2.95);
   [Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064)), and it is trainable
   ([Pfordresher 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.14815)). **[Strong]**
8. **Real-time visual pitch feedback helps, but only with knowledge of results (how far off,
   which direction), and it should be faded.** A pitch trace with no target did not help
   ([Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064)). Feedback against a target
   did help, and it was the only condition whose gains carried over
   ([Berglin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211026730);
   [Blanco et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693)). **[Moderate]**
9. **Match the reference to the singer.** People imitate voice-like timbres better than synthetic
   ones, and their own voice best
   ([Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064);
   [Pfordresher & Mantell 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2013.12.005)). The research
   standard (SSAP) matches model-voice gender and range to the participant and corrects octave
   artefacts ([Pfordresher & Demorest 2020](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/PfordresherDemorest_2020_AIRS_chapter.pdf)).
   Octave equivalence is partly learned, so it must be taught, not assumed
   ([Jacoby et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.08.020)). **[Moderate]**
10. **Test, space, interleave.** Retrieval practice beats rereading
    ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x);
    [Adesope et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306)). Spaced review beats massed
    review ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x)). Short tests
    inside online lectures cut mind-wandering
    ([Szpunar et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1221764110)). **[Strong]**
11. **Learners misjudge what works.** A fluent lecturer raised ratings of learning but not
    learning itself ([Carpenter et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0442-z)).
    Active classes felt worse to students and taught more
    ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)). Comments that say
    "so clear!" are not a learning metric. **[Strong]**
12. **Motivation: competence, autonomy, belonging, and forgiving streaks.** Music learners stop
    when those three needs are thwarted ([Evans et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735612441736)).
    Unbroken streaks drive engagement, but broken streaks cause drop-off unless they can be
    repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). Missing one day
    does not hurt habit formation ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
    **[Moderate]**

The full playbook of 25 rules is in Section 6.

## 1. Learning science

### 1.1 Cognitive load theory and dual coding

- **Working memory is narrow. Instruction that makes novices search, cross-reference or decode
  wastes it.** Cognitive load theory began with the finding that conventional problem solving
  uses capacity that would otherwise build schemas
  ([Sweller 1988](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4)). **[Strong]**
- **Dual coding: verbal and visual channels are partly separate, so words plus a matching picture
  give two retrieval routes** ([Clark & Paivio 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01320076)).
  Mayer's theory adds limited capacity per channel and active processing (selecting, organising,
  integrating) ([Mayer 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09842-1)). **[Strong]**
- **Music-specific evidence exists.** In music instruction, text integrated with notation, or
  explanations given aloud while the notation is shown, beat physically separated text and
  notation (split-attention and modality effects). Presenting music, explanation and notation
  _simultaneously_ beat presenting them one after another
  ([Owens & Sweller 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410701369146)). **[Moderate]**
- **Expertise reversal.** Supports that help novices (worked examples, integrated labels) can hurt
  more experienced learners ([Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)).
  Implication: let returning or trained users skip scaffolds in the explorer. **[Strong]**

### 1.3 Mascots on screen: helpful or distracting?

**Verdict.** A mascot is a small net positive when it carries social cues and _points the
learner's attention at the content_. It is neutral or negative when it is decoration competing
with a diagram.

- **Overall effect: small.** A meta-analysis of 43 studies (N = 3,088) found a small but
  significant learning benefit of pedagogical agents. It was larger for K-12 than for
  post-secondary learners, and larger when agents used on-screen text than narration
  ([Schroeder, Adesope & Gilbert 2013](https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.49.1.a)). A later
  meta-analysis found g+ = 0.20, with 2D agents (g+ = 0.38) beating 3D agents (g+ = 0.11). Most
  agent features (gesture, voice, motion) did not moderate the effect
  ([Castro-Alonso et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09587-1), figures via
  [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/133141)) **[secondary for figures]**. An earlier
  systematic review found 9 of 15 controlled experiments showed no learning difference
  ([Heidig & Clarebout 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2010.07.004), via
  [JOTLT summary](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/jotlt/article/view/3968))
  **[secondary]**.
- **Motivation.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 58 articles found agents raised self-efficacy and
  interest but not other motivational beliefs
  ([Gladstone et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2)). Self-efficacy is exactly
  what "I can't sing" learners lack.
- **Gesture is the active ingredient.** A gesturing agent improved transfer (d of about 0.8) and
  retention and drew more eye fixations to the target material. A _static_ agent added nothing
  ([Wang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000221)). High embodiment (gesture,
  expression, gaze, movement) improved transfer, but only with a human voice
  ([Mayer & DaPra 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028616)). A gesture meta-analysis found
  embodied agents improved retention and near transfer, though nearly all used pointing gestures
  ([Davis 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.05.002), via
  [Educational Technology Journal 2021](https://educationaltechnologyjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41239-021-00256-z))
  **[secondary]**.
- **Faces on the content itself help a little.** Adding anthropomorphic faces and pleasant colours
  to multimedia graphics improved retention (d+ = 0.39), comprehension (d+ = 0.32), transfer
  (d+ = 0.33) and intrinsic motivation (d+ = 0.26), and lowered perceived difficulty, with no major
  publication-bias problem ([Brom, Stárková & D'Mello 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.09.004),
  [author manuscript](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/emodesign_manuscript_1rev_180928_FINAL_norev.pdf)).
  A replication found similar effects ([Wong & Adesope 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09545-x)).
  Quicksilver droplet characters fit this "emotional design" evidence well. See also
  [Um et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026609) and
  [Mayer & Estrella 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.02.004).
- **Dialogue between two agents beats monologue, if the learner asks deep questions.** Students
  who overheard a virtual tutee ask deep questions wrote more relevant content than after a
  monologue. Tutee _comments_ or shallow questions gave no advantage
  ([Driscoll et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.2190/Q8CM-FH7L-6HJU-DT9W)). When a deep-reasoning
  question preceded each tutor sentence, vicarious learners outperformed every other condition,
  including the interactive tutor ([Craig et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci2404_4)).
  Students watching a tutoring session together learned as much as the tutee did
  ([Chi, Roy & Hausmann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/03640210701863396)). Muller's viewers
  "judged themselves to be similar to the student in the multimedia"
  ([Muller, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20244)).

**Design consequences.**

- Orange Merc is the viewer's proxy. Its questions should be the _why_, _how_ and _what if_
  questions a real beginner has, plus its honest wrong predictions. Not "Wow!" or "Cool!".
- Blue Merc points: a pseudopod or gaze toward the wave, key or spiral. The research's active
  ingredient is the deictic gesture ([Wang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000221)).
- When a diagram carries the explanation, shrink or park the mascots at the edge. Bring them back
  to react, ask or sing.
- 2D-reading characters may do better than heavy 3D ones
  ([Castro-Alonso et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09587-1)). A WebGL Merc that
  reads as a clear, flat silhouette is the safer choice. **[Weak inference]**
- Adults benefit less from agents than children
  ([Schroeder et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.49.1.a)), so the mascots must earn their
  place through function, not charm.

### 1.4 Retrieval practice, spacing and interleaving

- **Retrieval practice.** Testing produced better 2-day and 1-week retention than restudying,
  even though restudying made students _more confident_
  ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)). A
  meta-analysis found practice tests beat restudy and all other comparison conditions
  ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306)). Practice testing and
  distributed practice were the two techniques rated "high utility"
  ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)). **[Strong]**
- **Tests inside videos.** Interpolated memory tests in online lectures reduced mind-wandering,
  increased note-taking, improved learning and reduced test anxiety
  ([Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1221764110)). **[Strong]**
- **Spacing.** Across 839 assessments, the best gap between study sessions grows with how long
  you want to remember ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)). The
  optimal gap was about 20-40% of a 1-week retention interval and about 5-10% of a 1-year interval
  ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x)). Practically: review
  lesson 1 about 1-2 days later, again about a week later, then within lessons 3-5
  ([Kang 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215624708)). **[Strong]**
- **Music skills consolidate offline.** Piano-melody accuracy improved after sleep but not after
  equal waking intervals ([Simmons & Duke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/002242940605400308)).
  Sessions 24 hours apart improved accuracy more than sessions 5 minutes or 6 hours apart
  ([Simmons 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429411424798)). **[Moderate]**
- **Interleaving.** Mixing problem types improved delayed test performance
  ([Rohrer & Taylor 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8)). Interleaving painters'
  styles improved induction of style, while learners believed blocking worked better
  ([Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x)). A meta-analysis
  found interleaving works best when categories are similar to each other and varied within, and
  warned against it for expository text and words
  ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)). In music performance,
  interleaved practice schedules helped advanced clarinettists
  ([Carter & Grahn 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251)). Ear training (octave vs not
  octave, intervals, major vs minor) is a textbook case of hard-to-tell-apart categories. Once each
  category is introduced, interleave them. **[Strong in general, Moderate for music]**

### 1.5 Worked examples, fading and guided discovery

- **Worked examples.** For novices, studying worked examples beat solving equivalent problems
  ([Sweller & Cooper 1985](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3)). **[Strong]**
- **Fading.** Move gradually from full examples to independent problems by removing solution steps
  ([Renkl & Atkinson 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3)). **[Strong]**
- **Guidance beats unguided discovery.** Minimal guidance is less effective for novices
  ([Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1)). Across 164
  studies, unassisted discovery lost to explicit instruction (d = -0.38). _Enhanced_ discovery
  (feedback, worked examples, scaffolding, elicited explanations) beat other methods (d = 0.30)
  ([Alfieri et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021017), figures via
  [Utrecht PDF](https://www.fisme.science.uu.nl/publicaties/literatuur/2011_alfieri_discovery_learning.pdf)).
  **[Strong]**
- **Productive failure: try first, then instruction.** Problem solving _followed by_ instruction
  beat instruction-first (g = 0.36), but not for younger children or domain-general skills
  ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)). This fits the
  predict-then-reveal pattern. A short attempt is fine if teaching follows it. **[Moderate]**
- **Lesson explorer consequence.** Give it a question, constrained controls and immediate
  feedback. "Drag the second tone until the waves line up every cycle" beats a free sandbox.

### 1.6 Desirable difficulties, generation, pretesting and prediction

- **Desirable difficulties.** Varying practice conditions, interleaving, spacing and testing slow
  apparent progress but improve retention and transfer. They become undesirable if the learner
  lacks the background to respond
  ([Bjork & Bjork 2011](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)).
  **[Strong]**
- **Generation effect.** Generated items are remembered better than items that are read
  ([Slamecka & Graf 1978](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.4.6.592); meta-analysis
  [Bertsch et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193441)). **[Strong]**
- **Pretesting (prequestions).** Testing before reading improved learning even for items
  answered wrongly at pretest ([Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496)).
  Unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance later learning
  ([Kornell, Hays & Bjork 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015729)). With video, prequestions
  improved learning of both prequestioned _and_ non-prequestioned content
  ([Carpenter & Toftness 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2016.07.014)). The effect is
  robust but varies with procedure ([Pan & Carpenter 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5)).
  **[Strong]**
- **Prediction plus surprise.** Generating a prediction before seeing an outcome boosted
  learning. A pupil-measured surprise response appeared only when a prediction had been made,
  and it scaled with learning ([Brod, Hasselhorn & Bunge 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.013)).
  In children, predicting before seeing an outcome that contradicted a misconception increased
  surprise and belief revision, d = 0.56 ([Theobald & Brod 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13582)).
  **[Moderate]**
- **Fluency illusions.** Fluent instructors raise perceived learning but not actual learning
  ([Carpenter et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0442-z)). Students in active
  classrooms learned more and felt they learned less
  ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)). Blocked practice feels
  better and works worse ([Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x)).
  **[Strong]**

### 1.7 Seductive details and background music

- **Seductive details hurt.** Interesting but irrelevant additions reduced recall of main ideas
  and transfer. Placing them at the start was worst
  ([Harp & Mayer 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414)). Meta-analyses confirm a
  negative effect ([Rey 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003)), estimated at
  g = -0.33 across 58 studies ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4),
  figure via [e-teaching.org summary](https://www.e-teaching.org/materialien/literatur/sundararajan-adesope-2020))
  **[secondary for the figure]**. Summaries disagree on which moderators matter
  ([ACU summary](https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/seductive-details);
  [QIC-WD summary](https://www.qic-wd.org/sites/default/files/Umbrella%20Summary%20-%20Seductive%20Details%20in%20Training%20041821.pdf)).
- **Background music: genuinely mixed.**
  - Adding background music and sounds to a narrated animation reduced learning
    ([Moreno & Mayer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.92.1.117)).
  - Background music disturbs reading and has small negative effects on memory, but improves
    emotional response ([Kämpfe, Sedlmeier & Renkewitz 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735610376261)).
  - A 2023 meta-analysis (47 studies) found a small _positive_ mean effect (d = 0.31), largest when
    the music was played _before_ the assessment and for classical music
    ([de la Mora Velasco et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356231153070)).
  - **Interpretation for a pitch course.** Music under narration competes in the same channel as
    the narration _and_ as the content, because the content is sound. Bed music is
    defensible in intros, outros and transitions. It is not defensible under a listening example
    or a pitch-matching task. The lesson-01 brief already ducks the pad to silence under every
    listening example. Extend that to all dense explanation. **[Moderate]**
- **Interest can offset coherence costs.** In Muller's classroom study, an extended lesson with
  extra fascinating material produced the same learning as the concise one
  ([Muller, Lee & Sharma 2008](https://doi.org/10.14742/ajet.1223)). A wonder hook is acceptable
  when it is _about the idea_, for example the spiral of pitch, rather than unrelated trivia.

### 1.8 Video and animation evidence

- **Video helps.** Replacing other teaching with video gave g = 0.28. Adding video to existing
  teaching gave g = 0.80 ([Noetel et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654321990713)).
  **[Strong]**
- **Animation vs static pictures.** Overall d = 0.37, larger for representational animation
  (d = 0.40), and largest for procedural-motor knowledge (d = 1.06)
  ([Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.09.013), figures via
  [IPN record](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis)).
  See also [Berney & Bétrancourt 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2016.06.005). Singing is
  procedural-motor, so animated demonstrations of the voice doing the thing are well supported.
  **[Strong]**
- **Watching it being drawn.** Watching an instructor draw diagrams, with the hand visible, beat
  viewing finished diagrams for transfer (d = 0.35-0.58). Drawing without a visible hand did not
  help ([Fiorella & Mayer 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000065)). This is evidence for
  "build it on screen" motion (the 3Blue1Brown habit) _with a visible agent of the drawing_, a role
  Merc can take. **[Moderate]**
- **Engagement data, 6.9 million MOOC sessions** ([Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239),
  [full text](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)):
  - Shorter videos are much more engaging. Median engagement was at most about 6 minutes whatever
    the length. Recommendation: chunks under 6 minutes.
  - A talking head interspersed with slides beats slides alone.
  - A personal feel beats high-fidelity studio recordings.
  - Khan-style drawing beats slides ("motion and continuous visual flow").
  - Fast, enthusiastic speakers are more engaging.

  Caveat: this measures engagement in course videos, not learning, and not voluntary YouTube
  viewing. Veritasium and 3Blue1Brown routinely run 15-25 minutes. Read "6 minutes" as a segment
  length. Use chapters and internal checkpoints. **[Moderate]**

## 2. Misconception-based teaching: Muller (Veritasium) and Sanderson (3Blue1Brown)

### 2.1 Derek Muller's research (PhD, University of Sydney, 2008)

- **Thesis.** _Designing Effective Multimedia for Physics Education_, University of Sydney, 2008.
  A design-experiment approach with iterated cycles of design, evaluation and redesign
  ([Muller 2008, ComPADRE record](https://www.compadre.org/portal/items/detail.cfm?ID=11344);
  [Wikipedia: Derek Muller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Muller);
  [publication list, veritasium.com](https://veritasium.com/publications)).
- **"Saying the wrong thing" (JCAL 2008).** 364 first-year physics students were randomised to
  four treatments on Newton's laws:
  - Exposition.
  - Extended Exposition, with interesting extra information.
  - Refutation: misconceptions stated and refuted.
  - Dialogue: a student-tutor discussion of the same material.

  Refutation and Dialogue produced the greatest gains, d = 0.79 and d = 0.83 against Exposition.
  Low-prior-knowledge students benefited most, and high-prior-knowledge students were not harmed
  ([Muller, Bewes, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x)).
  **[Strong]**

- **"Raising cognitive load with linear multimedia to promote conceptual change" (Science
  Education 2008).** Students who watched a dialogue built around alternative conceptions
  invested _more_ mental effort and scored higher than students who watched a lecture-style
  presentation. Interviews showed a more active approach when misconceptions were raised.
  Viewers identified with the on-screen student
  ([Muller, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20244)). This is the key
  theoretical twist: for conceptual change, the goal is not minimum load but _useful_ load.
  **[Strong]**
- **The headline experiment, as Muller tells it.** On a 26-item test, students averaged about 6
  correct. After a clear expository video they still scored poorly, and some said they did not
  attend closely because they already knew it. After the dialogue video, in which a student voiced
  misconceptions and a teacher corrected them, scores roughly doubled to almost 12/26. Students
  called the expository video "clear, concise and easy to understand" and the dialogue
  "confusing". Muller: "Making a learner think seems best achieved in a social environment with
  other learners and a caring teacher" ([THE Journal 2015](https://thejournal.com/articles/2015/05/05/watching-videos-does-not-necessarily-lead-to-learning.aspx))
  **[secondary]**.
- **Vicarious conceptual change** in an authentic physics setting
  ([Muller et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9017-6)).
- **Coherence vs interest.** An extended lesson with fascinating extras matched the concise one in
  a real classroom ([Muller, Lee & Sharma 2008](https://doi.org/10.14742/ajet.1223)).
- **Refutation more broadly.** Refutation text research
  ([Tippett 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10763-010-9203-x)), with a meta-analysis showing
  g = 0.41 across 44 comparisons ([Schroeder & Kucera 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z)).
  A virtual human delivering refutation vs expository narration showed no difference in one study,
  and message trust predicted learning ([Schroeder et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/07356331221139859)).
  The _dialogue_ format, not just a refutation sentence, may matter. **[Moderate]**

### 2.2 How Veritasium structures videos

From a 2025 profile ([Béchard 2025, Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-youtube-star-derek-muller-of-veritasium-is-challenging-scientific/)):

- Muller holds that "some level of discomfort seems to be essential" for learning.
- The channel began with street-corner quizzes that let people's confidence break down, then
  revealed answers through slow motion and experiment, letting viewers test their own intuitions.
- Later videos layer demonstrations, expert interviews and gradual reveals, structured like a
  mystery, with misconceptions as the hook and Muller's own uncertainty on show.
- He observed that students nod along to demonstrations but retain little unless they predict
  first.

A teacher's description of the format is: open with plausible misconceptions so viewers are
unsure, then run the experiment, then explain why
([ChemEdX, "Veritasium in the Modeling Classroom"](https://chemedx.org/node/3390))
**[secondary]**.

**Veritasium skeleton for our videos:**

1. **Hook question** that the viewer thinks they can answer.
2. **Orange Merc's confident wrong answer**, the common misconception, stated sympathetically.
3. **Viewer prediction beat.**
4. **Demonstration or experiment**, which must be audible in a pitch course.
5. **Surprise:** orange's answer fails.
6. **Explanation**, built visually.
7. **Orange re-explains it in its own words** (generation; the viewer's proxy self-explains).
8. **Retrieval questions.**

### 2.3 Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown)

- **Concrete before abstract.** "Resist the temptation to start with the general abstract thing
  and then populate it with examples to explain later... Pretty much all textbooks and
  explanations do it the other way around"
  ([Kosner 2021, Dropbox Blog interview](https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/grant-sanderson-channels-his-passion-for-math-into-marvelously-i)).
  "The way that understanding works is you start at the lowest level you can get it"
  ([Lex Fridman Podcast #64, 2020, transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/lexfridman/grant-sanderson-3blue1brown-and-the-beauty-of-mathematics-lex-fridman-podcast-64)).
- **Visuals first, then meaning, gives ownership.** "When we put the visuals first, then start to
  articulate the meaning, you can get a sense of ownership over it"
  ([Stanford Daily 2020](https://www.stanforddaily.com/2020/01/24/3blue1brown-creator-grant-sanderson-15-talks-engaging-with-math-using-stories-and-visuals)).
- **The "could have discovered it" goal.** From his "about" page as quoted in 2019: the aim is to
  find "that little nugget of explanation that really clarifies why something is true" so "you
  come away feeling that you could have discovered the fact yourself"
  ([Hawking 2019, Guardian via Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/opinion/op-eds/science-never-quite-clicked-for-me-then-i-discovered-youtube-1.62464159)).
  The Essence of Calculus series opens with the goal of making viewers feel they could have
  invented calculus ([quote collection](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-grant-sanderson/);
  [Manning course description](https://www.manning.com/livevideo/3blue1brown-essence-of-calculus))
  **[secondary]**.
- **Curiosity is the hook. The visual is the prompt.** "That bit of curiosity is the hook of the
  video... The visual is heavily intertwined into the story, it is the prompt"
  ([Kosner 2021](https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/grant-sanderson-channels-his-passion-for-math-into-marvelously-i)).
- **Summer of Math Exposition criteria** ([Sanderson 2021](https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1)):
  - Clarity: explain jargon, with empathy for newcomers.
  - Motivation: "It should be clear to the reader/viewer within the first 30 seconds why they
    should care."
  - Novelty: covering common topics in better ways is valuable.
  - Memorability: beauty, enthusiasm or an aha moment.
  - Length: the substance should be visible in a 5-10 minute view.
- **JMM 2023 JPBM lecture, "Math's pedagogical curse".** Per a viewer's summary, the checklist
  covers motivating examples for definitions, rediscoverability, the author's personality,
  diagrams for key ideas, and making relative importance clear
  ([zeli.app summary of HN comment](https://zeli.app/de/story/49940482);
  [VoLL-KI 2023](https://www.voll-ki.fau.de/en/?p=433): diagrams, concrete examples, motivating
  questions; [lecture page](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/pedagogical-curse))
  **[secondary; verify against the lecture]**. Also: "People don't want to feel like they're just
  being watered down or talked down to"
  ([quote collection](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-grant-sanderson/))
  **[secondary]**.
- **Evidence alignment.** "Concrete first" matches concreteness fading: begin with concrete,
  manipulable representations and fade to abstract ones
  ([Fyfe et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9249-3)).

**Synthesis: Veritasium plus 3Blue1Brown.** Muller supplies the _conflict_: confront the
misconception and make the viewer predict. Sanderson supplies the _construction_: concrete visual
first, one object that transforms into the next idea, and a path the viewer feels they could have
walked alone. The Merc pair can do both. Orange carries the wrong intuition. Blue builds the
concrete picture until the right idea feels inevitable.

## 3. Music pedagogy

### 3.1 Sound before sign or symbol

- **Pestalozzi and Lowell Mason.** Mason's 1834 _Manual of the Boston Academy of Music_ lists
  first "to teach sounds before signs". Pupils were to sing before learning notation, discover
  through ear and imitation rather than explanation, practise rhythm, melody and expression
  separately, and master each step before the next
  ([MSU thesis quoting the Manual](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/39681/FULL_TEXT/view);
  [Wikipedia: music education in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_education_and_programs_within_the_United_States))
  **[Practitioner, secondary]**.
- **Experimental support (limited, consistent).**
  - Combining auditory and visual presentation of rhythms sped learning and retention over
    auditory-only or visual-only ([Shehan 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/3344987)). This
    supports "sound first, then sound plus symbol", not symbol alone.
  - In a three-year study of 157 beginning instrumentalists, the amount of practice and reading
    rehearsed notation were inadequate explanations of progress. The sophistication of mental
    strategies, assessed across sight-reading, memory, ear playing and improvising, mattered more
    ([McPherson 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735605048012)).
  - **[Weak-Moderate]**
- **Implication.** In every lesson the learner _hears_ (and ideally sings) the phenomenon before
  it is named, and names it before seeing notation. For lesson 1: hear two tones, hear them fuse
  at 2:1, then call it "octave", then show A3/A4, then (later lessons) the staff.

### 3.2 Kodály

- **Principles.** Sound before symbol, singing first, folk material, pentatonic starting
  material, movable-do solfège reflecting scale function, Curwen hand signs placed vertically to
  show pitch height ([Wikipedia: Kodály method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kod%C3%A1ly_method);
  [Dynamic Music Room](https://dynamicmusicroom.com/kodaly-solfege/))
  **[Practitioner, secondary]**.
- **Rhythm syllables.** Lineage from Kodály-era syllables to Takadimi
  ([Journal of Music Theory Pedagogy, rhythm syllable pedagogy](https://journals-upgrade.shareok.org/jmtp/article/view/649)).
  Among second and third graders, a syllable system that distinguishes duple from triple
  subdivision improved recognition more than one that did not. Word-based patterns improved
  performance and notation most ([Colley 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345075)). **[Weak-Moderate]**
- **Hand signs.**
  - Gesture and movement training improved children's intonation in a randomised
    three-group study ([Liao & Davidson 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0255761415614798)).
  - An earlier qualitative study found gestures served focus, guidance and intonation
    ([Liao & Davidson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/0255761407074894)).
  - Curwen-sign-specific studies are small and mixed
    ([Boisvert 2019, Université Laval record](https://crires.ulaval.ca/publication/61a6d84d036ba74f163684e5);
    [University of Calgary thesis](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/23172))
    **[Weak, secondary]**.
  - **Implication.** Merc's pseudopod can trace pitch height while singing: a vertical gesture
    mapped to pitch. This is supported by the embodiment research and by gesture-intonation
    research.

### 3.3 Dalcroze eurhythmics

- Body movement as a route from concrete doing to abstract understanding
  ([Juntunen & Hyvönen 2004](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0265051704005686), a theoretical
  paper). A 2023 PRISMA review of 21 studies reports broadly positive musical and non-musical
  effects, but most studies are small
  ([Positive Impact of Dalcroze Eurhythmics: A Systematic Review, 2023](https://hrmars.com/ijarbss/article/view/24387/Positive-Impact-of-Dalcroze-Eurhythmics-A-Systematic-Review)).
  **[Weak]**
- **Implication.** For rhythm lessons, ask viewers to move (tap, step, sway) before reading. In
  the web explorer, tap-along input can stand in for movement.

### 3.4 Orff Schulwerk

- Musicianship through singing, playing, speech and movement, with process over performance. The
  stages are imitation, exploration, improvisation and composition
  ([AOSA, The Orff Schulwerk Approach](https://aosa.org/about-aosa/the-orff-schulwerk-approach/);
  [Wikipedia: Orff Schulwerk](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schulwerk)) **[Practitioner]**.
- **Implication.** Speech rhythms are a low-threshold entry to rhythm for adults ("ap-ple",
  "wa-ter-me-lon"). Each lesson ends with a tiny _creative_ act: make your own two-note octave
  call.

### 3.5 Edwin Gordon's Music Learning Theory

- **Audiation:** "comprehension and internal realization of music", hearing music that is not
  physically present, which Gordon framed as what thought is to language
  ([Wikipedia: Gordon music learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory)).
- **Skill learning sequence** (same source):
  - Discrimination learning: aural/oral, then verbal association, then partial synthesis, then
    symbolic association, then composite synthesis.
  - Inference learning: generalisation, creativity/improvisation, theoretical understanding.
  - Students first imitate tonal and rhythm patterns on neutral syllables.
  - **[Practitioner]**
- **Evidence link.** Poor-pitch singing correlates with weaker auditory imagery
  ([Pfordresher & Halpern 2013](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Halpern_2013_PBR.pdf)).
  A model ties singing accuracy to imagery-based pitch-to-vocal-fold associations
  ([Pfordresher, Halpern & Greenspon 2015](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2015.32.3.242)). Audiation
  practice ("hear it in your head, then sing") has a plausible mechanism. **[Moderate mechanism,
  Weak intervention evidence]**
- **Implication.** Sequence each lesson as: hear and imitate on "ooh" (aural/oral), then attach
  the name (verbal), then show the symbol (symbolic), then generalise and create. Add "audiate
  first" prompts: "hear Merc's note in your head... now sing it".

### 3.6 Suzuki (ear first)

- Modelled on first-language learning: daily listening, imitation, repetition and praise, with
  reading introduced only after the child plays several pieces well
  ([UWSP Suzuki handbook](https://www3.uwsp.edu/suzuki/Documents/research/handbook/Method.pdf);
  [Wikipedia: Suzuki method](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Suzuki_method)). Critics note the
  language analogy has limits ([Comeau, University of Ottawa](https://piano.uottawa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Publications/Methods/Suzuki%20Method/Natural%20Learning%20Process%20article.pdf))
  **[Practitioner]**.
- **Implication.** Repetitive, varied listening to the target sounds (the octave, the scale)
  across lessons. Supports a "listening library" in the explorer.

### 3.7 Movable do vs fixed do

- In four Texas high schools (N = 414), movable-do students sight-sang significantly better than
  fixed-do students. The authors caution that groups also differed in private lessons, the
  consistency of early solfège training and assessment procedures. Years of choir experience was
  the strongest predictor ([Demorest & May 1995](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345676)). A 2012
  dissertation with college music majors found the opposite, favouring fixed do for chromatic
  material ([USF dissertation](https://repository.usfca.edu/diss/38)) **[secondary]**. Overall
  the evidence is mixed and confounded. **[Weak]**
- **Reasoning for adults.** Most listeners hear music relationally. Absolute pitch is rare and
  appears to depend on early learning, roughly before age 6
  ([Takeuchi & Hulse 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.113.2.345)). A movable system
  (scale degrees or movable do) matches how adult beginners actually hear. **[Moderate inference]**
- **Implication.** Teach scale-degree function (movable do or numbers) for hearing and singing.
  Teach letter names for the keyboard and staff. Do not force fixed do on beginners.

### 3.8 How adults learn theory

- **Adult barriers** include time, confidence, anxiety and fit with life
  ([Making Music UK symposium](https://www.makingmusic.org.uk/node/46594);
  [scoping review on older adults' self-efficacy](https://revistaseug.ugr.es/index.php/dedica/article/view/369-392))
  **[Weak]**.
- **Self-assessment.** Self-evaluations of singing correlate fairly well with measured ability
  (r = 0.66) in a large twin sample. Not every "I'm bad" is inaccurate, so respect the learner's
  starting point while showing it can move ([Yeom et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15081)).
- **What the general evidence predicts for adults** (Sections 1 and 5):
  - Brief conversational lessons ([Ginns et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-013-9228-0)).
  - Respect for prior knowledge, with skippable scaffolds
    ([Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)).
  - Autonomy ([Evans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1029864914568044)).
  - Not being talked down to ([Sanderson, quote collection](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-grant-sanderson/)).

### 3.9 Pitch, height and loudness: the spatial metaphor problem

- **"High" and "low" are ambiguous words.** In French, _haut/bas_ (like English high/low) label
  pitch, space _and intensity_. French children taught single-meaning terms (_aigu/grave_)
  labelled pitch more accurately ([Costa-Giomi & Descombes 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345594)).
  First graders discriminated pitch direction nonverbally better than they could name it
  ([Hair 1977](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.2307/3345304)).
- **The metaphor is language-shaped.** Dutch speakers (high/low) and Farsi speakers (thin/thick)
  represent pitch differently, and training changed this
  ([Dolscheid et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612457374)). Dutch children did not
  reliably map height to pitch until about age 11
  ([Oxford record, Dolscheid et al. 2022](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:de15b129-68c2-4083-85ee-2a0bbbc22c69/files/r8623hz47v))
  **[secondary]**. Adults who use the metaphor show a vertical pitch-space mapping (SMARC effect)
  ([Rusconi et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2005.01.004)).
- **Pitch and loudness genuinely interact.**
  - The dimensions interact perceptually ([Melara & Marks 1990](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207084)).
  - Dynamic frequency change alters judged loudness
    ([Neuhoff, McBeath & Wanzie 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.25.4.1050)).
  - In the voice, fundamental frequency rises 2-6 Hz per cm H2O of subglottal pressure, so
    pushing harder raises pitch, and beginners tend to get louder as they go higher
    ([Titze 1989](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.397562)).
- **Evidence status of "people confuse high and loud".** Well supported for children's
  vocabulary and for language ambiguity. Direct adult evidence is thin. Keep it as orange Merc's
  _question_, as lesson-01 FACTS.md does. **Recommendation:** add Costa-Giomi & Descombes (1996)
  to FACTS.md item 4. It is the most direct source, and Hair (1977) is about labelling pitch
  direction, not loudness specifically.
- **Implication.** Use one consistent visual grammar everywhere:
  - Pitch is vertical position and wave speed.
  - Loudness is size or brightness or wave height.
  - Show a loud-low next to a quiet-high early, to pull the two apart.

### 3.11 Attention focus and practice structure in music

- An **external focus** on the effect (keys, hammers, sound) gave more accurate keyboard
  performance than an internal focus on the fingers, and the more distal the focus the better
  ([Duke, Cash & Allen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429410396093)). Trained singers' "ring"
  and overall tone quality were rated higher as focus moved farther from the body ("fill the
  room", distant targets) than with focus on the soft palate
  ([Atkins 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429416673842)). General motor-learning review:
  [Wulf 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2012.723728). **[Strong general, Moderate for
  singing]**
- **"It's not how much; it's how."** Practice time and number of trials did not predict next-day
  retention. The proportion of correct trials and fewer errors in practice did
  ([Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429408328851)). **[Moderate]**

## 4. Singing pedagogy: what is evidence-based

### 4.1 Voice science basics worth teaching

- **Source and filter.** The vocal folds create the voiced sound, and the vocal tract shapes it.
  Semi-occlusion raises supraglottal pressure, increases source-tract interaction and can make
  phonation more efficient and economical in tissue collision
  ([Titze 2006](<https://doi.org/10.1044/1092-4388(2006/035)>)).
- **Pitch rises with pressure.** This explains why beginners go sharp and loud together
  ([Titze 1989](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.397562)).

### 4.2 Semi-occluded vocal tract (SOVT) exercises: straw, lip trill, hum, "ng"

- **Rationale.** Lip trills, tongue trills, bilabial fricatives, humming and tube or straw
  phonation have long been used. Titze's simulation gives the physical rationale above
  ([Titze 2006](<https://doi.org/10.1044/1092-4388(2006/035)>)). **[Moderate mechanism]**
- **Outcomes.**
  - In a sham-controlled trial in dysphonic patients, lip-trill and straw programmes improved
    objective voice quality, and water-resistance therapy was rated most comfortable
    ([Meerschman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/1460-6984.12431)).
  - Reviews in dysphonic and untrained adults report reduced phonation threshold pressure and
    perceived effort ([Pozzali et al. 2024; Apfelbach & Guzmán 2024, via ASHA Evidence Maps](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/5baa3e22-e96e-4b14-a922-e848d530023f))
    **[secondary]**.
  - In 14 professional singers, 21 days of straw or lip-trill practice (four 5-minute sessions a
    day) improved perceived physical vocal function, with no difference between the two
    ([Dargin 2016, KU ScholarWorks](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/entities/publication/bf2ca266-e148-43eb-86f6-910235efb300);
    results as reported in a [Voice Foundation 2025 abstract](https://voicefoundation.org/view/2025-abstracts/entry/3014/))
    **[secondary for results]**.
  - Singer-specific evidence is small. **[Moderate for safety and ease, Weak for
    singer-specific performance gains]**
- **Implication.** Use SOVT as the default warm-up and the "safe sound" for pitch slides
  (sirens on lip trill or "ng"). They are low-effort, low-volume and hard to strain.

### 4.3 Breath support: myths vs evidence

- **"Sing from your diaphragm" is at best shorthand.**
  - The diaphragm is an inspiratory muscle. Some singers use it only during inhalation and to
    reduce subglottal pressure at high lung volumes, and others co-contract it through the phrase
    ([Sundberg 1992, KTH STL-QPSR](https://www.speech.kth.se/qpsr/1992/1992_33_1_049-064.pdf);
    [Leanderson, Sundberg & von Euler 1987](https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1987.62.1.259))
    **[secondary for the 1987 paper's details]**.
  - Professional classical singers used a greater abdominal contribution to lung volume and
    showed consistent pre-phonatory inward abdominal-wall movement. Untrained people did not
    ([Salomoni, van den Hoorn & Hodges 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0155084)).
  - Whether this pattern _causes_ better singing is not shown.
- **"Support" lacks an agreed operational definition**
  ([Voice Foundation 2026 abstract, "On Support in Phonation"](https://voicefoundation.org/view/2026-abstracts/entry/7046/))
  **[Weak]**.
- **What is safe to teach beginners.**
  - Comfortable posture.
  - A relaxed, low inhalation.
  - Steady, gentle airflow (SOVT makes this audible and felt).
  - Do not push for pitch or volume, since pressure raises pitch and loudness together.
  - **[Moderate inference]**

### 4.4 Registration: chest, head, mix (M0-M3)

- **Laryngeal mechanisms.** M0 (fry), M1 (modal or chest, including male head voice), M2
  (falsetto or female head) and M3 (whistle) are physiologically distinct vibratory mechanisms
  ([Henrich 2006](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00344177);
  [Roubeau, Henrich & Castellengo 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvoice.2007.10.014)).
- **"Mix" is not a third mechanism.** It is produced in M1 or M2 with adjustments that imitate the
  other mechanism's timbre, roughly between 200 and 400 Hz
  ([Lamesch et al. 2007](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Membres/Castellengo/publications/2007f-Voix%20mixte%20Pedagogy-CIM07.pdf)).
- **Implication.** Present register "breaks" as a normal mechanism switch, not a defect. Teach
  sirens across the switch gently on SOVT. In pitch-matching tasks, let learners choose the
  mechanism, since a light head voice or falsetto is a valid way to reach a target.

### 4.5 Resonance and vowels

- Classically trained male voices cluster formants into a "singer's formant" around 3 kHz,
  attributed to larynx-tube and pharynx configuration
  ([Sundberg 1974, summarised by HyperPhysics](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Music/singfor.html))
  **[secondary]**.
- Sopranos tune the first vocal-tract resonance to the pitch at high notes, which changes the
  vowel ([Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe 2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/427116a)).
- **Implication.** Vowel modification on high notes is physics, not cheating. For beginners,
  start pitch work on "ooh" or "ng" (closed, SOVT-like) before open vowels. Merc's "ooh" voice
  set already does this.

### 4.6 "Tone deafness", amusia and poor-pitch singing

- **Prevalence.**
  - Congenital amusia is about 1.5% in a sample of about 20,000 not reliant on self-referral
    ([Peretz & Vuvan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejhg.2017.15), figures via
    [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/070961.full.pdf)).
  - The older 4% figure traces to a single 1980 estimate
    ([Peretz et al. 2008, Western Sydney record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/on-line-identification-of-congenital-amusia/))
    **[secondary]**.
- **Self-labelling.**
  - Nearly 17% of over 2,000 students self-reported tone deafness. Self-report explained only 7%
    of variance in measured music perception, and many self-labelled people had no perceptual
    deficit ([Cuddy et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1196/annals.1360.026)).
  - The general public uses "tone deaf" to mean "poor singing". Self-defined tone-deaf people
    scored lower but lacked amusics' dense deficits, and both groups sang better _with
    accompaniment_ ([Wise & Sloboda 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/102986490801200102)).
- **Most people can sing in tune, especially slowly.** Most occasional singers sang as accurately
  as professionals when slowed down. Only 2 of 15 kept high error rates, without impaired
  perception ([Dalla Bella, Giguère & Peretz 2007](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2427111)).
- **Mechanism: mapping, not hearing.**
  - 10-15% were poor-pitch singers. Their errors were consistent transpositions with compressed
    intervals, and their pitch discrimination was normal. The authors attribute this to
    "mismapping of pitch onto action", and accompaniment sometimes hindered them
    ([Pfordresher & Brown 2007](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.25.2.95)).
  - Nonmusicians matched pitch better with a slider than with their voice. They matched their
    own voice better than a synthetic timbre (timbral translation), and did _not_ improve over
    repeated attempts or with a visual pitch representation
    ([Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064)).
  - Everyone imitates recordings of themselves better, and poor-pitch singers gain most
    ([Pfordresher & Mantell 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2013.12.005)).
- **Trainable and stable across adulthood.** In a large online sample (ages 6-99), accuracy
  improves to young adulthood and does not decline with age. Training helps, there is no gender
  difference, and the data are consistent with singing as a learned motor skill
  ([Pfordresher 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.14815)).
- **Implication.**
  - Never use "tone deaf" as a label. The lesson-01 brief already forbids it.
  - Frame it as: "Your ears are probably fine. Your voice is learning where the notes are."
  - Use slow tempos, single sustained notes before melodies, voice-like reference timbres and
    replay of the learner's _own_ best attempt as a target.

### 4.7 Octave errors and the cross-gender reference problem

- **Timbre and range matching is the research standard.** The SSAP chooses a _female_ model voice
  for all participants except males aged 14 and over (male model). It finds each participant's
  comfortable range automatically and sets keys to fit. It also includes an octave-correction step
  because pitch trackers often jump octaves within a note
  ([Pfordresher & Demorest 2020](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/PfordresherDemorest_2020_AIRS_chapter.pdf)).
  It also tests imitation of piano timbre separately, because voices are imitated better than
  instruments, especially by less accurate singers (same source).
- **Octave equivalence is learned.** Sung octave equivalence was undetectable in Tsimane' adults
  and varied with Western musical expertise
  ([Jacoby et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.08.020)). A beginner may genuinely not
  recognise that their response an octave below Merc's is "the same note".
- **Direct studies** of adult men matching female models are scarce. One study of undergraduate
  non-music majors found no accuracy difference between male-voice and sine-wave stimuli, or
  between men and women ([Price 2000, "Interval matching by undergraduate nonmusic majors"](https://digitalcommons.kennesaw.edu/facpubs/3415))
  **[Weak, secondary]**.
- **Implication for app and lesson.**
  1. Offer blue Merc's reference in the learner's register. The app's A3 and A4 "ooh" set covers
     typical lower and higher voices.
  2. Score by pitch class (octave-tolerant), and _show_ the learner that they matched an octave
     away, celebrating it as a match.
  3. Teach octave equivalence explicitly in lesson 1. The current storyboard does this.
  4. Guard against tracker octave jumps (median filtering, smoothness tolerance) before telling
     anyone they are off.

**Motor-learning overlay.**

  drops when it is removed ([Salmoni, Schmidt & Walter 1984](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.95.3.355)).

  ([Winstein & Schmidt 1990](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.16.4.677)), though replications
  are mixed ([review summary, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00190/pdf))
  **[secondary on replication]**.
- An external focus ("land on the line") beats an internal focus ("tighten your folds")
  ([Wulf 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2012.723728)).

**Design consequences for MercuryPitch.**

1. Always show the _target_ band and the _error_ (direction and cents), not just a raw trace.
2. Use a "sing, then see" mode where the trace appears after the note. This builds the learner's
   internal error-labelling, as in Welch's schema.
3. Fade feedback across sessions.
4. Vary targets: different pitches and ranges, not drilling one note.
5. Keep the display simple to limit decoding load.

### 4.9 Vocal health and safe practice

- **Fatigue and recovery.** After a 2-hour oral-reading loading task, voices recovered 90% within 4-6 hours
  and fully at 12-18 hours ([Hunter & Titze 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/000348940911800608)).
  Implication: short sessions, with rest between heavier days.
- **Hydration.** Dehydration (systemic and surface) is detrimental. Evidence that _extra_
  hydration improves voice is less robust
  ([Sivasankar & Leydon 2010, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2925668/)). A
  meta-analysis found a non-significant trend toward lower phonation threshold pressure with
  hydration ([Leydon et al., University of Memphis record](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/15508))
  **[secondary]**.
- **General voice care** ([NIDCD 2025](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taking-care-your-voice)):
  - Drink water.
  - Take "vocal naps", and do not speak or sing when hoarse or tired.
  - Avoid extremes such as screaming _or_ whispering.
  - Use humidity of about 30%.
  - Manage reflux.
- **Warm-up.** Systematic reviews report positive acoustic, perceptual, self-rated and
  aerodynamic changes from warm-up and cool-down. Typical warm-ups lasted 15-30 minutes, using
  voiced fricatives and lip or tongue trills on ascending scales, with cool-downs of 5-15 minutes
  on descending nasals and trills. There is no agreement on ideal duration or exercises
  ([Ribeiro et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1590/1982-0216201618617215)). The studies are small
  and often unblinded. **[Weak-Moderate]**
- **Implication.**
  - Start each singing lesson with 2-4 minutes of SOVT (lip trill or "ng" sirens).
  - Use a comfortable middle range and moderate volume.
  - Put "stop if it hurts or you are hoarse" on screen.
  - Merc models gentle singing, never belting, in early lessons.

### 4.10 Recommended beginner practice session length

No controlled trial has fixed the optimal session length for adult beginner singers. An
evidence-informed recommendation combines:

- Attention and engagement drop after about 6 minutes of video
  ([Guo et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239)).
- Offline consolidation favours sessions spread across days
  ([Simmons 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429411424798);
  [Simmons & Duke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/002242940605400308)).
- Quality of trials matters more than minutes
  ([Duke et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429408328851)).
- Vocal fatigue recovery takes hours ([Hunter & Titze 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/000348940911800608)).
- A singer study used four 5-minute SOVT sessions a day without harm
  ([Dargin 2016](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/entities/publication/bf2ca266-e148-43eb-86f6-910235efb300)).
- Practitioner guidance is 15-20 minutes for beginners, building gradually and stopping at
  fatigue ([30 Day Singer tutorial](https://new.30daysinger.com/tutorial/daily-routine-practice-plan-for-beginning-singer/1))
  **[Practitioner]**.

**Recommendation.** 10-15 minutes a day, five or more days a week:

- About 3 minutes SOVT warm-up.
- About 7-10 minutes focused pitch work, with many short, varied, mostly successful trials.
- About 2 minutes of easy "sing something you like" and cool-down.

Optional extra micro-sessions of 3-5 minutes. Stop at hoarseness or discomfort. **[Inference
from the sources above]**

## 5. Motivation for adult music learners

- **Self-determination theory.** Competence, autonomy and relatedness are basic needs. Satisfying
  them supports intrinsic motivation and well-being
  ([Ryan & Deci 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68)). In music education, SDT offers
  a unifying framework, and motivation is internalised to the extent these needs are met
  ([Evans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1029864914568044)). Decisions to stop playing were tied to
  thwarted competence, relatedness and autonomy
  ([Evans, McPherson & Davidson 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735612441736)). **[Moderate-Strong]**
- **Rewards and feedback.** Expected tangible rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation
  (d = -0.28 to -0.40 depending on contingency). _Positive feedback_ enhanced it
  (d = 0.33 behaviour, d = 0.31 interest) ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)).
  **[Strong]**
- **Gamification.**
  - Small positive effects on cognitive (g = 0.49), motivational (g = 0.36) and behavioural
    (g = 0.25) outcomes. Only the cognitive effect held up in rigorous studies. Game fiction and
    collaboration plus competition helped ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)).
  - A semester-long gamified course (badges, leaderboards) lowered intrinsic motivation and exam
    scores relative to a control course
    ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019), via
    [author profile summary](https://osu.academia.edu/MichaelHanus)) **[secondary]**.
  - **[Moderate]**
- **Streaks: pros and cons.**
  - _Pro:_ Highlighting an intact streak increased subsequent engagement compared with
    highlighting a broken one. People treat maintaining a logged streak as a goal in itself.
  - _Con:_ A broken streak _reduces_ engagement, more so when people blame themselves. The drop is
    smaller when the streak can be "repaired". Authors' commentary: messages emphasising the
    break do not work, and people drop off
    ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029);
    [CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)).
  - _Habit science:_ habit automaticity took a median of about 66 days (range 18-254), and
    "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit
    formation process" ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
  - _Design:_ weekly targets ("5 of 7 days"), streak freezes or repair, and framing that
    celebrates _returning_. Never shame a break. Make the streak counter secondary to visible
    competence growth, such as a range map or accuracy trend. **[Moderate]**
- **Agents and self-efficacy.** Pedagogical agents raised self-efficacy and interest
  ([Gladstone et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2)). Merc's job includes
  saying "you can do this" credibly: after evidence of progress, never as empty praise.

### Rule 1. Hook with a question the viewer cares about within 30 seconds

- **Why:** Motivation must be clear early. Curiosity is the hook.
- **Source:** [Sanderson 2021, SoME criteria](https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1);
  [Kosner 2021](https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/grant-sanderson-channels-his-passion-for-math-into-marvelously-i).

  sound different?"), with a play button for the two sounds.
- **Video:** First line by orange Merc, about 0-8 seconds, over the phenomenon itself, not a logo.

### Rule 2. Make the viewer predict before every reveal

- **Why:** Prequestions and predictions improve learning even when wrong. Surprise after a
  committed prediction drives belief revision.
- **Source:** [Richland et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496);
  [Carpenter & Toftness 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2016.07.014);
  [Brod et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.013);
  [Theobald & Brod 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13582).
- **Web page:** A two-choice or sing-your-guess widget locks in an answer before the explanation
  unlocks. Show the class distribution of guesses afterwards.
- **Video:** "Pause and guess" card with a 2-3 second hold. Orange states its guess. Blue says
  "Let's find out". Then the demonstration.

### Rule 3. Voice the misconception, then refute it on screen

- **Why:** Exposition alone reinforces wrong ideas. Refutation and dialogue gave d of about 0.8.
- **Source:** [Muller et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x);
  [Muller, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20244);
  [Schroeder & Kucera 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z).
- **Web page:** A "Common mix-up" card per concept: the belief, why it seems right, the evidence
  against it, then the better idea.
- **Video:** Orange holds the plausible wrong belief sincerely. The demo breaks it. Blue never
  mocks it ("that is exactly what most people think").

### Rule 4. Orange Merc asks deep questions, not filler

- **Why:** Vicarious learning improves when the virtual tutee asks deep-reasoning questions.
  Comments and shallow questions do not help.
- **Source:** [Driscoll et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.2190/Q8CM-FH7L-6HJU-DT9W);
  [Craig et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci2404_4).
- **Web page:** Section headings are orange's questions ("Why does doubling sound the same?").
- **Video:** Script audit: every orange line is a why, how or what-if question, a prediction, or a
  self-explanation. Cut lines like "wow" and "cool".

### Rule 5. Sound before symbol

- **Why:** Hearing and singing before naming and notation is the shared core of
  Pestalozzi/Mason, Kodály, Orff, Gordon and Suzuki. Combined sound plus symbol beats symbol alone.
- **Source:** [MSU thesis on Mason 1834](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/39681/FULL_TEXT/view);
  [Gordon MLT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory);
  [Shehan 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/3344987).
- **Web page:** Every concept block opens with an audio player or mic task. The term appears
  after. Notation comes last and is collapsible.
- **Video:** Order within each beat: hear it, sing it (invitation), name it, then show the symbol.

### Rule 6. Concrete first, abstract last, so the viewer could have invented it

- **Why:** Starting from a specific example builds ownership. Concreteness fading transfers
  better.
- **Source:** [Kosner 2021](https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/grant-sanderson-channels-his-passion-for-math-into-marvelously-i);
  [Stanford Daily 2020](https://www.stanforddaily.com/2020/01/24/3blue1brown-creator-grant-sanderson-15-talks-engaging-with-math-using-stories-and-visuals);
  [Fyfe et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9249-3).
- **Web page:** Start with the specific case (220 Hz and 440 Hz), then the general rule (2:1),
  then the model (spiral).
- **Video:** One persistent object transforms into the next idea (line to spiral to keyboard).
  State the general rule only after the viewer has seen it happen.

### Rule 7. Pre-train the names

- **Why:** Knowing the parts' names and behaviour first improves transfer.
- **Source:** [Mayer, Mathias & Wetzell 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.8.3.147).
- **Web page:** A "Three words you will meet" strip at the top (pitch, hertz, octave), each with
  a 1-line definition and a sound.
- **Video:** In the first 30-45 seconds, name and show each key object once before the mechanism
  that connects them.

### Rule 8. One idea per segment, learner-paced

- **Why:** Segmenting helps. Engagement drops after about 6 minutes.
- **Source:** [Rey et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4);
  [Guo et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239).
- **Web page:** Step cards with "next", each 30-90 seconds of reading or doing. Progress dots.
- **Video:** YouTube chapters, one per idea, each ending on a mini-payoff. Keep episodes about
  4-10 minutes, or chapter longer ones.

### Rule 9. Narrate the visual; label, do not transcribe

- **Why:** Spoken words with graphics beat printed words (modality). Duplicating narration as
  on-screen text hurts (redundancy). The same holds in music instruction.
- **Source:** [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E);
  [Ginns 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2005.07.001);
  [Owens & Sweller 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410701369146).
- **Web page:** Keep body text short and _next to_ its visual. Embedded clips carry the
  explanation by voice.
- **Video:** On-screen text only for 2-4-word labels and the key term. Captions are a viewer
  toggle, not burned-in sentences (except for sound-off social cuts).

### Rule 10. Put words where and when the picture is

- **Why:** Spatial contiguity (median d = 1.10) and temporal contiguity (median d = 1.22). In
  music, simultaneous sound, explanation and notation beat successive presentation.
- **Source:** [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E);
  [Owens & Sweller 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410701369146).
- **Web page:** Labels sit on the diagram. Audio buttons sit on the thing they sound. No legends.
- **Video:** The tone sounds exactly while its wave moves. The word "octave" lands on the frame
  where the cycles align.

### Rule 11. Signal the one thing that matters

- **Why:** Signalling helps retention and transfer. In animation, cues that guide attention to
  locations work best.
- **Source:** [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E);
  [de Koning et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-009-9098-7).
- **Web page:** Gold (`#ffd84d`) is reserved for "the fit" or the key relation. Everything else is
  muted.
- **Video:** At any moment only one element moves or glows. Dim the rest. Merc points to it.

### Rule 12. Cut seductive details; no music under listening or explaining

- **Why:** Coherence (23/23 tests, median d = 0.86). Seductive details harm. In a pitch course,
  music competes with the content itself.
- **Source:** [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E);
  [Harp & Mayer 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414);
  [Moreno & Mayer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.92.1.117);
  [de la Mora Velasco et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356231153070).
- **Web page:** No autoplay ambience. Fun facts go in an optional "Go deeper" drawer at the end,
  never in the main path.
- **Video:** Pad only in intro, outro and transitions, ducked to silence under every example and
  dense explanation. Each "wow" tangent must be about the idea (wonder about the spiral), not
  unrelated trivia.

### Rule 13. Mascots act; they do not decorate

- **Why:** The agent's image alone does not help. Gesturing and pointing agents do, and static
  agents add nothing. Faces on content give a modest learning and motivation boost.
- **Source:** [Moreno et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1207/S1532690XCI1902_02);
  [Mayer & DaPra 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028616);
  [Wang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000221);
  [Brom et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.09.004).
- **Web page:** Merc appears beside a concept to ask, react or sing. During dense diagrams Merc
  sits small at the margin. Merc's gaze or pseudopod indicates the active element.
- **Video:** Blue gazes at and points to the content when explaining. Mascots shrink or exit for
  diagram-heavy beats and return to react. No idle loops during explanation.

### Rule 14. Speak like a person to a person

- **Why:** Conversational style improves retention (d = 0.30) and transfer (d = 0.54). Human
  voices beat machine voices, and the embodiment effect needs a human voice.
- **Source:** [Ginns et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-013-9228-0);
  [Mayer et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.419);
  [Mayer & DaPra 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028616);
  [Craig & Schroeder 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0735633118802877).
- **Web page:** "You" and "we", short sentences, British English per brand.
- **Video:** Final cast human (or the most natural voice available), warm and moderately fast.
  Blue sounds like a kind expert, orange like a smart friend.

### Rule 15. Make confusion safe and normal

- **Why:** Productive struggle feels worse but teaches more. Fluent delivery inflates feelings of
  learning.
- **Source:** [Muller, Sharma & Reimann 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20244);
  [Carpenter et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0442-z);
  [Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116);
  [Bjork & Bjork 2011](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf).
- **Web page:** Microcopy such as "If that felt tricky, good: that is the feeling of learning".
  Analytics judge lessons by retrieval scores, not "was this clear?".
- **Video:** Orange is visibly puzzled and then gets it. Blue says once per episode that being
  surprised means your brain is updating.

### Rule 16. Worked example, then fade

- **Why:** Novices learn more from examples first, then from gradually removed steps.
- **Source:** [Sweller & Cooper 1985](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3);
  [Renkl & Atkinson 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3).
- **Web page:** Blue does it (full demo). Orange does part (the learner finishes). The learner
  does it alone. "Show me again" stays available.
- **Video:** Blue sings the octave pair. Orange tries one half. The viewer is invited to try both.

### Rule 17. Guided exploration, never an empty sandbox

- **Why:** Unassisted discovery underperforms (d = -0.38). Enhanced discovery with feedback and
  scaffolds wins (d = 0.30). Try-first then instruction works.
- **Source:** [Alfieri et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021017);
  [Kirschner et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1);
  [Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105).
- **Web page:** Each explorer widget states a goal ("Find the frequency that locks with
  220 Hz"), limits the controls and gives immediate feedback. Free play unlocks afterwards.
- **Video:** Show what the explorer does and what question to bring to it, then hand off: "Try it
  on the lesson page".

### Rule 18. Retrieve at the end, retrieve again later

- **Why:** Testing beats restudy. Interpolated tests cut mind-wandering. Spacing amplifies
  retention.
- **Source:** [Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x);
  [Szpunar et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1221764110);
  [Cepeda et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x);
  [Kang 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215624708).
- **Web page:** Three free-recall questions with ear and voice items at the end. The next visit
  (day 1-2, then about day 7) opens with two questions from earlier lessons. Mistakes feed back
  into review.
- **Video:** A mid-video checkpoint question, an end-of-episode three-question recap held long
  enough to answer, and each new episode opening with one question from the last.

### Rule 19. Interleave once each category has been introduced

- **Why:** Interleaving improves discrimination of similar categories. Learners wrongly prefer
  blocking.
- **Source:** [Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x);
  [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209);
  [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251).
- **Web page:** Ear-training sets mix octave, non-octave and unison (later intervals and
  major/minor) rather than blocking.
- **Video:** The recap mixes the new concept with earlier ones ("octave or not?" alongside "higher
  or louder?").

### Rule 20. One visual grammar for sound, forever

- **Why:** "High" is ambiguous (pitch, space, loudness). Pitch-space mappings are learned and
  language-shaped. Pitch and loudness interact perceptually and in the voice.
- **Source:** [Costa-Giomi & Descombes 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345594);
  [Dolscheid et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612457374);
  [Neuhoff et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.25.4.1050);
  [Titze 1989](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.397562).
- **Web page:** Pitch is always vertical position (as in the app's pitch canvas). Loudness is
  always size or brightness or wave height. A glossary tile shows "quiet-high vs loud-low".
- **Video:** Never animate pitch change with a size change. Early in the series, show a loud low
  note beside a quiet high note.

### Rule 21. Every voice is learning; no labels, ever

- **Why:** Self-labelled "tone deaf" is about 17% of people, amusia about 1.5%. Poor-pitch singing
  is mostly mapping, not hearing. Accuracy is a learnable skill at any adult age.
- **Source:** [Cuddy et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1196/annals.1360.026);
  [Peretz & Vuvan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejhg.2017.15);
  [Pfordresher & Brown 2007](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.25.2.95);
  [Dalla Bella et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2427111);
  [Pfordresher 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.14815).
- **Web page:** Feedback describes the sound ("a little under the target, slide up"), never the
  singer. A "Can't sing?" explainer frames it as voice-ear calibration.
- **Video:** Orange voices "I can't sing" once. Blue answers with the evidence and a first
  success. Merc never says wrong, bad or failed.

### Rule 22. Match the model to the singer, and accept the octave

- **Why:** Voice-like and self timbres are imitated best. The research protocol matches gender
  and range. Octave equivalence is learned, so it needs teaching. Slow tempo raises accuracy.
- **Source:** [Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064);
  [Pfordresher & Mantell 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2013.12.005);
  [Pfordresher & Demorest 2020](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/PfordresherDemorest_2020_AIRS_chapter.pdf);
  [Jacoby et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.08.020);
  [Dalla Bella et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2427111).
- **Web page:** A range finder before the first task. Reference tones in a voice timbre in the
  learner's register. Octave-tolerant matching with an explicit "you matched an octave lower:
  same note" message. "Replay your best try" as a target.
- **Video:** Blue sings at A3, orange at A4 (or vice versa), showing both are "A". Say plainly
  that matching in your own octave counts.

### Rule 23. Feedback with knowledge of results, then fade it

- **Why:** A raw pitch trace alone did not help. Feedback against a target with direction and size
  did help and was retained. Constant feedback can breed dependence.
- **Source:** [Welch 1985](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735685131001);
  [Hutchins & Peretz 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025064);
  [Berglin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211026730);
  [Blanco et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693);
  [Salmoni et al. 1984](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.95.3.355).
- **Web page:** A target band plus a cents arrow. A "sing first, then see" mode. Feedback
  frequency drops as accuracy stabilises. Targets vary.
- **Video:** Show the app's line hitting the band as the _picture_ of success. Model "sing,
  listen, adjust" rather than staring at the screen.

### Rule 24. Cue the effect, not the anatomy

- **Why:** An external focus improves motor performance and learning, including keyboard accuracy
  and singers' tone ring.
- **Source:** [Wulf 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2012.723728);
  [Duke et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429410396093);
  [Atkins 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429416673842).
- **Web page:** Instructions such as "land your line on the gold band", "slide like a siren" and
  "send the sound to the far wall". Avoid "raise your soft palate" for beginners.
- **Video:** Merc's metaphors are about the sound's path and target, not body parts.

### Rule 25. Short, gentle, frequent practice, with streaks that forgive

- **Why:** Offline consolidation, the quality of practice, vocal fatigue recovery, SDT needs and
  streak research all point the same way.
- **Source:** [Simmons 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429411424798);
  [Duke et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429408328851);
  [Hunter & Titze 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/000348940911800608);
  [NIDCD 2025](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taking-care-your-voice);
  [Evans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1029864914568044);
  [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029);
  [Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674).
- **Web page:**
  - A 10-15 minute daily plan: 3 minutes SOVT, 7-10 minutes pitch work, 2 minutes free singing.
  - "Stop if hoarse" notice.
  - Weekly goals with streak repair.
  - A progress view of competence (range, accuracy trend), not points.
  - Optional choice of exercises (autonomy).
- **Video:** End card: "10 minutes, gently, most days. Missed a day? Just come back." Merc warms
  up with a lip trill on screen.

## 7. Notes specific to Lesson 1 ("What is a note?")

Based on `output/motion/lesson-01/BRIEF.md` and `FACTS.md` (read only, not modified).

1. **FACTS.md item 4 (high vs loud).** Strengthen the citation with
   [Costa-Giomi & Descombes 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345594), which explicitly addresses
   high/low labels carrying pitch, space and intensity meanings.
   [Hair 1977](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.2307/3345304) shows children
   discriminate pitch direction better than they can name it, not loudness confusion. Keeping it
   as orange's question, not a statistic, is the right call.
2. **FACTS.md item 9 (octave equivalence).** "Most people hear one note, just brighter" is fair for
   a Western-enculturated audience, but octave equivalence is not universal
   ([Jacoby et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.08.020)). Avoid "everyone" or
   "always". Consider a light line: "with a little listening, your ear starts to hear them as the
   same note". That turns a fact into a skill, which also suits Rule 21.
3. **Spiral (FACTS 12).** [Shepard 1982](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.89.4.305) is the right
   citation. The line-to-spiral transformation is a textbook "one object transforms into the next
   idea" move (Rule 6).
4. **Audio bed.** The brief ducks the pad to silence under listening examples, consistent with
   Rule 12. Extend the ducking to dense explanation beats too.
5. **Scratch TTS voices.** Modern TTS can approach human narration for learning
   ([Craig & Schroeder 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0735633118802877)). However, the voice and
   embodiment principles favour human voices
   ([Mayer et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.419);
   [Mayer & DaPra 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028616)), and the mascots sing. The planned
   re-voicing with a human cast is evidence-aligned.
6. **Merc's A3 and A4 sung notes.** These map well onto Rule 22: the two mascots can sing the
   same note an octave apart, the exact situation viewers will meet in the app.
7. **CTA "try singing one note in Voice Mirror".** This is a generative, retrieval-like act right
   after learning. Add the three recap questions _before_ the CTA with a hold, then the CTA (Rule
   18). Add a one-line safety cue: "gently, in a comfortable range" (FACTS 20).
8. **Predict beat.** Make sure there is at least one explicit viewer prediction before the 2:1
   reveal. For example: "Merc will play 220 and then 440. Same note or different?" (Rule 2).

## 8. Evidence gaps and open questions

- **Agents for adults.** Agent benefits are smaller for post-secondary learners
  ([Schroeder et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.49.1.a)). No study has tested _singing_
  mascots that model the target behaviour. Our format extends the evidence rather than resting
  directly on it. Worth A/B testing on the web page (Merc present vs absent in one section),
  measuring retrieval scores.
- **Kodály, Orff, Dalcroze, Gordon and Suzuki** have rich practice traditions but few randomised
  trials. Use their shared core (sound before symbol, movement, singing first) and test specifics.
- **Fixed vs movable do.** Research is confounded ([Demorest & May 1995](https://doi.org/10.2307/3345676)).
  Movable or scale-degree is recommended on reasoning about relative pitch, not on decisive
  trials.
- **Visual feedback dose and fading schedule for adults.** Promising
  ([Berglin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211026730)) but short-term. MercuryPitch
  analytics could generate real evidence on retention without the display.
- **Practice length for adult beginner singers** has no direct trial. The recommendation is an
  inference.
- **Background music** has conflicting meta-analytic signals
  ([Moreno & Mayer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.92.1.117) vs
  [de la Mora Velasco et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356231153070)). In a pitch course,
  the conservative choice is justified by content-channel competition.
- **Unverified primary details.** Items marked **[secondary]** (for example the JMM 2023 checklist
  wording, Wilson et al. 2008 specifics, Hanus & Fox exact statistics, the Sundberg 1974 mechanism)
  should be checked against originals before being quoted on screen.
