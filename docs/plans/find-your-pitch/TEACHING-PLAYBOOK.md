# Find Your Pitch: teaching playbook

Twenty-five rules every lesson follows, in the video and on the web page. Each rule carries the
evidence it rests on. The research behind them (learning science, misconception teaching, music
and singing pedagogy, motivation) was gathered for this plan in October 2026; journal claims were
checked against the paper's abstract or full text.

## The five that matter most

1. **Voice the misconception, then refute it.** Muller (2008): a clear physics video left scores
   almost unchanged and made viewers more confident in their wrong ideas; a version that stated the
   misconception and refuted it, as narration or as a tutor-student dialogue, raised scores with
   effect sizes of d = 0.79 and 0.83. Orange Merc holds the plausible wrong belief; blue Merc and
   the demonstration break it.
2. **Orange Merc asks deep questions.** Overhearing a dialogue beats a monologue only when the
   learner character asks why, how or what-if questions; shallow comments add nothing (Driscoll et
   al. 2003; Craig et al. 2006). Every orange line is a question, a sincere guess or a
   re-explanation in its own words.
3. **Predict before every reveal.** Committing to a guess improves learning even when the guess is
   wrong (Richland et al. 2009), in video too (Carpenter and Toftness 2017).
4. **Sound before symbol, concrete before abstract.** Hear it, sing it, name it, then (Season 2)
   read it. Start from 220 Hz and 440 Hz, then the 2:1 rule, then the spiral.
5. **Retrieve at the end, and again later.** Testing beats rereading (Roediger and Karpicke 2006);
   short tests inside a video reduce mind-wandering (Szpunar et al. 2013); spacing multiplies the
   effect (Cepeda et al. 2008).

## House rules for claims and safety

These come from the app's guided-voice claims rules (`src/lib/guided-voice/claims.ts`,
`docs/specs/guided-voice-check.ears.md` §§ 3 and 8) and `PRODUCT.md`, and bind every lesson:

- Never rate breath support. Never frame registers or breaks as a problem. No health or injury
  claims.
- Never score lip trill, hum or straw exercises; keep them optional, short, with a stop rule.
- Never reward the longest hold or the widest range; use comfortable fixed lengths (5, 10, 15,
  30 s).
- Never claim to check pronunciation or vowels; never give one overall "voice score".
- Discomfort overrides any result: "Stop immediately if anything feels uncomfortable."
- Distinguish absolute pitch, relative pitch and pitch accuracy (the app's own copy currently uses
  "perfect pitch" for "inside the Perfect band"; lessons must not).
- Every number, name or claim in a lesson has a line in that lesson's `FACTS.md`.

## How we judge a lesson

Not by "that was so clear!": fluency inflates perceived learning (Carpenter et al. 2013). Judge by
retrieval: the share of learners who answer the lesson's three questions correctly on the page,
and again at the next visit. YouTube retention and the page's step completion are engagement
signals, not learning signals.
