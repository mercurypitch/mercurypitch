# Find Your Pitch: teaching playbook

Twenty-five rules every lesson follows, in the video and on the web page. Each rule carries the
evidence it rests on. The research behind them (learning science, misconception teaching, music
and singing pedagogy, motivation) was gathered for this plan in October 2026 and is in
[research/pedagogy-research.md](research/pedagogy-research.md); journal claims were checked against
the paper's abstract or full text.

## The five that matter most

1. **Voice the misconception, then refute it.** Muller et al. (2008): a clear physics video
   (exposition) left scores almost unchanged and made viewers more confident in their wrong ideas;
   stating the misconception and refuting it beat exposition whether it was narrated (d = 0.79) or
   played as a tutor-student dialogue (d = 0.83). The gain is the refutation, not the second
   character: Tavi is how we voice the plausible wrong belief, and her deep questions
   (Craig et al. 2006; rule 4) are what make a dialogue pay. Merc and the demonstration break
   the belief, or show why it is only half right.
2. **Tavi asks deep questions.** Overhearing a dialogue beats a monologue only when the
   learner character asks why, how or what-if questions; shallow comments add nothing (Driscoll et
   al. 2003; Craig et al. 2006). Every Tavi line is a question, a sincere guess or a
   re-explanation in its own words.
3. **Predict before every reveal.** Committing to a guess improves learning even when the guess is
   wrong (Richland et al. 2009), in video too (Carpenter and Toftness 2017).
4. **Sound before symbol, concrete before abstract.** Hear it, sing it, name it, then (Season 2)
   read it. Start from 220 Hz and 440 Hz, then the 2:1 rule, then the spiral.
5. **Retrieve at the end, and again later.** Testing beats rereading (Roediger and Karpicke 2006);
   short tests inside a video reduce mind-wandering (Szpunar et al. 2013); spacing multiplies the
   effect (Cepeda et al. 2008).

## House rules for claims and safety

These come from the app's guided-voice claims rules (`src/lib/guided-voice/claims.ts`,
`docs/specs/guided-voice-check.ears.md` §§ 3 and 8) and `PRODUCT.md`, and bind every lesson:

- Never rate breath support. Never frame registers or breaks as a problem. No health or injury
  claims (the referral line below is standard guidance, not a health claim).
- Never score lip trill, hum or straw exercises; keep them optional, short, with a stop rule.
  (Lesson 1's hum-along is unscored; its scored task is singing back on "ooh" in Voice Mirror.)
- Never reward the longest hold or the widest range. Holds are 5, 10 or 15 s; 30 s (the app's
  longest timer preset) is only ever an optional advanced length with "breathe whenever you need",
  never a checkpoint.
- Never claim to check pronunciation or vowels; never give one overall "voice score".
- Before any segment beyond a comfortable hum (holds, sirens, range), ask: "Sore, hoarse or unwell
  today? Just listen along." (REQ-GVC-020).
- Discomfort overrides any result: "Stop immediately if anything feels uncomfortable." With it, a
  neutral referral line: "If hoarseness or discomfort lasts more than a couple of weeks, see a
  doctor or a voice specialist" (REQ-GVC-021; wording for the vocal coach to approve).
- A vocal coach signs off every voice lesson at the day-1 script gate and the day-6 publish gate
  (MASTER-PLAN § 7); MASTER-PLAN § 12 item 8 decides who the coach is, not whether there is a
  review.
- Distinguish absolute pitch, relative pitch and pitch accuracy (the app's own copy currently uses
  "perfect pitch" for "inside the Perfect band"; lessons must not).
- Every number, name or claim in a lesson has a line in that lesson's `FACTS.md`.

## How we judge a lesson

Not by "that was so clear!": fluency inflates perceived learning (Carpenter et al. 2013). Judge by
retrieval: the share of learners who answer the page's three or four quick-check questions
correctly on the first try (Lesson 1 has four), and again at the next visit, when two are asked
afresh. Each question has three or four options, so scores are reported guess-corrected
(MASTER-PLAN § 9). The Phase 1 Lesson telemetry track collects them; until it exists, answers stay
in the viewer's browser. YouTube retention and the page's step completion are engagement signals,
not learning signals.
