from `script.json`. Estimated running time 6:54 (414.2 s). The times are an estimate, not a cue
sheet: every line is timed at 2.6 words a second and every other event at its `dur`, placed exactly
as `tools/cues.py` places them (`gap` after the previous event, or `offset` from the event named in

captions and the picture write them as on screen ("A3", "220 Hz", "C♯" in the picture, "C sharp"
in captions). Every claim is in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md). Line ids keep their round 1 names, so the
critics' reports stay traceable: b25 and b44 are gone, o5a and b52a are new, and b43 now comes
before b42a and b42b.

## s10-tone: Tones, and where the five come from (2:48.1)

- `2:48.1` _Sound: tone-split (3.7 s)._
- `2:52.2` **BLUE MERC:** Two semitones make a bigger step: a tone. Some call it a whole step.
- `2:58.2` **ORANGE MERC:** So that's where the other five come from: the gaps a tone wide.
- `3:03.5` **BLUE MERC:** Yes. Five pairs of neighbouring letters are a tone apart, and each gap holds one note.
- `3:10.0` **BLUE MERC:** B to C and E to F are only a semitone, so there's no gap to fill.

## s11-names: Sharps and flats: two names (3:17.1)

- `3:19.5` **BLUE MERC:** Now the five need names. They borrow them from their neighbours.
- `3:24.1` **BLUE MERC:** Sharp means one semitone up. Flat means one semitone down.
- `3:28.3` _Sound: two-ways (3.4 s)._
- `3:32.1` **BLUE MERC:** Up from C, it's C sharp. Down from D, it's D flat. The same key.
- `3:38.2` **BLUE MERC:** One key, two names.

## s12-direction: A direction, not a colour (3:40.3)

- `3:40.3` **ORANGE MERC:** So black keys are the sharps and flats, and white keys are the plain ones?
- `3:46.5` **BLUE MERC:** Not quite. Try it: one semitone up from E. A black key, or a white one?
- `3:53.0` _Pause: predict-e (1.8 s)._
- `3:55.1` _Sound: e-f (1.5 s)._
- `3:56.9` **BLUE MERC:** White. It lands on F. So E sharp is just another name for F. Sharp is a direction, not a colour.
- `4:05.4` **ORANGE MERC:** Then how do musicians choose?
- `4:07.7` **BLUE MERC:** Both are right. It depends on the music around it.
- `4:11.9` **BLUE MERC:** When MercuryPitch draws your voice, it uses the sharp names, like C sharp.

## s13-cents: A hundred cents (4:17.5)

- `4:19.8` **ORANGE MERC:** And how big is a semitone, exactly?
- `4:22.9` **BLUE MERC:** Let's measure it. Listen to a slow glide from A up to A sharp.
- `4:28.6` _Sound: cents-glide (3.6 s)._
- `4:32.5` **BLUE MERC:** That's a hundred cents. In the tuning MercuryPitch uses, every semitone is a hundred cents wide.
- `4:39.2` **BLUE MERC:** Twelve semitones make an octave, so an octave is twelve hundred cents.
- `4:44.3` **BLUE MERC:** MercuryPitch tells you how far you are from the nearest note, in cents.
- `4:49.7` **BLUE MERC:** Fifty cents is halfway. Past that, you're nearer the next note.
