# Script: Find Your Pitch, Lesson 2, "Twelve notes"

Draft v3, after the round 2 script and storyboard critics (DECISIONS, "Round 2 response"), written
from `script.json`. Estimated running time 6:50 (410.2 s). The times are an estimate, not a cue
sheet: every line is timed at 2.6 words a second and every other event at its `dur`, placed exactly
as `tools/cues.py` places them (`gap` after the previous event, or `offset` from the event named in
`with`). The first voice pass replaces them with measured times; Lesson 1's scratch voices ran at
2.73 words a second, so the measured film may come in nearer 6:36. Scene headings follow the scene
table proposed in STORYBOARD.md (`tools/gen-root.py` cuts each act mark 0.15 s early).

Words in the script are spelled for the voice ("A three", "two hundred and twenty", "C sharp");
captions and the picture write them as on screen ("A3", "220 Hz", "C♯" in the picture, "C sharp"
in captions). Every claim is in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md). Line ids keep the names the critics' reports
use: b25 and b44 are gone (round 1), o5a and b52a are new, and b43 comes before b42a and b42b.

## s10-tone: Tones, and where the five come from (2:48.1)

- `2:48.1` _Sound: tone-split (3.7 s)._
- `2:52.2` **BLUE MERC:** Two semitones make a bigger step: a tone. Some call it a whole step.
- `2:58.2` **ORANGE MERC:** So that's where the other five come from: the gaps a tone wide.
- `3:03.5` **BLUE MERC:** Yes. Five pairs of neighbouring letters are a tone apart, and each gap holds one note.
- `3:10.0` **BLUE MERC:** B to C and E to F have no gap to fill.

## s11-names: Sharps and flats: two names (3:15.2)

- `3:17.5` **BLUE MERC:** Now the five need names. They borrow them from their neighbours.
- `3:22.2` **BLUE MERC:** Sharp means one semitone up. Flat means one semitone down.
- `3:26.4` _Sound: two-ways (3.4 s)._
- `3:30.2` **BLUE MERC:** Up from C, it's C sharp. Down from D, it's D flat. The same key.
- `3:36.3` **BLUE MERC:** One key, two names.

## s12-direction: A direction, not a colour (3:38.4)

- `3:38.4` **ORANGE MERC:** So black keys are the sharps and flats, and white keys are the plain ones?
- `3:44.6` **BLUE MERC:** Not quite. Try it: one semitone up from E. A black key, or a white one?
- `3:51.0` _Pause: predict-e (2.0 s)._
- `3:53.3` _Sound: e-f (1.5 s)._
- `3:55.1` **BLUE MERC:** White: F. So E sharp is another name for F. Sharp is a direction, not a colour.
- `4:02.2` **ORANGE MERC:** Then how do musicians choose a name?
- `4:05.2` **BLUE MERC:** It depends on the music around it. Both are right.
- `4:09.4` **BLUE MERC:** When MercuryPitch draws your voice, it uses the sharp names, like C sharp.

## s13-cents: A hundred cents (4:15.0)

- `4:26.1` _Sound: cents-glide (3.6 s)._

- `4:36.7` **BLUE MERC:** Twelve semitones make an octave, so an octave is twelve hundred cents.
- `4:41.8` **BLUE MERC:** MercuryPitch tells you how far you are from the nearest note, in cents.
- `4:47.2` **BLUE MERC:** Fifty cents is halfway. Past that, you're nearer the next note.
