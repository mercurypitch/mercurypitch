## Tuning and arithmetic

1. Twelve-tone equal temperament (12-TET) divides the octave into twelve equal steps, the semitones | Wolfe, "Note names, MIDI numbers and frequencies", UNSW (newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/notes.html): "In equal temperament, an octave comprises 12 equal semitones" | fetched; quoted
2. Each equal-tempered semitone multiplies the frequency by 2^(1/12) = 1.059463..., about 1.0595 | Wolfe (as 1): "Each semitone therefore has a ratio of 2^1/12 (approximately 1.059)" | fetched; arithmetic to four decimals
3. Twelve such steps multiply to exactly 2, the octave's 2:1 ratio: (2^(1/12))^12 = 2^(12/12) = 2 | arithmetic; the octave is 2:1 (ANSI/ASA S1.1, Lesson 1 FACTS 7) | arithmetic
4. So twelve semitones up from A3 (220.00 Hz) is A4 (440.00 Hz): "two hundred and twenty hertz has doubled to four hundred and forty" (b10) | 3 and the table in 6 | arithmetic
5. The hertz size of a semitone grows with pitch while its ratio stays the same: A3 to A#3 is 13.08 Hz, G#4 to A4 is 24.70 Hz, A4 to A#4 is 26.16 Hz; a semitone raises the frequency by about 5.95 % | 2 and 6 | arithmetic; on screen in S6a as "+13 Hz" (A3 to A♯3) and "+25 Hz" (G♯4 to A4), the climb's first and last steps, rounded to whole hertz; the page's Go deeper prints 13.08 and 26.16

## The keyboard, semitones and tones

10. Neighbouring keys on a keyboard, white or black, are one semitone (half step) apart, and the half step is the smallest interval in Western notation | Open Music Theory, 2nd ed. (Gotham et al.), 1.5 "Half Steps, Whole Steps, and Accidentals": "A half step is considered to be the smallest interval, or distance between two notes, in Western musical notation" | fetched (LibreTexts edition); said only scoped: "the smallest step a piano has" (b19), "the smallest step between them" (b5, between the twelve), and the S3 kicker "THE SMALLEST STEP ON A PIANO"; b13 says only "That step has a name. A semitone.", and the act 1 card and its chapter read "The very next note"
11. A tone (whole step) is two semitones | OMT 2e 1.5: "A whole step is the equivalent of two half steps" | fetched
12. "Semitone" and "tone" are also called "half step" and "whole step" | OMT 2e 1.5 uses half and whole steps; the ABRSM Grade 1 syllabus says "tones and semitones" (research/curriculum-benchmark.md § 1.1); the app's own Learn chapter says "100 cents = 1 semitone (half step)" (`src/types/walkthrough.ts:834`) and Ear Lab Contour names its tiers "Whole steps" and "Half steps" (`src/lib/ear/banks.ts:176-188`) | fetched and code; said as "Some people call it a half step" (b14), "Some call it a whole step" (b24)
13. Every octave of a keyboard has seven white keys (the letters A to G) and five black keys, in groups of two and three: twelve keys | OMT 2e 1.4 "The Keyboard and the Grand Staff": "The black keys are grouped into sets of either three or two", letters "repeating in a loop", "repeating the pattern for each octave"; the count of keys per octave | fetched; counted (OMT does not state the count)
14. E and F, and B and C, have no black key between them, because they are already a half step apart | OMT 2e 1.5: "Two pairs of white keys—E/F and B/C—do not have black keys in between them ... This is because E–F and B–C are both half steps" | fetched; said as "B and C are already neighbours, a semitone apart, so no key fits between them" (b21)
15. Of the seven neighbouring letter pairs (A–B, B–C, C–D, D–E, E–F, F–G, G–A), five are a tone apart and two (B–C, E–F) a semitone; the five tone-wide gaps each hold one note, so 7 + 5 = 12, and one note in every gap (Tavi's guess, o1) would make 7 + 7 = 14 | follows from 11, 13 and 14 | counted; o5a, b26 and b27 say it, and the page's cold-open reveal uses the 14
16. The twelve-step climb A3 to A4 passes A3, A#3, B3, C4, C#4, D4, D#4, E4, F4, F#4, G4, G#4, A4: thirteen notes, twelve steps | 6 | counted; hence "Count the steps with me" (b8), not the notes

## Sharps, flats and two names

17. A sharp (♯) raises a note by a semitone; a flat (♭) lowers it by a semitone | OMT 2e 1.5: "A sharp (♯) ... raises a note by a half step"; "A flat (♭) ... lowers a note by a half step" | fetched
18. The black key between C and D is C♯ (a semitone above C) and D♭ (a semitone below D): spelled differently, the same sound, "enharmonic" | OMT 2e 1.5: "Notes have enharmonic equivalence when they are spelled differently but sound the same"; "C♯ and D♭ are enharmonically equivalent" | fetched; the film says "One key, two names" (b31, b51) and the word "enharmonic" is on the page only (LESSON step 5)
19. The five black keys of an octave and both their names: C♯/D♭, D♯/E♭, F♯/G♭, G♯/A♭, A♯/B♭ | 17 and 18 | derived
20. A sharp or flat can land on a white key: one semitone up from E is F, so E♯ is F; likewise F♭ is E, B♯ is C and C♭ is B | OMT 2e 1.5: "F is also known as E♯, and E is also known as F♭"; "C is also known as B♯, and B is also known as C♭" | fetched; the film uses E♯ only (b32's predict, b33); the web toggle shows the other three as extras; the page's step 5 predict asks the E♯ case before any arrow shows it, its Try and Notice use F♭ ("Then tap F and press Flat"; "F♭ is another name for E"), and on phones step 5's close-up of B3 to F4 holds all four rare names
21. Both names are correct; which one is written depends on the music around it (the key) | OMT 2e 1.12: "the B major and C♭ major scales have different key signatures ... but they sound the same because the notes B and C♭ are enharmonically equivalent"; research/pedagogy-research.md § 3.10 ("Spelling encodes function in a key", OMT v2, Lavengood et al. 2023) | fetched (1.12); the OMT v2 page itself returned 403, so its wording is the research report's; said only as "It depends on the music around it. ## Cents

## Unsupported, so left out of the script and the page

- "The semitone is the smallest step in music": false in general (quarter tones exist, and Ear
  Lab's own Contour plays 50- and 25-cent gaps it names "Quarter tones" and "Hairline gaps",
  `src/lib/ear/banks.ts:189-202`). The script says "the smallest step a piano has" and "the
  smallest step between them" instead (10).
- "Every semitone is a hundred cents" as a law of music, and "the tuning most music uses today":
  no source read for this lesson says either (Wolfe's page states the 12-TET definition, not its
  prevalence). The film scopes the hundred to "the tuning MercuryPitch uses" (29a).
- "Pianos are tuned in exact equal temperament": not checked against any tuning source for this
  lesson (tuners are widely said to stretch octaves, but no source was read). The film plays exact
  synthesised 12-TET (9) and never claims a real piano's tuning.
- "On a real piano every white key is the same width": not checked. The page and the film talk
  about the drawn keyboard instead.
- "Ear Lab has a drill for telling a semitone from a tone": it has none today. Leap asks all
  twelve intervals and Contour asks only up, down or same (32, 35), so the film says "listen for
  the semitone and the tone" in Leap, and the page says Leap mixes all twelve.
- "Ear Lab needs no account": no sign-in gate appears in the Ear Lab code read

  `src/features/ear-lab/IdentificationDrillView.tsx`), but no repo statement says so, unlike
