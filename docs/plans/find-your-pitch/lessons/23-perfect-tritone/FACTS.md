## Unsupported, not used

- "Fa and ti, the two notes that lean hardest" (the season's card): OMT 1e names ti and le as the
  most prominent tendency tones; fa's pull is as a chord's seventh (31).
- That the perfect intervals are called perfect because of their simple ratios: no source read
  says so; the plan's "Why perfect?" is replaced by "Halfway in hertz", which gives the ratios and
  claims nothing about the name (35).
- Any English quotation of Fux: the plan's own translation is not quoted, as the lead decided, and
  Mann's published translation was not read (37).
- A date for the nickname, a church ban, or a punishment of singers: the encyclopedia gives "at
  least the early 18th century or the late Middle Ages", cites Arnold for a medieval origin and
  Smith for none, and gives no source for its "likely fanciful"; RESEARCH quoted only the first half
  of the dating sentence and read none of the cited works. The page says nothing of them, as the
  season's card asks until those sources are read (36, 38).
- The word's history: no source read says where "tritone" comes from, beyond "spanning three
  whole tones" (19).
- "Every fourth between white keys is perfect except F up to B" as a method (OMT's white-key method,
  the plan's fourth drawer): true and read (27), but Step 4 already says it, so the drawer is cut.
- OMT's "We do not recommend this method" on counting half steps: the series counts semitones, as
  Lesson 6 did, and the page counts letters for the number and semitones for the quality, so the
  quality is read from a count the learner has done since Lesson 2 (RESEARCH, "Where the sources
  and the card disagree", item 4). The page does not quote it.
- That one tritone resolution is the only one: the page plays one, B and F to C and E, in C major
  and in D, and says no more (29).
