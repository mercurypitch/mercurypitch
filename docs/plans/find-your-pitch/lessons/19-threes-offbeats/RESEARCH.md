## Where a source contradicts the card or the season plan

1. **The standard texts count 6/8 "1 la li", not "1 and a".** Open Music Theory 2e counts compound
   divisions "la" and "li" and calls this "American traditional counting"; a second open textbook does
   the same and gives Takadimi's "ta ki da" beside it (R19-5). "1 and ah, 2 and ah" is found in a
   practitioner guide (Musical U), not in a standard text read here (R19-6). SEASON-2 § 7 item 3's
   choice therefore rests on continuity, and on one reason the season plan did not give: in a singing
   course "la" is already the sixth degree, so "1 la li" would put a solfège syllable into the count.
2. **The card's hook already tells the learner that one grouping feels like two beats and the other
   like three**, so a predict that asks "how many beats" leaks. The plan's predict asks which grouping
   feels like two (R19-1 and the plan's Cold open).
3. **Subdivide's 3/4 is not "six eighths grouped in twos".** It plays three quarter-note hits; its 6/8
   plays six eighth-note hits; both bars last 1.5 s (R19-11). So the drill contrasts three onsets with
   six, not one row of six notes grouped two ways, which is the lesson's picture. The card's citations
   are right; the article must say what the drill plays.
4. **The AP Chief Reader reports were not re-read.** Both College Board links in pedagogy-research.md
   now redirect to an index page; the claim that "compound meter" is a recurring error stays as
   pedagogy-research.md:677 reports it (Practitioner, secondary) and the plan does not quote it.
5. **The app writes no 6/8 anywhere.** The rhythm notation writes 4-beat bars only and the sheet view's
   time signature is always "n/4" (R19-12); SEASON-2 § 5.3 says the first, not the second.

## Claims

**R19-1. Six notes grouped in threes make two beats; grouped in twos, three beats.**
OMT 2e 1.10: in 6/8 "each measure has two beats (6÷3=2)", each a dotted quarter of three eighths
(paraphrase); "Compound meters are meters in which the beat divides into three". OMT 2e 1.9: simple
meter is one "in which the beat divides into two"; triple meters "contain beats that are grouped into
threes". Musical U p. 3: grouping the six "as three groups of two rather than two groups of three" means
"dividing each of the three beats into two, it's then "simple" meter again. With three beats each
divided in two, what you actually have is 3/4 time!" Full text. Strong.

**R19-2. In 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 the top number counts beats; in 6/8 it counts eighth notes, the
divisions.**
OMT 2e 1.9: "the top number of a time signature represents the number of beats in each measure" (simple
meter); OMT 2e 1.10: in 6/8 "the top number (6) means that each measure will contain six divisions" and
"the bottom number (8) means that the eighth note is the division". Full text. Strong. This is the
card's misconception shown half right.

**R19-3. Beams show the grouping: in 6/8 eighths are beamed in threes.**
Lesson 17 R17-2 (beams join flagged notes in a beat) applied to the dotted-quarter beat; Sharkey 4.3:
the eighths "are instead grouped into two larger pulses of three eighth notes". Full text. Strong.

**R19-4. 6/8 can be felt as six or as two; at a quick tempo, two.**
Musical U pp. 2-3: "6 eighth-note beats: 1,2,3,4,5,6" or "2 dotted-quarter-note beats: 1... 2..."; "when
you count all six you actually emphasise beats 1 and 4"; "It mostly comes down to tempo ... When the
piece is very fast it's too much of a mouthful ... to count out every eighth-note beat". Practitioner.
Sharkey 4.3 mentions a "1-2-3 4-5-6" count. Moderate.

**R19-5. Standard texts count compound divisions "1 la li", or with Takadimi "ta ki da".**
OMT 2e 1.10: "Divisions are counted using the syllables "la" (first division) and "li" (second
division)"; "Open Music Theory privileges American traditional counting, but this is not the only
method." Sharkey 4.3: "One method of counting is using numbers for the initial part of the beat ... and
then the syllables "la" and "li" for the other two parts of the beat", and, with Takadimi, "the syllable
"ta" is always on the strong beat" (captions "1-la-li 2-la-li" and "ta-ki-da"). HPW 1996 p. 14: "In
compound meter, "Ta" again represents an attack on the beat, and the syllables "ki" and "da" serve to
articulate the divisions of the beat." Full text. Strong.

**R19-6. "1 and a, 2 and a" (or "1 and ah") is a common practitioners' count for 6/8.**
Musical U p. 3: in 2/4 "each of those beats would divide in two (e.g. "1 and 2 and 1 and 2 and") while in
the 6/8 time signature each one divides in three (e.g. "1 and ah 2 and ah 1 and ah 2 and ah")."
Practitioner. Weak as a standard (no textbook read uses it). The plan uses it as SEASON-2 § 7 item 3
recommends, says once that books also use "1 la li", and gives the reason: "la" is a solfège syllable.

**R19-7. A system that tells duple from triple divisions helped children recognise rhythms.**
Colley 1987, abstract: "a syllabic system that differentiated between duple and triple subdivisions of
the beat improved recognition skills to a greater degree than one that did not" (160 second- and
third-graders; patterns combined into "complete measures of 4/4 or 6/8"). Abstract only. Weak to
Moderate. "1 and, 2 and" against "1 and a, 2 and a" does tell them apart.

**R19-8. A triplet is three notes in the time of two, marked with a 3; it borrows compound metre's
division.**
OMT 2e 1.11: "Triplets are a type of tuplet in which a beat (or subdivision, or multiple beats) in
simple meter is divided into three parts"; "Triplets are sometimes thought of as "borrowed" from
compound meter". ABRSM 2023 p. 12, Grade 2 item 1: "Triplets, and triplet note groups with rests".
Full text. Strong. Count: "1 trip-let 2 trip-let" (SEASON-2 § 7 item 3's recommendation; no source
read gives it, so it is a choice, like R19-6).

**R19-9. Syncopation is an accent off the beat; the offbeat is the "and".**
OMT 2e 1.11: "Syncopation happens when there are off-beat rhythmic accents, which can be created by
ties, dots, rests, and/or dynamics." The "and" is the half-beat (Lesson 17 R17-8). Full text. Strong.

**R19-10. ABRSM sets triplets at Grade 2 and 6/8 at Grade 3.** ABRSM 2023 p. 12: Grade 2 item 1
("Triplets"); Grade 3 item 1 ("Compound time signatures of 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8"). Full text. Strong. So
this lesson reaches past the first grades on purpose, as the season's promise says (SEASON-2 § 1.1).

## Interesting findings

1. **Same six notes, same length of bar, two metres.** In the app's own Subdivide drill a 3/4 bar and a
   6/8 bar each last 1.5 s; what changes is how the time is cut (R19-11). Strong. **Possible Step 1
   line** ("the bar is the same length; the grouping is the difference").
2. **"Two groups of two" is not "two beats".** The plan's cold open turns on it: groups of three give
   two beats, groups of two give three (R19-1). Strong. **Possible cold open.**
3. **Takadimi gives every position in the beat its own syllable**, unlike the other systems its authors
   surveyed: HPW 1996 p. 14, "(Note that unlike the other systems examined Takadimi specifies a unique
   syllable for each subdivision.)" Strong. **Possible Go deeper.**
4. **Counts that tell twos from threes helped children hear the difference** (R19-7). Weak to Moderate.
   **Possible Go deeper**, the reason for "and" against "and a".
5. **A triplet is borrowed from compound metre** (R19-8): the three-way split that 6/8 has in every beat
   appears for a moment in 4/4. Strong. **Possible wrap line.**
