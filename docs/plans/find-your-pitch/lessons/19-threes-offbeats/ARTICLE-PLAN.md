## Four words you'll meet

- **Simple metre**: each beat splits in two, as in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4. [Play: 3/4, eighths]
- **Compound metre**: each beat splits in three, as in 6/8. [Play: 6/8, eighths]
- **Triplet**: three notes squeezed into the time of two, marked with a 3. [Play: 1 trip-let 2]
- **Syncopation**: an accent off the beat. [Play: a clap on "2 and", held]

Facts: R19-1, R19-8, R19-9. Words: about 70.

## Step 1 of 4: What do the numbers in 6/8 mean?

- Point (the card's misconception, shown half right): in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 the top number counts beats.
  In 6/8 it counts eighth notes: six of them, in two groups of three, so two beats, each a dotted
  quarter. 3/4 also holds six eighths, in three groups of two. The beams show the grouping: in threes in
  6/8, in twos in 3/4.
- Common mix-up card: Belief "The top number is how many beats you feel." Why it seems right: it is, in
  2/4, 3/4 and 4/4. The evidence: 6/8 has six eighths and two beats (Open Music Theory, 1.10). The better
  idea: in 6/8, count the groups.
- Examples: one bar of six beamed eighths in 6/8, then in 3/4, each played with its accents on the pads,
  then written on one line (rhythm notation with beams by beat, film kit § 6.1 item 3).
- Try: switch 6/8 · 3/4 and watch the beams regroup. Notice: the notes don't move; the beams and the
  accents do.
- Toy: pads with a written bar above, 6/8 · 3/4.
- Facts: R19-1, R19-2, R19-3. Words: about 130 with the card.

## Step 2 of 4: How do I count 6/8?

- Point: two ways. Slowly, count all six, leaning on 1 and 4. At speed, count the two beats and their
  thirds: "1 and a, 2 and a". 3/4's six eighths count "1 and 2 and 3 and". Books also write 6/8's thirds
  as "1 la li" or "ta ki da"; here "la" is a syllable you sing, so this series says "and a".
- Examples: the 6/8 tune written for the lesson, at 60 dotted-quarter beats a minute, in C on C4 (C3
  for the bass view): | do re mi (eighths) so (dotted quarter) | mi re do (eighths) re (dotted quarter) |
  do re mi mi re do (eighths) | do (dotted half) |; Merc counts it both ways. Then the 3/4 tune at 90: | do re mi | so (half) mi | re re do | do (dotted
  half) |, counted "1 and 2 and 3 and" over its eighth pulse.
- Try: count along with Count on. Notice: "and a" for threes, "and" for twos.
- Toy: written tune with count syllables under it; 6/8 · 3/4.
- Facts: R19-4, R19-5, R19-6, R19-7. Words: about 120.

## Step 3 of 4: What's a triplet?

- Point: three notes in the time of two, borrowed from compound metre into 4/4, with a bracket and a 3
  over them. Count "1 trip-let 2".
- Examples, in 4/4 at 80 BPM: | 1 (quarter) 2 (two eighths) 3 (triplet) 4 (quarter) |: "1, 2 and, 3
  trip-let, 4"; then Pulse's "triplet on 1" pattern (R19-13).
- Plays: the pads squeeze three into the space of two, and the bracket draws over them (card's
  signature).
- Try: switch Eighths · Triplet on beat 3. Notice: beat 3 still starts on time; it's split three ways.
- Toy: pads with a written bar, Eighths · Triplet.
- Facts: R19-8, R19 finding 5. Words: about 100.

## Step 4 of 4: What makes a rhythm syncopated?

- Point: the offbeat is the "and" between beats. A syncopation puts an accent there: by holding a note
  over the next beat (a tie or a dot), by a rest on the beat, or simply by stressing the "and".
- Examples, in 4/4 at 80 BPM: claps on every "and" (offbeats); then | 1 (quarter) 2 (eighth) and
  (quarter tied to an eighth, held through 3) and 4 |, the accent on "2 and", so beat 3 has no new
  note; then the same bar unsyncopated, for contrast.
- Plays: a clap is pulled off the beat onto an "and" (card's signature).
- Try: drag the clap from 3 to "2 and". Notice: beat 3 feels empty, and the bar leans forward.
- Toy: pads with an accent you drag.
- Facts: R19-9, R19-13. Words: about 110.

- An accent on the "and" is a syncopation.
