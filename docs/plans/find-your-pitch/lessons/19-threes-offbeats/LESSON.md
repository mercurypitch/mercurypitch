## Step 1 of 4: What do the numbers in 6/8 mean?

Tavi: "Six on top means six beats a bar, doesn't it?"

In 4/4 the top number counts beats and the bottom names the beat's note. [Play 6/8] In 6/8 both
numbers are about eighth notes: six of them, in two groups of three. Each group is a beat, a dotted
quarter split in three, so the 8 names the split, not the beat. That's compound metre. [Play 3/4]
3/4 holds six eighths too, in three groups of two: three quarter-note beats, each split in two.
That's simple metre. Beams join each beat's eighths.

> **Common mix-up:** "The top number is how many beats you feel."
> **Why it seems right:** in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4, it is.
> **The evidence:** a bar of 6/8 holds six eighth notes, but Open Music Theory, a free textbook,
> counts two beats in it: six divided by three.
> **The better idea:** in 6/8 the top number counts eighths. Count the groups of three to find the
> beats, each a dotted quarter (in Britain, a dotted crotchet).

**Try:** switch 6/8 · 3/4 and play each.
**Notice:** the six notes don't move. The beams and the accents do.

_Toy: six pads with the bar written above them on one line, 6/8 · 3/4, on Subdivide's drums._

## Step 2 of 4: How do you count 6/8?

Slowly, you can count all six, leaning on 1 and 4. At speed, count the two beats and say their
thirds: "1 and a, 2 and a". 3/4's six eighths count "1 and 2 and 3 and". Books often say "1 la
li"; this series keeps "la" for singing. An 1881 song book prints the round "Row, row, row your
boat" in 6/8: each "mer-ri-ly" fills one beat, "1 and a", then "2 and a". Two tunes written for this
lesson: [Play the 6/8 tune] [Play the 3/4 tune]

**Try:** switch on Count, play each tune, and say the counts as they light.
**Notice:** "and a" splits a beat in three; "and" splits it in two.

_Toy: the 6/8 tune and the 3/4 tune on your staff, with Count, 6/8 · 3/4, Clef and See it on the
pitch line._

> **Go deeper**
>
> - **Takadimi**, the syllables from Lesson 17's Go deeper, counts each beat of 6/8 "ta ki da", with
>   "ta" on the beat. Its authors note that, unlike the other systems they looked at, it gives every
>   place in a beat its own syllable.
> - **A count for threes.** In one study, young schoolchildren taught a count that told twos from
>   threes improved more at recognising rhythms than children taught one that didn't.
> - **Exam grades.** In ABRSM's theory exams, triplets come at Grade 2 and 6/8 at Grade 3.

## Step 3 of 4: What's a triplet?

In 4/4 a beat usually splits in two: "1 and". A triplet splits it in three, three notes in the time
of two, marked with a 3. It borrows compound metre's split for one beat. Count it "1 trip-let".
[Play] This bar counts "1, 2 and, 3 trip-let, 4". At 60 BPM the triplet's notes come a third of a
second apart, as fast as the 6/8 tune's eighths.

**Try:** switch Eighths · Triplet on beat 3, and play each.
**Notice:** beat 3 still starts on its click. It just splits three ways.

_Toy: the pads with one bar of 4/4 written above them, at 60 BPM, with Eighths · Triplet and Count._

## Step 4 of 4: What makes a rhythm syncopated?

An offbeat falls between the beats, such as on an "and". A syncopation puts an accent there, off the
beat. A note can start on an "and" and be held over the next beat, by a tie or a dot. A rest can
take the beat. Or the "and" can simply be played louder. [Play] Here the note on beat 3 moves half a
beat earlier, to "2 and", and is held through beat 3, so nothing new starts on 3. It counts "1, 2 and
(3) 4".

**Try:** switch On 3 · On 2 and, and listen to each.
**Notice:** with the note on "2 and", the click on 3 falls inside a held note.

_Toy: the pads with one bar of 4/4 written above them, at 60 BPM, with On 3 · On 2 and and Count._

## Tap the beat

**Tap the beat.** Each tune plays twice. The first time, the click plays the beat: tap with it, so
the page can line your taps up with the sound. The second time the click stops: keep tapping the
beat on your own. That's two taps a bar in the 6/8 tune and three in the 3/4 tune. Each tap sounds a
clap. You can say the count aloud as you tap, if you like.

[ Start: the 6/8 tune ] [ Start: the 3/4 tune ]

(After either Start, the click counts in one bar, beat 1 on the high click, then runs on under the
tune's first time through and stops for its second. The pad takes a tap on a press, Space or Enter,

After a take, each tap from the second time through shows as a tick where it fell, against the beat
