## Four words you'll meet

- **Note value**: how long a note lasts, as a share of a whole note. [Play: a whole note, then two
  halves]
- **Rest**: a written silence, with its own value. [Play: note, rest, note, rest]
- **Dot**: a dot after a note adds half its value. [Play: a dotted half, then a quarter]
- **Tie**: a curve joining two notes of the same pitch into one sound. [Play: two tied quarters]

Facts: R17-3, R17-4. Words: about 70.

## Step 1 of 5: What is a quarter note a quarter of?

- Point: a whole note. Halve it and you get two half notes, then four quarter notes, eight eighth notes,
  sixteen sixteenth notes. Each halving changes the shape: a stem, a filled head, a flag; flags join
  as beams. In 4/4 the bottom 4 says the quarter note gets the beat, the top 4 that there are four of
  them in a bar. In Britain they're called semibreve, minim, crotchet, quaver and semiquaver (named
  once).
- Signature: Lesson 8's pads grow noteheads and stems; halving a pad makes two eighths, which a beam
  joins; joining two makes a half note (card).
- Examples, at 60 BPM on C4, each bar played then written: one whole note; two halves; four quarters;
  eight eighths; then one beat of four sixteenths.
- Try: press Halve and Join on the pads and play each bar. Notice: every bar lasts four beats, however
  it is split.
- Toy: the pads, then the written bar on one line (as The Chart draws it, `RhythmScore.tsx:1-12`).
- Facts: R17-1, R17-2, R17-5, R17-6. Words: about 120.

## Step 2 of 5: So what sets how long a note lasts?

- Point (the misconception shown half right): within one tempo each value has one length, so "a length
  of time" is half right. But the values are fractions of each other, and the tempo sets the clock: at
  60 BPM a quarter note lasts 1 s, at 120 BPM half a second, Lesson 8's BPM.
- Common mix-up card: Belief "A note value is a length of time." Why it seems right: at one tempo, a
  quarter note always lasts the same. The evidence: the same written bar at 60 and at 120 BPM: every
  note halves, and the page doesn't change. The better idea: a note value is a share of a whole note;
  the tempo turns shares into seconds.
- Examples: bar of two quarters and a half note, at 60 then 120 BPM, the stopwatch strip reading
  1 s, 1 s, 2 s, then 0.5 s, 0.5 s, 1 s.
- Try: drag the tempo from 60 to 120 and play. Notice: the noteheads never change.
- Toy: written bar with a tempo slider and the stopwatch strip.
- Facts: R17-7. Words: about 120 with the card.

## Step 3 of 5: How do you count the short ones?

- Point: numbers on the beats, "and" between them, Lesson 8's counting carried on. Eighths: "1 and 2
  and 3 and 4 and". Sixteenths: "1 e and a". Many teachers count this way; others use syllables such
  as Takadimi (Go deeper).
- Examples, at 60 BPM: a bar of eighths counted aloud by Merc; then quarter, two eighths, four
  sixteenths, quarter ("1, 2 and, 3 e and a, 4").
- Try: switch on Count, and say the count with Merc. Notice: the numbers always land on the beats.
- Toy: written bar with count syllables under the notes (fade on second play).
- Facts: R17-8, R17-11. Words: about 100.

## Step 4 of 5: What does a silence look like?

- Point: every value has a rest of the same length: whole, half, quarter, eighth and sixteenth rests.
  Count through a rest as you would a note.
- Examples, at 60 BPM: quarter, quarter rest, quarter, quarter rest; then the Ear Lab's "rest on 2"
  pattern, onsets on 1, 3 and 4 (R17-14).
- Try: tap a pad off to make a rest, and play. Notice: the beat keeps going through the silence.
- Toy: pads with Note · Rest per pad, written bar above.
- Facts: R17-3. Words: about 90.

## Step 5 of 5: How do you write a note longer than its shape?

- Point: dots and ties. A dot adds half the note's value: a dotted half lasts three beats, a dotted
  quarter one and a half. A tie joins two notes of the same pitch into one sound, even across a bar
  line.
- Examples, at 60 BPM: dotted half then quarter; dotted quarter, eighth, half; a quarter tied over the
  bar line to a quarter (two beats, one sound).
- Try: add a dot, then a tie, and play. Notice: tied notes sound once, for both values.
- Toy: written bar with Dot and Tie buttons.
- Facts: R17-4. Words: about 100.

## Wrap

- Note values are fractions of a whole note: half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth. Each has a rest.
- The tempo turns them into seconds: at 60 BPM a quarter lasts 1 s, at 120 half a second.
- Count beats with numbers and the "and" between them; a dot adds half; a tie joins two notes into one
  sound.
