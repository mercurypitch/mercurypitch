Of the alternative they support "harder" in part: copied intervals came out compressed, in proportion to their size, so along the group's trend a bigger interval fell further short in cents (24 to 26); tunes passed along by singing drifted towards small intervals, while tunes copied with a slider drifted towards large ones, and the authors' hypothesis for the difference, which they explored with a simulation and did not measure, is that large intervals "are harder to produce by the vocal system than smaller ones" (29); and a leap asks the folds for a bigger change in one move, an inference (28). ## Unsupported, not used

- "A leap is so much harder to sing than a step": no step-versus-leap contrast is reported (P&B
  2007's compression slope runs across steps and leaps alike, 23, 24), and the slopes put both
  shortfalls under a semitone for accurate singers (1, 26). Anglada-Tort 2023's "harder to produce"
  is its authors' hypothesis, explored with a simulation (29). The alternative hook keeps the
  words, as the owner asked, and the page answers it only in part.
- "Melodies move by steps more than leaps because leaps are harder to sing": OMT 2e 2.1 lists
  "Pitch proximity: The tendency for melodies to progress by steps more than leaps and by small
  leaps more than large leaps." as a feature of melodies, with no cause. Anglada-Tort 2023 finds
  sung tunes drifting towards small intervals and slider-copied ones towards large intervals, and
  gives vocal production as one possible explanation, explored with a simulation (29). The page
  states the transmission finding and names no cause.
- "Mi is the best note to aim with": no source tests which middle note helps a singer land a fifth.
  The predict asks which note splits the leap most evenly, which arithmetic answers, and the reveal
  never says that mi, or any middle note, helps a singer aim (11, 12).
- "Imagining the note makes you sing it more accurately": P&H 2013 is a correlation on a monotone
  task (32). Not said.
- "Slow practice trains leaps": L-M & M 2014 and Dalla Bella 2007 are about the tempo a song was
  sung at (41), not training. Not said.
- "Compression happens because the voice undershoots a large movement": P&B 2007's discussion
  weighs explanations without settling one. Not used; the page's physical sentence is the
  inference in 28.
- Kodály claims beyond the article's paragraph (40), including any sequence for adults: Choksy
  was not read. Not used.
- The app's help for Interval Trainer, "Anchor on the first note before you leap" (Lesson 6
  FACTS 40), is advice without a source; the page's own advice, hear the landing note first, rests
  on 31 and 32.
- Every sentence of the Ascent's week 4 (61): not repeated as a fact.
- Any claim that an interval is "the hardest", or a figure for how often leaps miss: no source
  read gives one.
