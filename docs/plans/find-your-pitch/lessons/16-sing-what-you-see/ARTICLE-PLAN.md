## Four words you'll meet

- **Sight-singing**: singing a tune from the page, the first time you see it. [Play: do re mi re do]
- **Relative pitch**: hearing a note by its distance from another. [Play: C4, then D4]
- **Absolute pitch**: naming or singing a note with no other note to start from; often called perfect
  pitch. [no sound]
- **Tonic triad**: do, mi and so, the key's home chord, here sung one note at a time. [Play: C4 E4 G4]

Facts: R16-1, R16-8. Words: about 75.

## Step 1 of 5: Where is do on the page?

- Point: Lesson 4's staircase rotates into the staff (the card's signature) and the house lands on
  do's line or space. In C, do is C: middle C's ledger line in the treble clef, the second space in the
  bass clef. From do, each line or space up is the next syllable: re, mi, fa, so.
- Examples: play do re mi fa so in C, 0.6 s each, in the learner's clef (treble C4 to G4, bass C3 to G3),
  syllables under the notes, the house on C.
- Plays: the staircase first (Lesson 4's picture, sound with it), then it turns into the staff.
- Try: tap the house, then any note, to hear do and that note. Notice: count from the house and you know
  the syllable.
- Toy: staff toy with the house (`home-glyph.ts`), clef by the voice choice, syllables on.
- Facts: R16-7, R16-9, R15-5. Words: about 110.

## Step 2 of 5: Do I need perfect pitch for this?

- Tavi, deep question: "So how did I do that without perfect pitch?"
- Point (refuted, and half right): reading doesn't use absolute pitch. Researchers think it can be
  learned only very early, up to about age 6, and that after that we come to hear how notes relate
  (Takeuchi and Hulse, 1993). Reading needs a first note, not a stored one. Half right: you do need that first
  note, which is why exams play it: the Royal Conservatory's examiner plays do-mi-so and do before a
  singer reads.
- Common mix-up card: Belief "You need perfect pitch to sing from the page." Why it seems right: a
  singer who reads well seems to pull the notes out of the air. The evidence: absolute pitch is thought
  to be learned in early childhood (Takeuchi and Hulse, 1993); sight-singing exams give the key chord
  and first note before every test (RCM, 2019). The better idea: hear do, then count steps from it.
- Note for the writer: no line says absolute pitch is "rare" or gives a share of people until a
  prevalence source is read (R16-1).
- Try: play do, then sing re from the staff before tapping it to check. Notice: one note gave you the
  next.
- Toy: staff toy with Play do · Check the next note.
- Facts: R16-1, R16-2. Words: about 120 with the card.

## Step 3 of 5: How do I hear the next note before I sing it?

- Point: "hear it inside". Each note lights a beat before it sounds; in that beat, imagine it. Singers
  call this audiation, a teacher's idea (Gordon); in one study, people who said they heard tunes vividly
  in their heads sang more of them in tune. That's a link, not proof that practising it helps.
- Examples: do re mi re do in C; each note lights, 1 s of silence, then plays 0.6 s. The pitch-line
  toggle is off from here.
- Try: switch on Hear it inside, and hum the note in the silent beat before it plays. Notice: the sound
  often lands where you already heard it.
- Toy: staff toy with Hear it inside on.
- Facts: R16-5. Words: about 100.

## Step 4 of 5: What about a skip?

- Point: the tonic triad. Do, mi and so sit on three lines in a row in the treble clef (middle C's
  ledger line, E4, G4), or three spaces in a row in the bass clef (C3, E3, G3): line to line, or space to
  space, skips one letter. Do to mi is 4 semitones and mi to so 3, Lesson 9's ladder.
- Examples: play do mi so mi do in C, 0.6 s a note, in the learner's clef; then do re mi and do mi side
  by side.
- Try: play do, hear mi inside, then tap it. Notice: a skip on the staff jumps a line, or a space.
- Toy: staff toy, Hear it inside on.
- Facts: R16-8; Lesson 9 quick check 2. Words: about 100.

## Step 5 of 5: Can I read it without the names?

- Point: the syllables fade. The first time a phrase is shown, its syllables sit under the notes; the
  second time only the house is left (concreteness fading). Do is still there to count from.
- Examples: do re mi re do with names, then without; do mi so mi do with names, then without.
- Try: read each phrase with names off before you play it. Notice: the house is enough.
- Toy: staff toy, Names on · off.
- Facts: R16-6. Words: about 80.
