## Unsupported, not used

- How common absolute pitch is, "1 in 10,000" or "rare": found only in a secondary citation, not
  verified (R16-1). The card's "rare" and any "most singers" stay off the page, as the lead answered
  (21).
- "Choirs take a note from a piano" (the card): no source read; the exam board stands in (23).
- Which method, movable or fixed do, teaches sight-singing better: Weak and confounded (R16-4,
  D&M 1995's abstract); the page claims nothing about methods. D&M's "years of school choir
  experience was the strongest predictor" was offered for the Wrap; it is left out, as abstract-only
  and outside the lesson's question.
- The plan's three Go deeper items rest on sources read, and are cut for length (3): the Royal
  Conservatory's first sight-singing test is this lesson's shape (R16-2, Strong); people with
  absolute pitch name notes with learned labels, and "do not have superior memory for pitches per
  se" (T&H 1993's abstract, Moderate); exams accept fixed do, movable do, numbers or "la" (R16-3,
  Strong). Any of them may return if a later edit frees the words.
- That hearing a note inside trains accuracy: no intervention was tested (R16-5); the page says "a
  link, not proof" (25).
- P&H 2013's "Pitch discrimination was not predicted by auditory imagery scores": true of the
  study, and not needed on this page.
- The sight-reading article's lines on how many students can sight-sing: not used, since no
  figure is sourced there.
- Henry (2004): not a test of pattern teaching against note-naming (RESEARCH, finding 4); not used.
- That adults cannot learn absolute pitch: "difficult or impossible to acquire" is a 1993 review's
  summary. A later paper is titled "Absolute pitch can be learned by some adults" (Van Hedger,
  Heald and Nusbaum 2019, PLoS One 14(9): e0223047, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0223047, PubMed
  31550277); only its PubMed record was seen, on 2 November 2026, and the paper was not read, so
  the page claims nothing from it, dates the review's finding and leaves out "hard to learn after
  that" (20).
