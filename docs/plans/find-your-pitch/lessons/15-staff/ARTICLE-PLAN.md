## Four words you'll meet

- **Staff**: five lines, and the spaces between them, that notes sit on. [Play: E4 up to F5]
- **Clef**: the sign at the start of a staff that names one line, so you can count the rest. [Play: G4]
- **Ledger line**: a short line that carries the staff on past its top or bottom. [Play: C4]
- **Grand staff**: a treble staff and a bass staff joined, as piano music is written. [Play: C3, C4, C5]

Facts: R15-1, R15-3, R15-6, R15-8. Words: about 75.

## Step 1 of 5: Which nine notes does a staff hold?

- Point: the treble clef curls round the second line from the bottom and names it G, the G above
  middle C, G4. Count letters up and down from it: lines E G B D F, spaces F A C E. One line or space
  up is always the next letter.
- Examples: play G4 alone; then G4 A4 B4 (up two positions) and G4 F4 E4 (down two). Treble clef.
- Plays: each note sounds and lights on the pitch line first, then lands on the staff.
- Try: tap any line or space to hear its note and see its letter. Notice: line, space, line: each
  step up is the next letter, never one skipped.
- Toy: the staff toy, treble clef, nine positions, tap to play; pitch-line toggle.
- Go deeper (optional): one mnemonic per clef, OMT's "Every Good Bird Does Fly" and "FACE" (R15-5).
- Facts: R15-2, R15-3, R15-5. Words: about 110.

## Step 2 of 5: Is every step up the staff the same size?

- Tavi, sincere guess (card): "Each step up the staff looks the same. Isn't it the same step in sound?"
- Point (shown half right): in letters, yes, every step is one letter. In sound, no: E to F and B to C
  are semitones, the other steps tones, Lesson 4's uneven staircase. The staff counts letters, not
  semitones. A sharp or flat before a note moves it a semitone and keeps its line or space.
- Examples: play E4 F4 G4 (a semitone, then a tone) and B4 C5 D5 (the same); then F4, then F♯4 on F's
  space with a ♯ to its left; then B4 and B♭4 on the middle line.
- Plays: with the pitch-line toggle on, the twelve semitone lanes stay under the staff and the black
  lanes fold away, so the white-key lanes close up at E to F and B to C (the card's signature).
- Common mix-up card: Belief "Every step up the staff is the same step in sound." Why it seems right:
  the lines and spaces are evenly spaced. The evidence: E to F and B to C have no black key between
  them; on the pitch line they sit a semitone apart, the others a tone. The better idea: the staff
  counts letters; a sharp or flat adjusts the sound.
- Try: play E to F, then F to G, with the toggle on. Notice: one lane, then two.
- Toy: the staff over the pitch line, toggle on.
- Facts: R15-10, R15-11; Lesson 2 FACTS 13 to 15; Lesson 4's staircase (lessons/04-major-scale/LESSON.md:91).
  Words: about 120 with the card.

## Step 3 of 5: What happens past the top or bottom line?

- Point: ledger lines. A note one letter above the top line sits in the space above (G5); one more
  needs a short line of its own (A5). Below the treble staff, D4 sits under the bottom line and middle
  C on one ledger line. This season reads up to two ledger lines each way.
- Examples, treble clef: G5 then A5 (one ledger line), C6 (two); D4, then C4 (one), A3 (two).
- Plays: each note, pitch line first.
- Try: drag a note above the staff and back. Notice: ledger lines appear only as far as the note.
- Toy: staff toy with a draggable note, ledger lines drawn up to two each way.
- Facts: R15-6 (and ABRSM Grade 2's "two ledger lines below and above each stave"). Words: about 100.

## Step 4 of 5: Where do the low notes go?

- Point: the bass clef names the second line from the top F, the F below middle C, F3: its two dots sit
  either side of that line. Lines G B D F A, spaces A C E G. Low voices, and Merc, read this clef.
- Examples: play F3 alone (Merc sings it on "ooh"); then G2 (bottom line) up to A3 (top line), nine
  notes; then C4, one ledger line above the bass staff.
- Plays: Merc's demonstration in the bass clef, Tavi's in the treble (SEASON-2 § 1.5 item 2).
- Try: switch Treble · Bass and tap the same line in each. Notice: the same line names a different
  note; the clef decides.
- Toy: staff toy with a clef switch.
- Facts: R15-4, R15-5, R15-17 (the app's lower presets start below the treble staff). Words: about 110.

## Step 5 of 5: Where do the two staves meet?

- Point: the grand staff, treble above bass, joined by a brace. Middle C sits on the one ledger line
  between them: one below the treble, one above the bass, the same key and the same sound. Under it,
  the keyboard: every staff note has one key. The hook's answer: a piano writes its 88 keys, A0 to C8,
  on two staves of five lines, with ledger lines past their edges.
- Examples: play C4 written below the treble staff, then C4 written above the bass staff (same sound);
  then the 21 white keys G2 to F5 in one sweep, 0.25 s each. Do not play A0 or C8 (27.5 Hz will not
  sound on most phone speakers; say the range in words).
- Then "Which clef is yours?": lower voices read the bass clef, higher voices the treble; the
  sing-along asks which.
- Try: tap any key to see its note light on the grand staff, and any staff note to hear its key.
  Notice: the black keys borrow a letter's line, with a sharp.
- Toy: grand staff over Lesson 1's keyboard (the card's signature: the keyboard slides under it).
- Facts: R15-7, R15-8, R15-9, R15-12. Words: about 120.

## Wrap

- A staff's five lines and four spaces hold nine notes, one letter each. A clef names one line: the
  treble clef G4, the bass clef F3. Count letters from it.
- The staff counts letters, not semitones: E to F and B to C are semitones, the other steps tones.
- Ledger lines carry on past the edges, and the grand staff joins the two staves at middle C.
