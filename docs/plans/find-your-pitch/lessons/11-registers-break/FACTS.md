## Unsupported, not used

- "Your voice flips at roughly the same note every time you slide up": no source read follows one
  singer's flip over repeated slides. Henrich 2006 gives the overlap as an average range, inside
  which the gear can be a choice (19); Cardoso 2023 found breaks in fewer than a third of the slides
  (28); Švec 1999 found the jump up higher than the jump down (29); and Švec 2008 gives one
  untrained woman's switch only as "around tones B4-C#5", one singer (82). Roubeau 2009's abstract
  says the pitches of transitions "are described in detail", but its full text was not read. The old
  hook was an alternative until the owner dropped it on 22 October 2026 (1).
- A historical teacher's break "around F4 or F4# in both male and female voices, individual
  differences apart" (Henrich 2006, p. 5, on Behnke): a nineteenth-century account. Not used.
- Mixed voice and belting: Henrich 2006 places "belting" and the voix mixte in M1 or between the
  mechanisms (pp. 10 and 11), and Herbst & Elemans 2025 § 3 describes "'mixing' or 'blending'" as a
  possible transition; the page teaches neither, per the brief (write-l11 rule 4). This PR drops
  "mix" from S12, in the curriculum and in the season's name for it (2).
- Loudness: one study's healthy untrained man (Hoppe 2003, PMID 14513959, abstract) showed a level
  drop into falsetto; one subject, abstract only. The model changes no loudness (74).
- How long smoothing takes, or that any exercise smooths the switch: no source read measures
  training. Arneson's guide is advice, and its own first page says exercises "have no intrinsic
  value" outside how they are used (34). The page reports the suggestion and claims no effect.
- "A flip is never a sign of a voice problem": no source read rules a problem out from a flip, and
  REQ-GVC-026 forbids clearing anything from audio. The page teaches a flip on a slow slide as a
  change of gear and keeps the comfort check and the doctor lines (12, 52, 56; the coach's list).
- Cardoso 2023's explanations for why breaks were few (training, the software, the task): its
  discussion weighs them without testing them. Not used.
- The CoMeT committee's report itself: read only as Henrich 2006 reports it, through Hollien (36).
- Herbst & Elemans 2025 § 4's amateurs "'stuck' in vocal register M1": read, not used, since the
  page claims nothing about amateurs.
- Echternach 2010 (18 untrained men), Tokuda 2010, Dippold 2015, Schlegel 2024 (dogs), Herbst and
  colleagues' 2025 monkey yodels and Roubeau 2004 (42 subjects, in French): read as abstracts while
  searching, not used.
- Jazz, blues and rock as styles that show a flip: no source read names them. Only classical singing
  and the yodel are named (35).
- Lehoux, Herbst, Dobiáš and Švec 2023, the excised-larynx study Herbst & Elemans 2025 § 2(b) cites:
  not read; the page rests its lab sentence on Henrich 2006, Švec 1999 and Tokuda 2007 (24).
