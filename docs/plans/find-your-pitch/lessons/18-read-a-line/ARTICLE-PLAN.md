## Four words you'll meet

- **Count-in**: a bar of the beat before the music starts, to set the tempo. [Play: four clicks at 60]
- **Dynamics**: marks for loud and quiet: p for quiet, f for loud. [Play: do mi so, p then f]
- **Staccato**: a dot over or under a note: sing it separated, with space after it. [Play: do mi so
  staccato]
- **Slur**: a curve over different notes: sing them smoothly, joined. [Play: do re mi slurred]

Facts: R18-3, R18-5, R18-6. Words: about 80.

## Step 1 of 4: Do readers really name every note?

- Point (shown half right): names help at first, as in Lesson 16. But sight-singing is taught as
  patterns, the scale step and the tonic triad: in one study, 67 beginning high-school singers learned 15
  such skills over 12 weeks and both groups improved; across 92 studies, aural training and singing or
  solfège were among the kinds of training that helped, though the effects were small.
- Common mix-up card: Belief "Sight-singing means working out each note's name, then singing it." Why it
  seems right: that's how Lesson 16 began, one note at a time. The evidence: Henry (2004) and Mishra
  (2014), as above, in plain words; no source says how far ahead readers look, so the card says nothing
  about it. The better idea: read in groups, keep the beat.
- Example: the cold open's line read twice: names first (Merc stops on each, the beat runs on without
  him), then in groups (he keeps up).
- Try: play both reads. Notice: naming each note fell behind the click.
- Toy: staff with the beat grid and cursor; Read note by note · Read in groups.
- Facts: R18-1, R18-2, R18-4. Words: about 130 with the card.

## Step 2 of 4: Which patterns should my eye look for?

- Point: three to start: a run of steps (neighbouring lines and spaces), a repeated note (the same line
  or space twice), and do-mi-so (three lines, or three spaces, in a row).
- Examples: | do re mi fa | so (half) so (half) |; then | so mi do do | re (half) do (half) |; each
  pattern brackets itself as the cursor reaches it (card's signature).
- Try: tap each bracket to hear that pattern alone. Notice: the shape on the staff is the pattern in
  sound.
- Toy: staff with brackets, Patterns on · off.
- Facts: R18-10. Words: about 100.

## Step 3 of 4: How do I keep the beat going?

- Point: count in a bar first; exam singers tap one bar of the beat before they sing. Then keep going: if
  a note slips, catch the next one on its beat. Halves last two beats: count through them ("3, 4").
- Example: | do mi so so | mi re do (half) |, count-in then sung, the cursor at 60 BPM, the count shown
  under the notes ("1 2 3 4 | 1 2 3 (4)").
- Try: tap the beat on the pad while Merc sings. Notice: the cursor never waits.
- Toy: staff with cursor and a beat pad (Lesson 17's).
- Facts: R18-3, R17-7. Words: about 100.

## Step 4 of 4: What do the other marks on the page ask for?

- Point (T32, light): p and f, quiet and loud; a hairpin that opens, getting louder, and one that closes,
  getting quieter; a staccato dot, separated; a slur, smooth; a breath mark, a comma above the staff at
  a phrase's end, where you breathe without stopping the beat. Louder never means higher or pushing
  (Lesson 14's swell). Each mark appears over the sound it asks for (card's signature).
- Examples: do mi so mi do p, then f; so held two beats under a hairpin that opens then closes (Lesson
  14's swell, small); do mi so staccato; do re mi slurred; a breath mark after bar 1.
- Try: tap each mark to hear what it asks for. Notice: none of them changes a note's pitch.
- Toy: staff with the marks, each playable.
- Facts: R18-5, R18-6, R18-7, R18-8, R18-9. Words: about 130.

## Wrap

- Readers see patterns, not single notes: a run, a repeat, do-mi-so.
- Count in, then keep the beat going; a breath mark takes its time from the note before it.
- p and f, hairpins, staccato and slurs shape a line; none of them moves a note.
