## What a vibrato is, and its two measures

11. A vibrato is a regular, roughly sinusoidal wave in a held note's pitch, described by its rate, the swings it makes a second, and its extent, how far the pitch moves up and down from its average, often written with ± | R1 (Moderate): Sundberg 1994, p. 48, "the vibrato physically corresponds to a periodic, rather sinusoidal, modulation of the phonation frequency"; "The vibrato rate specifies the number of undulations per second while the extent describes how far phonation frequency departs up and down from its average during a vibrato cycle. Often the extent is preceded by plus and minus signs" | carried; the four words (Vibrato, Rate, Extent: "It's written with ±"), Step 2, the Wrap. The page says "a regular wave in a held note's pitch", Lesson 5's words (10), and "how far each swing reaches from the middle, up and down" for the average
12. Measured trained singers' vibratos run at about 5 to 8 swings a second, with an extent under a semitone either side, ±100 cents | R3 (Moderate): Sundberg 1994, p. 57, "an undulation of the fundamental frequency ranging from 5 to 8 Hz in rate and less than ±1 semitone in extent" (the scan prints "+1"); p. 51, "the extent is generally narrower than ±1 semitones"; the review centres on Western operatic singing (p. 45) | carried; Step 2's "Trained singers have been measured at about 5 to 8 swings a second, usually under ±100 cents, a semitone either way". "Trained singers have been measured", never "singers' vibratos are": the review is of trained, mostly operatic singers. A semitone is 100 cents (Lesson 2's)
13. Measured averages sit near six swings a second: 5.4 (men) and 5.9 (women) for sustained vowels in the laboratory, 6.6 across 29 recorded artists, 6.1 across ten singers on record | R4 (Moderate): Sundberg 1994, p. 50 (Shipp, Leanderson and Sundberg 1980; Seashore 1938); Prame 1992, p. 73, abstract ("the mean rate across singers was 6.1 Hz") | carried; Step 2's "averages sit near six"
14. Hertz counts cycles a second, so a vibrato's rate in hertz is its swings a second: Voice Mirror's "5.6 Hz" is 5.6 swings a second | Lesson 1 FACTS 2 (BIPM's SI Brochure: 1 Hz is one cycle a second; carried); Lesson 1's Words, "Hertz (Hz): swings per second."
