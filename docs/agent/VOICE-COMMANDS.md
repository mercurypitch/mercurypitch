# What you can say

Every navigation phrase voice control answers to, as it is in the code today.
Transport, mixing and room-specific commands are registered by the surfaces
that own them and are not listed here.

Regenerate rather than edit: the lists live in
[`room-navigation-commands.ts`](../../src/features/voice-control/room-navigation-commands.ts)
(the rooms and the way back into the studio) and
[`navigation-commands.ts`](../../src/features/voice-control/navigation-commands.ts)
(the studio's own tab and library set), and a doc kept by hand goes stale the
first time somebody adds a phrase.

## How matching works, in one paragraph

A phrase has to consume the **whole** utterance. That is what keeps a bare
name safe: `go to guitar` reaches the guitar tab and `go to guitar night`
reaches the room, with nothing to arbitrate between them. When no phrase
matches outright, three fallbacks run in order — the wake word is restored
(`mercury sing`), a stutter is collapsed (`sing sing` is one `sing`), and
finally up to two leading tokens are dropped for a self-correction
(`guitar, i play guitar`). Numbers are never collapsed, so `back two two`
fails honestly rather than becoming `back two`.

A command only fires if the surface it belongs to is visible under the
current practice scope and simple-mode setting, so this list is the maximum,
not what is live on any one screen.

## Known gaps

- **Voice Mirror and Break Glass** have no commands. They are tools rather
  than rooms, so it is a judgement call whether they should.

All four nights now carry the room set. Piano Night and Drum Night reach it
through `RoomVoiceControl`, loaded with `lazy()` — voice control's static
graph pulls the app's router, half its stores, the practice timer and the
local Whisper engine, and a standalone room's first paint must not pay for
any of it. `assert-piano-night-bundle.mjs` and its drum twin fail the build
if it ever does.

- **`home` has no bare form**, deliberately: voice control listens while music
  plays, and it is in half the choruses ever written. `go home` and `take me
home` carry the intent.
