## What the reference is doing

The reference's richness comes from its hierarchy. Large bright pavilion silhouettes identify each destination. Curving ivory paths and luminous medallions draw the eye between them. Dark teal banners, cypresses and portrait panels provide contrast. Small planting, crystal and gold details fill the edges without covering that route. The scenery has a foreground, a middle distance and a hazy background; water descends into those atmospheric layers.

The live scene already has the correct broad vocabulary, but repeating parts alone cannot recreate the composition. The next gains come from where detail is placed, stronger material contrast and finishing transitions between objects.

The V5 close views make the remaining limits easier to see: First Light's illustrated inset now holds up beside its ornate rim, while the terrace is still almost featureless white and the medallion is visibly thick. The Conservatory's highly reflective column surfaces read like chrome fragments beside the quiet stone steps; these need a deliberate material assignment when its distinct kit is produced. The new pools establish a water source, but shaped stone lips and larger bank planting are still part of the landscape finishing pass. These are recorded follow-ups, not claims that V5 matches the concept completely.

## This batch's implementation contract

1. **Inspection controls:** mouse wheel/trackpad and tablet pinch zoom toward the selected island; drag changes orbit. Preserve default camera and clamp close distance/pitch to avoid clipping. Pinch/cancel must never select or enter a gallery. Reset restores the approved view. Touch hit targets and labels remain reachable.
2. **Portrait insert:** one shared decoded texture for all locked monuments, on the donor's exact arched inset; no geometric face. The existing saved portrait replaces it only after the earned image is ready. Missing/aborted map artwork follows normal map failure/catalogue fallback, and all bitmaps/materials are retired.
3. **Water:** one broad fall per physical landmass, softly curved across its width, with surface motion travelling downward. Start each at an authored pool. Dissolve the lower reach into mist/foam rather than a hard horizontal edge. Shared geometry/materials and bounded particles; no runtime fluid simulation or opaque pond floating below every island.
4. **Source planting:** reuse the accepted flowers/foliage in clusters around each basin. Avoid plants in the spillway, on a marker or in the route's clear centre.
5. **Verification:** inspect desktop, tablet and narrow phone; real wheel/drag and actual touch pinch/cancel; verify earned art is still upright and inside the rim; watch moving water from front and oblique angles. Record software-renderer cost as geometry/draw-call evidence, never as phone FPS.

## Prioritised follow-ups after owner testing

1. **Animated Merc loader** — inspect the current rig and create two short reusable clips, with obvious non-numeric progress. Preserve the right-side copy and the loading/disposal contract. See its separate plan; no new paid rig submitted merely for API research.
2. **Twin connector and conservatory modelling** — commission the missing structural silhouettes with Meshy, then separate/retopologise/UV/bake and preserve packed `.blend` sources. Compare fixed camera renders before replacing the accepted donors.
3. **Marble and medallion finishing** — modular inlay/trim textures, shallow luminous medallions and restrained gold response. A material test scene must cover morning environment, close zoom and mobile quality.
4. **Landscape composition** — larger planted pockets, cliff/root variation and source rocks, followed by very sparse distant silhouettes. Spend polygons where they alter the silhouette; use instances for repeated foliage.
5. **Performance and camera refinement** — physical tablet measurements with expanded geometry, then detail tiers/LOD and deliberate occlusion. Do not equate SwiftShader slowness with physical GPU performance.
