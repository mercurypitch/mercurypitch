## Teaching

### Objectives (CURRICULUM § 2.1, Lesson 5)

1. Know what pushing does to a long note: it gets louder and drifts higher, so a steady note needs
   steady air, not more of it.
2. Know what the diaphragm does: it mostly works on the breath in and can't push air out; full
   lungs push by themselves, so at first the body holds some of that push back.
3. Breathe in low and quietly, and start a note easily (air and sound together).
4. Hold one comfortable note for ten seconds, and know a steady note by its line: it settles into
   the band and stays, a scoop and a small vibrato included.

### Misconceptions (Tavi voices them sincerely; a demonstration breaks them)

1. "If I push the air out harder, the note holds steadier" (the cold open, o1; the CURRICULUM
   card's "Support means pushing or squeezing"). Broken by sound and picture: the pushed take gets
   louder and climbs out of the band, its wave and all; then Step 1's Push note rises with the push
   to the slider's top, stays above the band 1.5 s, and comes back as the push eases (Tavi's o4,
   "If I ease off, does it come back down?", is answered by the sound).
2. "The diaphragm is the muscle that pushes" (o6, "Then where does that push come from? Isn't it
   the diaphragm?"). Broken by the breath figure (the dome flattens to breathe in); by Step 2's
   predict, asked before any line says what the diaphragm can't do; by the gauge, heard before it
   is explained: Merc's A3 holds unchanged for 10 s while "Now: holding back" turns into "Now:
   adding" (FACTS 93); and, on "can't push air out", by an arrow up from the dome struck through.
3. "The biggest breath I can" (o9, Step 3's heading with her guess). Answered from FACTS 17 to 19
   (b20a: after the biggest breath, full lungs push hardest, so a quiet note needs a lot of holding
   back), the small gauge drawing a fuller breath's bigger "held back" (FACTS 92). It claims no
   benefit for any amount of breath; the coach confirms it.
4. "A steady note is a perfectly straight line" (o11). Broken by Tavi's own ten-second hold, tagged
   "Illustration": her line scoops in, wavers, settles into the band and keeps a small, even wave,
   and Merc calls it steady. Her surprise, "Is that still steady?", is made safe (playbook rule 15).
   Her dashed guess is the cold open's flat line again.
5. "So it's the steady air that keeps the note steady?" (o13): the hook's answer as a viewer might
   mishear it, said as her guess so it can be answered. Merc: "Not on its own. Muscles in your
   voice box set the note. A change in the air moves it; steady air doesn't." (FACTS 91).

And the mix-up card's belief, as Tavi's question (o8): "support" means pushing hard, or squeezing.
Merc answers with the card's evidence (pushing moves the note, the pushed take in miniature;
squeezing makes a pressed sound that needs more pressure, not less, with two level strips under one
push of air, the pressed one much shorter, FACTS 95), its definition (no one agreed meaning) and
its better idea.

### Signature object and its transformation

The band chart, the app's own picture of a sung note: Merc's amber band at A3 with his steady line
at its centre, a violet line for a voice, drawn with the wave its voice carries, and under it a
level strip whose height is loudness (never drawn on the pitch axis, playbook rule 20). In the cold
open it holds the two takes. In the recall only its strip's "about speaking volume" lights. In
chapter 1 it becomes the Push toy, a slider under it. In chapter 2 the level strip opens out
sideways into the breath gauge, the push of air across one 10-second note, the level gold line
"what the note needs" against the lungs' falling push, with Merc's small band beside it showing his
note unchanged, and folds back into the strip; in the mix-up card the strip doubles, easy and
pressed. In chapter 5 its window widens into the hold's 10-second frame for Tavi's line; on the
viewer's turn the same frame holds only a chrome playhead and the ring; in the recap it answers the
last of three questions. The breath figure and the three starts' trace strip are the two other
objects, each in its chapter; the hosts leave or sit small at the edge during them.

### Three signature moments

1. **One push, both moved.** The pushed take's line climbs out of the top of the band at the moment
   its level strip swells: louder and higher from one push, heard and seen together.
2. **The lungs push by themselves.** Merc's A3 holds unchanged for ten seconds while, on the gauge,
   the lungs' own push falls across the note and crosses the level gold line: "Now: holding back"
   turns into "Now: adding", heard before a word explains it; switched to Loud, the crossing jumps
   from 7 s to 3 s.
3. **A steady note settles.** Tavi's line scoops up, wavers, finds the band and stays with a small,
   even wave; the dashed straight line of her guess fades beside it. Then the same frame waits,
   empty, for the viewer's ten seconds.
