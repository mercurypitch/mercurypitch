# Script review 01: Lesson 5, "Breath and a steady note"

Saved by the lead from the critic's returned text (the critic could not write files), 18 November 2026.

**Verdict: ONE MORE PASS.** Every line traces to the facts files, and all the care lines are present, word for word. Chapter 5 (Tavi's hold, her "Is that still steady?") is as strong as anything in the series. But the cold open and recall pictures teach the opposite of chapters 5 and 1. From 3:00 to 4:59 the film is a lecture. The sung turn and the outro run about 80 s longer than Lesson 4's.

**Beats as read.** Cold open: sound, guess, predict, reveal, hook. Recall. Ch1 replays the cold open's push. Ch2 is a diagram with a predict. 2:56-4:59 is Merc alone, with three 1.6 s sounds. Ch5 is a worked example with Tavi. 6:01-7:40: care lines, instructions, a 10 s hold, the feelings question. Then recap, two cards, tease.

## Problems, most severe first

1. **0:00.6-0:28: the film draws the very idea it refutes at 5:31.** The easy take sounds with the "ooh"'s ±14-cent vibrato (FACTS 79), but s01 draws its line "level". This breaks the film's own rule (FACTS § 4: "a singer's violet line shows its wave"). So the first steady note the viewer sees is dead straight. It also gives the pill "steadier than before" nothing to look like. Fix: draw the easy take's wave and keep Tavi's flat dashed guess. The guess then reads "pushing would iron out that wave", and s10's straight dashed line becomes a callback to it.
2. **1:05-1:14: the recall contradicts the cold open, and no line says so.** About 35 s after "louder · higher", the level strip grows while the line holds its height ("quiet or loud: the same note"). Nobody mentions the clash until Tavi does at 1:39. Fix: light "about speaking volume" and drop the loudness animation, so chapter 1's o5/b9 can own the tension.
3. **1:15-1:48: chapter 1 promises a "why" it never gives.** In o4 Tavi asks why a harder push makes the note higher. b7 only says that it does, and the facts give no mechanism. Also, `push` peaks at +42 cents against the band's 35-cent edge and is outside it for 0.53 s, so "above the band" will flash rather than read. Fix: retitle the chapter "What pushing does". Change o4 to a question the demo answers ("If I ease off, does it come back?"). Push to 100 (+60 cents, FACTS 73) and hold there about 1.5 s.
4. **2:56-4:59: two minutes of exposition.** Tavi says 17 words in two step prompts. There is no predict and no misconception, and the three starts sound 6 to 9 s apart, too far apart to compare a 3 ms click with a 60 ms rise.
   - s08: give Tavi a guess the gauge can refute ("The biggest breath I can?"), answered from FACTS 18-19. The coach should confirm this is not a claimed benefit of a cue.
   - s09: replay the three starts back to back, with a listen-for prompt.
   - s07: refute squeezing with something seen. FACTS 26 says the same pressure gives a much quieter pressed note, which two level strips can show.
5. **2:52: "A loud note needs less holding back" is a half-truth to end a chapter on.** A beginner can hear it as "loud is easier", in a lesson that asks for speaking volume. Add the other half of FACTS 19: a loud note needs a lot of breathing-out effort once little air is left.
6. **The film is too long: 9:21 estimated, about 9:50 voiced, with 65 s from "Your turn" (6:01) to the first sung second (7:06.5).** Cuts, about 28 s in all: b38 and `pick` ("Sing my A, or Tavi's an octave up", as Lesson 4 did, about 7 s); "It goes listen, breathe, sing." (2 s); merge b1 and b2 (4 s); recap Q2, the cold open's predict asked a third time (12 s); trim b55 (3 s).
7. **Safety lines are whole, but two are badly placed.** The doctor lines are never spoken. A listen-along viewer gets 10 s of silence during the hold, and no "Rest your voice" until b58, about 100 s later. For the coach to decide: voice the doctor lines once in `rest` (37 words, about 16 s), and add "Listening along? Rest your voice and watch the ring." after b37. Tag `pushed` and `push` "listen only", as the catch is.
8. **5:52-5:59: the hook asks what *keeps* a note steady, and "That's it." confirms an answer viewers will hear as "steady air keeps it steady".** FACTS 10 forbids exactly that claim. Fix: add one Merc line on what sets the note (the voice-box muscles, L3 FACTS 23 as cited in L5 FACTS 10), carried into FACTS.md first.
9. **Smaller items:** 0:44.8 the promise offers "what pushing does" right after showing it: promise the diaphragm instead. 1:55 "So what does my diaphragm..." has no bridge from chapter 1: "Then where does that push come from? Isn't it the diaphragm?". 2:13 the predict comes straight after "It can't push air out", which gives the answer away. s10: Tavi's hold has no "Illustration" tag. b32 and s10 say "15 s" and "as long as you can": both are on the brief's must-never list as written, so change the brief or the line. The card's six-second hold follows straight after "5, 10 or 15 s". The hold has no audible start cue. The brief says Lesson 4's film ended on its hook "in type only", but Lesson 4's script voices it.

**What would put it beside Lesson 4:** play FACTS 74's unchanged A3 under the gauge for its 10 s, with no narration. The note holds while "holding back" turns into "adding". The hook's answer becomes something you hear, and chapter 2 gets a sound before its diagram, as rule 5 asks.

**Numbers relied on:** the draft's estimates (Merc 895 words at 2.32/s, Tavi 192 at 3.08/s); FACTS 72-74 and 79-80; Lesson 4's turn plus recap (7:08.8-8:55) against this film's (6:00-9:03).

**Could not judge:** whether beginners hear the pushed take's 60 cents as higher; whether 1.8 s is long enough for open recap questions; how the voiced pace actually lands; whether a Lesson 3 film exists for "From Lesson 3" to point to.
